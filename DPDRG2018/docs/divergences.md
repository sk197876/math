# Divergences from DDG, Appendix A

[README](../README.md) · [Appendix A](appendixA.md)

This register lists the places where the formalisation of Appendix A of
De Philippis, De Rosa and Ghiraldin (2018), pp. 1143–1144, departs from
the printed text or from the blueprint
([blueprint/dpdrg_blueprint.tex](../blueprint/dpdrg_blueprint.tex)). The
kinds are those of `federer/docs/divergences.md`:

- **Proof route.** A different argument from DDG's or the blueprint's.
- **Hypothesis or convention.** A changed hypothesis, a stronger or more
  general statement, or a convention made explicit.
- **Interpretation.** An explicit reading of notation needed to state the
  formal claim.
- **Not formalised.** A part of an item that is not formalised.

| A.1 | Hypothesis or convention | G(n, d) is defined by (A.1), as in Allard 2.3 (`orthogonal_projectors`); DDG define it as the set of d dimensional subspaces and identify. The identification with Federer's G(n, d) is Allard's `allard_2_3_identification`. | `ddg_A_1` |
| A.3 | Hypothesis or convention | T(t) is defined for every t: it is the value of Allard's projection formula when Q_t = J_t^* ∘ J_t + T^⊥ is univalent and T otherwise. Only \|t\| \|L\| < 1 matters, and there T(t) is the orthogonal projection onto (1 + t L)[im T]. | `ddg_moving_plane`, `ddg_moving_planeP` |
| A.4 | Proof route | DDG obtain Tan(G(n, d), T) ⊂ {A : A^* = A, A = A ∘ T + T ∘ A} by differentiating the identities of (A.1) along curves, and equality by a dimension count. Here the tangent cone is Federer's 3.1.21 cone; the inclusion ⊂ is proved from its definition (with k = S − T for S ∈ G(n, d), w − w ∘ T − T ∘ w = w ∘ k for w = r k), and the inclusion ⊃ from Lemma A.1, since X + X^* = T'(0) for L = X ∈ H_T. No dimension count is used. | `ddg_cone_symmetric`, `ddg_cone_derivation`, `ddg_tangent_S3_cone`, `ddg_grassmann_tangent` |
| A.4 | Not formalised | That G(n, d) is a submanifold of class ∞ of End(R^n) of dimension d(n − d), and the charts. The tangent cone is characterised and shown to be a vector subspace, which is what Lemma A.1, B_F and Lemma A.2 use. | — |
| A.5 | Hypothesis or convention | Lemma A.1 is stated for every family P(t) such that P(t) is the orthogonal projection onto (1 + t L)[im T] for \|t\| < δ, not only for the projection formula. The derivative is the derivative of Federer 2.9.22 in End(R^n) normed as in Allard 2.2. | `ddg_lemma_A_1` |
| A.5 | Proof route | DDG differentiate the projection formula, which needs the derivative of matrix inversion. The formal proof is direct: with P = T(h), the estimate \|(P − T)(v)\| ≤ 3 \|h\| \|L\| \|v\| for \|h\| \|L\| ≤ 1/2 and the decomposition of h^{-1}(P − T) − T'(0) into three terms give \|h^{-1}(P − T) − T'(0)\| ≤ 15 n^{1/2} \|L\|^2 \|h\|. | `ddg_plane_estimate`, `ddg_quotient_estimate`, `ddg_lemma_A_1` |
| def:BF | Interpretation | F is a function on End(R^n) whose values off G(n, d) play no role; D F(T) is the differential of F at T relative to G(n, d) of Federer 3.1.22, a partial function whose domain is Tan(G(n, d), T). B_F(T) is characterised by the displayed equation ([`is_ddg_B`]); its existence needs only differentiability at T relative to G(n, d). | `is_ddg_B`, `ddg_B_spec`, `ddg_B_formula` |
| def:BF | Hypothesis or convention | `ddg_B F T` is a total function, selected by a sealed choice; it has the characterising property when T ∈ G(n, d) and F is differentiable at T relative to G(n, d), and is unspecified otherwise. Only F not depending on the point is treated. | `ddg_B`, `DdgB.laws` |
| def:BF | Proof route | Existence of B_F(T) is proved by the explicit formula B = F(T) T + T^⊥ ∘ (u + u^*) ∘ T, with u the gradient of the ambient differential (Federer 3.1.1), not stated in DDG. | `ddg_B_exists` |
| A.6 | Hypothesis or convention | Formalised for an integrand not depending on the point; the frozen integrand F(x, ·) is of this kind. The formula holds for any B satisfying the characterising equation and any family of projections onto (1 + t L)[im T]. | `ddg_weighted_jacobian_derivative`, `ddg_B_derivative` |
| A.6 | Proof route | ‖∧_d T‖ = 1 is derived from Allard 2.4 and the unit norm of an orthonormal d-vector (Federer 1.7.5), not from the orthogonal basis of ∧_d R^n. The chain rule is applied to an ambient extension g of F with the differential D, through Federer 3.1.1(2) for curves, and F(T(t)) = g(T(t)) near t = 0. | `ddg_jacobian_projection`, `ddg_curve_chain` |
| A.7 | Hypothesis or convention | Ω = R^n, the case used by DPGPR2026. Instead of g ∈ D(Ω, R^n), g is of class 1 with Lip g ≤ K and \|g\| ≤ M, and t satisfies \|t\| K < 1. φ_t(x) = x is stated for g(x) = 0. | `ddg_flow_immersion` |
| A.7 | Not formalised | φ_t of class ∞ (Allard 3.2 needs class 1), and the assertions about Ω ≠ R^n. | — |
| A.8 | Hypothesis or convention | Ω = R^n. F is a real function on R^n × End(R^n), of class 1 on an open set U ⊃ R^n × G(n, d) for the product inner product of Federer 1.7.11 (`product_inner`); F > 0 is not needed. D_x F(x, S)(v) is read as DF(x, S)(v, 0), and B_F(x, S) is the matrix of def:BF for the frozen integrand F(x, ·). Instead of g ∈ D(Ω, R^n), g is of class 1 with Lip g ≤ K, \|g\| ≤ M, and g(y) = 0, Dg(y) = 0 for \|y\| ≥ ρ. | `ddg_lemma_A_2`, `ddg_point_first_variation_integrand` |
| A.8 | Interpretation | F(φ_t# V) may be infinite when V has infinite mass. `ddg_lemma_A_2` differentiates t ↦ ∫ F(φ_t(x), Dφ_t(x)(S)) ‖∧_d(Dφ_t(x) ∘ S)‖ − F(x, S) dV(x, S), whose integrand vanishes for \|x\| ≥ ρ; by Allard 3.2(1) this is F(φ_t# V) − F(V) when F(V) is finite, and `ddg_lemma_A_2_image` states the derivative of F(φ_t# V) itself under that hypothesis. The derivative assertion reads as in A.9: every function I with the stated integral values for \|t\| < δ has derivative c at 0. | `ddg_lemma_A_2`, `ddg_lemma_A_2_image` |
| A.8 | Proof route | As for A.9, with these changes. The pointwise derivative splits F(φ_t(x), T(t)) J(t) into [F(φ_t(x), T(t)) − F(x, T(t))] J(t) + F(x, T(t)) J(t): the second term is A.6 for the frozen integrand, the first is differentiated by the chain rule (Federer 3.1.1) along the curves t ↦ (x + t g(x), T(t)) and t ↦ (x, T(t)) of R^n × End(R^n). Domination uses the Lipschitz constant and the bound of F on the compact set {(y, S) : \|y\| ≤ ρ + M, S ∈ G(n, d)} and the V summable function C 1{\|x\| < ρ}; V is finite on compact sets because it is a Radon measure. Differentiation under the integral sign is the general lemma `ddg_integral_derivative`. | `ddg_point_flow_integrand_derivative`, `ddg_point_flow_integrand_estimate`, `ddg_point_compact`, `ddg_point_ball_summable`, `ddg_integral_derivative` |
| A.9 | Hypothesis or convention | Ω = R^n and ‖V‖(R^n) < ∞ (`V setT < +oo`). F is of class 1 on an open set W ⊃ G(n, d) of End(R^n), as in the blueprint; F > 0 is not needed. Instead of g ∈ D(Ω, R^n), g is of class 1 with Lip g ≤ K and \|g\| ≤ M. The integrals exist for \|t\| < δ, and the derivative assertion reads: every function I with I(t) = ∫ F d(φ_t)_# V for \|t\| < δ has derivative ∫ B_F(T) • Dg(x) dV(x, T) at 0. | `ddg_lemma_A_2_constant` |
| A.9 | Hypothesis or convention | A more general version assumes only that F is Lipschitzian and bounded on G(n, d) and differentiable at every T ∈ G(n, d) relative to G(n, d). | `ddg_lemma_A_2_constant_lipschitz` |
| A.9 | Proof route | DDG differentiate under the integral sign. Here the integral over (φ_t)_# V is moved to V by Allard 3.2(1) (`allard_3_2_1_integral`); the pointwise derivative at 0 is A.6; domination comes from the uniform estimate \|F(T(t)) ‖∧_d((1 + t L) ∘ T)‖ − F(T)\| ≤ C \|t\|, proved from the bounds (1 − c)^d ≤ ‖∧_d((1 + t L) ∘ T)‖ ≤ (1 + c)^d rather than from a mean value theorem; the limit is taken along sequences t_k → 0 by the dominated convergence theorem (Federer 2.4.9), and measurability of the limit integrand follows from its being a pointwise limit. | `ddg_jacobian_lower`, `ddg_jacobian_upper`, `ddg_flow_integrand_estimate`, `ddg_dominated_integral_limit`, `ddg_punctured_limit_sequence` |
