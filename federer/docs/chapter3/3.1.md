## 3.1.1 — Differentials and elementary calculus (pp. 209–211)

**Completed with the conventions and qualifications below.** The
formalisation covers the differential definition, elementary calculus,
gradient and polarity, sharp increment ratios, class-zero and class-one
maps, inverse-function theorems, Borel measurability and the partial-
derivative statements. The documented zero-dimensional qualifications
and the erratum correction remain explicit.

The source and target are arbitrary real vector spaces with supplied
finite-valued norms satisfying [`is_real_norm`](../../theories/RealNormUP.v#L26). Neither finite dimension
nor completeness is imposed on the opening definition. The scalar field
is the project's `realType`, following the convention of the preceding
normed-space development. A function with domain A is represented by A
and a total representative; [`norm_differential_domain_agreement`](../../theories/NormDifferentialLocal.v#L15) proves
that values outside A are immaterial.

- [`NormDifferentialUP.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialUP.v) specifies an interior domain point, a bounded
  linear map and an epsilon-small first-order remainder. The predicate
  [`has_norm_differential`](../../theories/NormDifferentialUP.v#L34) is a property to prove, not an additional axiom.
- [`NormBoundedLinearTopology.v`](../../theories/NormBoundedLinearTopology.v) proves that these interior and finite-bound
  conditions are exactly neighbourhood membership and continuity in the
  given norm topologies. In particular, the finite bound is not an added
  hypothesis on Federer's continuous linear map.
- [`NormDifferentialQuotient.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialQuotient.v) proves equivalence with the printed
  punctured quotient limit. The error estimate includes zero increments
  without assigning a value to the quotient there.
- [`NormDifferentialDirectional.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialDirectional.v) proves the directional quotient formula
  and uniqueness of the differential, including zero-dimensional spaces.
- [`NormDifferentialConstruction.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialConstruction.v) proves existence of the exact partial
  graph Df. [`NormDifferentialSealed.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialSealed.v) hides the witness behind an opaque
  signature. [`NormDifferential.v`](../../theories/NormDifferential.v) exposes the graph and domain laws;
  downstream modules use those laws rather than the construction.
- [`NormDifferentialLinear.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialLinear.v) proves the linear and constant cases and
  the bounded-linear algebra used in the calculus rules.
- [`NormDifferentialLocal.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialLocal.v) proves restriction, independence from the
  total representative, a pointwise increment bound and continuity at the
  differentiability point. The increment bound is not a claim that f is
  Lipschitz between every pair of nearby points.
- [`NormDifferentialChain.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialChain.v) proves 3.1.1(2) on the actual composite domain
  consisting of x in A with f(x) in the domain of g. Its interior property
  is proved, rather than assumed through a global domain inclusion.
- [`NormDifferentialScalar.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialScalar.v) connects the real-source special case with
  the curve derivative already specified for 2.9.22, with L(v)=v L(1),
  including the equivalence with existence of a derivative vector.
- [`NormDifferentialInverse.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialInverse.v) proves 3.1.1(1) from the local inverse
  identities, an interior inverse domain and continuity of the inverse
  at f(a). Differentiability of the inverse is a conclusion. The proof
  only needs a bounded linear left inverse of Df(a), a consequence of
  the printed linear-homeomorphism hypothesis.
- [`NormDifferentialArithmetic.v`](../../theories/NormDifferentialArithmetic.v) proves the supporting sum and scalar
  rules, including a zero scalar coefficient.

[`DifferentialGradientUP.v`](../../theories/DifferentialGradientUP.v) specifies the gradient by L(v)=v.grad f(a).
The private existence proofs in [`DifferentialGradientConstruction.v`](../../theories/DifferentialGradientConstruction.v)
cover both finite dimension, using a basis, and completeness, using the

## 3.1.2 — Approximate differentials (pp. 211–212)

**Completed with the domain and dimension conventions below.**
[`ApproximateDifferentialUP.v`](../../theories/ApproximateDifferentialUP.v) specifies the linear approximate differential
using the approximate-limit predicate of 2.9.12, actual Lebesgue measure
and the positive-radius centered closed-ball relation. The quotient has
exactly its punctured actual domain. The base point belongs to A because
f(a) occurs in the expression; no openness of A is assumed.

[`EuclideanApproximateContext.v`](../../theories/EuclideanApproximateContext.v) proves the centered relation is Vitali.
[`ApproximateDifferentialRemainder.v`](../../theories/ApproximateDifferentialRemainder.v) derives density zero of the missing
domain and the exceptional remainder sets. The uniqueness proof uses the
book's small-ball volume argument: two exceptional sets with less than
half the smaller ball's mass leave a point outside both. The formal proof
keeps the original base point and uses a sphere of radius r/2 with smaller
balls of radius epsilon r, rather than the printed radius (1-epsilon)r.
The same norm estimate and homogeneity imply that the linear difference
vanishes. Finite-dimensional boundedness is proved, not assumed.
[`CenteredZeroDensityCriterion.v`](../../theories/CenteredZeroDensityCriterion.v) proves the converse small-ball mass
criterion, including singleton spaces with nonunique radius representations.

[`ApproximatePartialDerivativeUP.v`](../../theories/ApproximatePartialDerivativeUP.v) separately specifies the scalar
Lebesgue approximate limits defining approximate partial derivatives.
Their uniqueness uses Hausdorff uniqueness from 2.9.12.
[`ApproximateDifferentialConstruction.v`](../../theories/ApproximateDifferentialConstruction.v) proves existence of both exact
partial graphs; [`ApproximateDifferentialSealed.v`](../../theories/ApproximateDifferentialSealed.v) hides these witnesses.
Downstream results use [`ApproximateDifferential.v`](../../theories/ApproximateDifferential.v) and
[`ApproximatePartialDerivative.v`](../../theories/ApproximatePartialDerivative.v).
[`ApproximateDifferentialLocal.v`](../../theories/ApproximateDifferentialLocal.v) proves that changing the representative
outside A affects neither differential nor partial derivative.

The project's dimension-zero Lebesgue measure has unit mass. With the
printed punctured quotient convention, the approximate differential then
has empty domain: there are no nonzero increments, and the missing
quotient domain has density one. This boundary differs from the ordinary
differential's epsilon-remainder formulation and is recorded explicitly.
Affine examples on domains with null complements use positive dimension.
No completeness or separability of the target is required.

## 3.1.3 — Measurable sections and approximate limits (pp. 212–213)

**Completed.** All five assertions are formalised with the conventions below.

1. [`euclidean_small_section_mass_measurable`](../../theories/EuclideanSectionMassMeasurable.v#L68) proves that the parameter
   set satisfying every sufficiently small ball-mass bound is measurable.
   Fubini gives measurability at each fixed radius; monotonicity of mass
   and continuity of the bound reduce all radii to rational radii.
   The supporting statement permits arbitrary real coefficients and cutoffs.
2. [`euclidean_approximate_upper_limit_measurable`](../../theories/EuclideanApproximateOrderMeasurable.v#L38) and
   [`euclidean_approximate_lower_limit_measurable`](../../theories/EuclideanApproximateOrderMeasurable.v#L74) prove measurability of
   approximate upper and lower section limits. Their supporting formulation
   allows extended values and proper conull domains. Complements of the
   good sets include undefined points, following the corrected 2.9.12
   convention. Matching finite upper and lower limits characterize real
   approximate limits, and their equality with a measurable parameter
   function defines a measurable set.
3. [`euclidean_epimorphism_preimage_measurable`](../../theories/EuclideanEpimorphismMeasurable.v#L74) proves measurability under
   every linear epimorphism. The coordinate-change laws are specified by
   [`is_euclidean_epimorphism_chart`](../../theories/EuclideanEpimorphismChartUP.v#L15); a private dual-basis extension proves
   existence, and [`EuclideanEpimorphismChartSealed.v`](../../theories/EuclideanEpimorphismChartSealed.v) hides that witness.
   The dimension inequality follows from surjectivity. The proof follows
   the printed product-cylinder identification and linear change of
   variables. Null-set transport and measurable-function precomposition
   are derived separately, including the conull-domain condition.
4. [`euclidean_almost_slice_approximate_continuity`](../../theories/EuclideanSliceApproximateContinuity.v#L28) proves the approximate
   limit identity along the first coordinate block at almost every point.
   The proof follows the printed joint translated function, measurable
   equality locus, almost-everywhere section measurability, 2.9.13 and a

5. [`euclidean_almost_slice_complement_density_zero`](../../theories/EuclideanSliceDensity.v#L25) applies (4) to the
