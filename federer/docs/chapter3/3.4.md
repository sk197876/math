### 3.4.1 — Critical values and the dimension problem (p. 310)

The opening item asks for the least real degree at which every class-k
map from an open subset of R^m to a normed space has null image on its
rank-at-most-nu locus. It announces the answer
s = nu + (m − nu)/k, whose upper bound and sharpness belong to 3.4.3
and 3.4.4. Both the upper theorem and sharpness are now validated.

[`CriticalValueProblem.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueProblem.v) states the universal nullity problem and its
least-degree property. Its predicates quantify over arbitrary normed
targets and original open source domains. They use the real-dimensional,
Gamma-normalized Hausdorff measure of 2.10.2. The upper nullity property is proved by 3.4.3. The least-degree
property is proved by 3.4.4.

[`CriticalValueLociUP.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueLociUP.v) specifies the original source set and its image.
Membership requires a genuine differential at the point and a finite
basis of that differential's range. [`CriticalValueLociExistence.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueLociExistence.v)
proves existence and uniqueness of both sets; [`CriticalValueLociSealed.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueLociSealed.v)
hides the witnesses. The public [`CriticalValueLoci.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueLoci.v) uses their laws
to prove source containment, monotonicity in the rank threshold,
independence of the differential witness, the full source-dimension
criterion, and the constant and bounded-linear cases on open domains.

[`FiniteSourceLinearRank.v`](../../theories/FiniteSourceLinearRank.v) proves that a map from a space with a finite
basis has a finite-dimensional range, bounded by the source dimension.
It also proves full source rank for injections. The target may have
infinite dimension. The proofs lift independent range vectors to the
source and transfer a source basis, using the project's results of 1.6.1
and 1.7.4.

[`CriticalValueDegreeUP.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueDegreeUP.v) specifies the printed scalar by its equation.
[`CriticalValueDegreeExistence.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueDegreeExistence.v) proves existence and uniqueness;
[`CriticalValueDegreeSealed.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueDegreeSealed.v) hides the witness. [`CriticalValueDegree.v`](../../theories/CriticalValueDegree.v)
proves nu < s ≤ m, positivity, s = m at k = 1, and s = m/k at nu = 0.
The public value retains the printed k ≥ 1 and m > nu ≥ 0.

Three regressions in [`CriticalValueProblemExamples.v`](../../tests/CriticalValueProblemExamples.v) use a nonzero
constant map from the plane to the line, the line identity at rank
thresholds zero and one, and an injection from the line into the space
of real sequences. The last supplies no finite target basis.

The final paragraph recalls an earlier finite-target consequence of
3.2.3 and says that the new proof will be independent of that area
formula. It supplies context for the proof of 3.4.3. The critical-set
and scalar interfaces assume neither the area formula nor the announced
image-nullity result. No erratum entry applies to p. 310.

### 3.4.6 — Weierstrass preparation (pp. 321–324)

Both original assertions are complete and validated. The public
interface is [`Weierstrass.v`](../../theories/Weierstrass.v).

The original analytic function has positive directional order $k$ at
zero: all lower directional derivatives vanish there and the $k$th is
nonzero. The public theorems retain the printed Banach source, its
original norm and direction. The functional in part (2) is arbitrary
subject to the printed continuity and $\xi(v)=1$ conditions. The
bounded-linear interface expresses this continuity by the project's
proved equivalence for normed spaces.

[`weierstrass_division`](../../theories/WeierstrassDivision.v#L20) gives unique quotient and remainder germs for every original
analytic dividend. [`weierstrass_division_on_neighbourhood`](../../theories/WeierstrassDivisionNeighbourhood.v#L25) supplies actual representatives that
satisfy $\Psi=Q\Phi+R$ on one neighbourhood. Every directional derivative
of $R$ of order at least $k$ vanishes throughout that same neighbourhood.
The abstract division laws precede existence; the witnesses are hidden
by [`WeierstrassDivisionSealed.v`](../../theories/WeierstrassDivisionSealed.v).

[`weierstrass_preparation`](../../theories/WeierstrassPreparation.v#L34) proves part (2):

$$
Q(x)\Phi(x)=W(x)=\xi(x)^k+
  \sum_{i<k}\xi(x)^i W_i(x-\xi(x)v).
$$

The original $Q(0)\ne0$ and every $W_i(0)=0$ are proved. The multiplier,
monic polynomial and each transverse coefficient germ are unique.
[`WeierstrassPreparationSealed.v`](../../theories/WeierstrassPreparationSealed.v)
hides these witnesses. Independent splitting laws identify its transverse
space with the actual kernel of the original functional and preserve
the inherited norm. Both coordinate maps are bounded. Downstream proofs
use the sealed equations, rather than a chosen subspace representation.

The proof retains the original mixed coefficients, with their two
factorials and degree-$j$ transverse polynomials. [`weierstrass_original_coefficient_grouping`](../../theories/WeierstrassCoefficientGrouping.v#L22) identifies
every finite total-degree grouping with the actual homogeneous Taylor
polynomial. [`weierstrass_mixed_taylor_expansion`](../../theories/WeierstrassMixedTaylorExpansion.v#L24) proves uniform convergence of that original mixed
expansion. [`weierstrass_all_derivative_series`](../../theories/WeierstrassDerivativeSeries.v#L29) realises the original germ by a normal homogeneous
series and proves all actual operator-valued derivative-series limits
on one common positive-radius neighbourhood. [`weierstrass_double_series_bound`](../../theories/WeierstrassDoubleSeriesBound.v#L19) proves the printed
absolute shifted-series majorant, including the binomial factor, both
inverse gaps, every finite rectangle and both coordinate tails.
[`weierstrass_zero_coefficient_characterization`](../../theories/WeierstrassZeroCoefficients.v#L20) proves that the germ is zero exactly when all original mixed
coefficients vanish.

The original quotient recurrence uses induction on $i+(k+1)j$.
Its actual coefficients remain homogeneous polynomials. The proof
establishes the positive majorant constants, the strict $\delta<1$
estimate and the geometric quotient bound, then uses the proved normal
series theorem of 3.1.24 to obtain an analytic quotient.

Two proof adaptations are explicit. First, the geometric constant absorbs
$\lVert v\rVert$; scalar normalization then makes the divided leading
coefficient one while preserving the original $v$, $\xi$ and projection.
The original leading value is restored before either numbered theorem
is stated. Second, write the finite remainder using its actual divided
fields $C_i$: $\sum_{i<k}\xi(x)^i C_i(\eta x)$, where
$\eta x=x-\xi(x)v$. Actual Taylor comparison on short transverse lines
identifies its low mixed rows. Mixed Taylor injectivity identifies its
germ with $\Psi-Q\Phi$. Finite binomial translation proves the required
derivative condition at every nearby point. Conversely, every candidate
remainder has zero high rows, so the same recurrence proves uniqueness.
Division of $\xi^k$ gives preparation. These expansions add no source
correction, project axiom or numbered-theorem hypothesis.

Four actual regressions test $\Phi(t)=t^3$, $\Psi(t)=t+1$, with opaque
quotient zero and remainder $t+1$; preparation of $2t$, whose leading
coefficient differs from one; preparation of $x^2+y$, with multiplier

incompatibility of positive directional regularity with the actual
zero-dimensional source. Every analytic and regularity premise is proved.
They are collected in [`WeierstrassExamples.v`](../../tests/WeierstrassExamples.v).
