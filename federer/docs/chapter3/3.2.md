## 3.2.1 — Ordinary and approximate Jacobians (p. 241)

**Completed with the domain conventions below.** [`EuclideanLinearJacobian.v`](../../theories/EuclideanLinearJacobian.v)
defines the k-dimensional linear Jacobian as the norm of the kth exterior
power, using the existing abstract exterior algebra and opaque operator
norm of 1.7.6. Every natural k is allowed, including zero and degrees above
an ambient dimension. The file proves nonnegativity, the operator-norm
power bound, positivity of the top-source Jacobian exactly for injective
maps, the absolute-determinant formula for square maps and agreement of
the target-degree Jacobian with the normal Jacobian used in Chapter 2.
The empty exterior product gives J_0 L=1 in all ambient dimensions.

[`JacobianUP.v`](../../theories/JacobianUP.v) specifies ordinary and approximate Jacobian values by the
existence of their actual corresponding differential with that exterior
operator norm. [`JacobianConstruction.v`](../../theories/JacobianConstruction.v) proves the two graph-existence

[`JacobianSealed.v`](../../theories/JacobianSealed.v) hides the selected graphs by opaque module ascription;
[`Jacobian.v`](../../theories/Jacobian.v) exposes only their exact graph and domain laws, nonnegative
values and agreement when the actual differential is common.

The ordinary Jacobian is defined exactly where the ordinary differential
exists; the approximate Jacobian is defined exactly where the approximate
differential exists. No value is supplied elsewhere, even for k=0. In
particular the existing zero-source punctured-density convention from
3.1.2 makes the approximate Jacobian domain empty on R^0. This is kept
explicit rather than silently changing a prior convention.

The remaining paragraphs of 3.2.1 announce the area formula of 3.2.3,
the coarea formula of 3.2.11, transformation of integrals in 3.2.5 and

interface. Their proofs remain obligations of the indicated items.

## 3.2.7 — Four counterexamples (p. 246)

**Completed and validated.** All four examples

[`federer_3_2_7_1`](../../theories/SineWeightCounterexample.v#L26) proves the full-sine example. The existing 2.2.4 gives
a nonmeasurable subset B of [-1,1]; let g=1+chi_B. Every attained level
has infinite full-sine multiplicity, so the target integral is infinity.
If the weighted source were measurable, its restriction to the bounded
principal branch would be summable. The first scalar identity of 3.2.6,
the branch's exact unit multiplicity and the superlevel set above 3/2
would then make B measurable, a contradiction. Extremal levels -1 and 1
are retained in the closed principal branch and covered by a regression.

[`federer_3_2_7_2`](../../theories/ProjectionWeightCounterexample.v#L42) proves the planar projection example. The actual linear
map (x_1,x_2) to (x_1,0) is nonexpanding and has zero top Jacobian
everywhere. Its characteristic-weight pullback is nonmeasurable: Fubini
would otherwise make the original scalar set measurable. The weighted
source and target area integrals both equal zero. The weight is finite
at every point; a zero Jacobian never supplies a missing derivative.

[`federer_3_2_7_3`](../../theories/OscillatorySubstitutionCounterexample.v#L52) proves the printed oscillatory example on [0,1].
[`oscillatory_substitution_curve`](../../theories/OscillatorySubstitutionCurve.v#L20) is |x| sqrt(|x|) sin(1/x), with value
zero at zero, exactly the printed |x|^(3/2) amplitude. The weight is
specified by [`is_reciprocal_root_weight`](../../theories/ReciprocalRootWeightUP.v#L17): 1/sqrt(|y|) on 0<|y|<=1 and
zero outside; its omitted value at zero is completed as zero. The weight
is summable on the whole line, and the curve is absolutely continuous
on every compact interval. Nevertheless no common signed substitution
integral value exists: its target interval integral is finite and its
signed source has no finite integral.

The expanded proof uses the opaque signed square root G and
F(t)=t^3 sin((t^2)^(-1)). The actual derivative of F is zero at zero
and bounded on every bounded interval; f=F composed with G. Monotonicity,
continuity, actual derivatives off zero and the existing 2.10.13 null-image
criterion prove local absolute continuity of G. The existing 2.9.19
