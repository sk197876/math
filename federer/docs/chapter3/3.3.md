### 3.3.1 — Cones and projection kernels

Federer, p. 287. The standing measure hypothesis requires an outer measure
on the Euclidean space with every closed set measurable. Local finiteness
and Borel regularity are separate notions and are not added here.

The first display specifies $X(a,r,V,s)$ by
$s^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(x-a,V)<|x-a|<r$, for $0<r\le\infty$ and
$0<s<1$. The next two displays identify distance to the kernel of an
orthogonal projection with the norm of its image, and hence identify the
same cone using the original projection. The final sentence asserts that
the kernel map is continuous and open and carries the actual normalized
projection probability to the Grassmann probability. Each of these
assertions is part of this item; no completion is claimed from the
definitions alone.

The independent specifications precede the proved existence and uniqueness
of opaque cone and kernel objects. The actual residual $x-p^*p(x)$ lies
in the kernel and attains the projection norm; contraction supplies the
other distance bound. Equivariance for the original orthogonal actions,
combined with their continuous open onto orbit maps, proves both topology
assertions. The projection probability and Grassmann probability are
specified independently. The normalized Haar measure and a base projection
are local witnesses used to identify both orbit-image measures on every set.

The definition is also meaningful for other radius and aperture parameters;
its laws are proved in that supporting generality, while the book uses
$0<r\le\infty$ and $0<s<1$. All natural dimensions $m\le n$ are
retained. The regressions use the actual line-to-zero projection, where
one is inside the infinite-radius cone and excluded at radius one; the
