The proof follows Federer's construction. After reducing to finite dimension,
the prescribed basis of T is extended without changing any initial vector.
The Grassmann expansion then gives both the bound and the factorisation.
[`Lambda_initial`](../../theories/InitialIndices.v#L46), the complementary index `nu`, and its zero inversion
count ensure that the initial factors occur in the printed order, with no
spurious sign. The construction also applies to any finite independent
family in T; taking a family of maximum possible length proves that T
itself has a finite basis even when V is infinite dimensional.

The four corollaries are stated on the actual exterior powers and the
actual associated subspaces. Auxiliary results prove that a nonzero
exterior product has independent factors, that its associated subspace
is their span, and that concatenating two independent families preserves
independence precisely when their spans have zero intersection. The
third corollary therefore includes the assertion that the entire
associated space is the sum, as well as directness.

**Literal degree-zero exception.** The opening definition makes the only
simple zero-vector the empty product 1. Every nonzero scalar nevertheless has
associated subspace {0}, of dimension zero. Consequently, the first corollary
as printed cannot include m=0.
[`simple_zero_iff`](../../theories/SimpleZeroDegree.v#L56),
[`scalar_associated_zero`](../../theories/SimpleZeroDegree.v#L69) and
[`zero_degree_simplicity_exception`](../../theories/SimpleZeroDegree.v#L161)
prove this precisely.
[`simple_dimension_zero_counterexample`](../../theories/SimpleBoundary.v#L30)
uses the scalar 2, with the same
[`associated_space`](../../theories/AssociatedDimension.v#L93) as the
dimension criterion, over every field of characteristic different from two.
The valid equivalence is stated for m>0; the printed definition is preserved.
The other corollaries retain zero degrees.

The existing characteristic-different-from-two hypothesis is retained.
No new axiom is introduced. [`SimpleVectors.v`](../../theories/SimpleVectors.v) is the public entry point.

The proofs use the existing sealed exterior algebra, alternating algebra,
Omega, and duality maps. One-covectors in associated subspaces are represented
by `Hom V K^o`, identified with degree-one forms by [`one_form`](../../theories/OneFormIdentification.v#L36) and [`one_hom`](../../theories/OneFormLinear.v#L50).
No new coordinate model for these algebras is introduced. The annihilator
of an intersection is proved directly by extending the linear assignment
`x+y -> a(y)` from the sum of the two subspaces; this identity and annihilator
separation hold in arbitrary dimension. The dimension arguments and duality
corollary assume a finite basis of the ambient space, as in Federer.

The existing characteristic-different-from-two hypothesis is retained
throughout the exterior-algebra arguments; the auxiliary annihilator
identities do not require it. No project axiom is introduced.

**Degree-zero corrections.** Literal simplicity in degree zero means being the
empty product 1. A nonzero scalar covector such as 2 has a zero-dimensional
associated space without being simple. Thus the reverse dimension criterion
requires m>0. Moreover, in dimension one, D_1 sends twice a basis vector
to the scalar 2, and D^1 sends twice its dual one-form to the scalar 2. Both
inputs are simple; neither output is a literal simple degree-zero element.
Consequently, the unrestricted preservation assertion in Federer requires these degree qualifications. The
two explicit counterexamples are proved in
[`DualityBoundary.v`](../../theories/DualityBoundary.v). These are local
corrections, not entries in Federer's erratum.

The annihilator identities and the intersection corollary retain **all
endpoint degrees, including n=0**. Internally, [`scaled_simple_covector`](../../theories/ScaledSimpleCovectors.v#L28)
allows a nonzero scalar factor before a literal simple covector, so the
proof covers scalar outputs without changing the definition of simplicity.
The condition on the intersection dimension is written `k+n=p+r`, avoiding
truncated natural subtraction. The regression
[`intersection_negative_expected_dimension`](../../tests/ExteriorExamples.v#L220) checks n=1, p=r=0: the intersection
has dimension zero but the dual product vanishes. [`intersection_zero_dimensional`](../../tests/ExteriorExamples.v#L241)
checks the nonzero product when n=p=r=0.

The public entry point remains [`GrassmannSpaces.v`](../../theories/GrassmannSpaces.v). The completed subsection
has passed a full clean build, recursive kernel check, and complete assumption
audit under `RP.2026.02.0~9.1~2026.01`; see `verification.txt`.

The proof follows the displayed adapted computation and supplies its full
spanning argument. Each pair of Grassmann basis products is partitioned into
missing, first-only, common, and second-only indices. A missing index yields
a volume factor annihilating both input words, so both contractions vanish.
Otherwise the adapted calculation applies. The proof tracks both permutation
signs and the scalar change of volume, then extends by bilinearity. Thus the
final theorem has no simplicity or transversality assumption.

The degree-specific statement explicitly includes k,l<=n and n<=k+l, which
are needed to form the displayed duality maps. Its degree reindexing only
reconciles n-(n-k+n-l) with k+l-n. The full-algebra version covers every input
degree, including degrees above n, and allows a zero top form. The scalar
field may have any characteristic different from two. No new project axiom
or external mathematical module is introduced.

The change-of-basis theorem uses the convention that the volume is pulled
back by f, giving the multiplier det(f). It also holds for a noninvertible
endomorphism. The original normalised-duality formulation follows through
[`dual_product_contraction`](../../theories/DualProductContraction.v#L43).

Source comments distinguish these supporting references from Federer's actual
identity. The local erratum has no mathematical correction to this item.

The empty-product regression checks that the result is the exterior unit.
The nontransverse regression takes k=l=1 in dimension two with both inputs
equal to the first basis vector and checks that the contraction is zero.
The 22 new theory modules and both examples are included in the successful
full clean build, recursive kernel check, and assumption audit recorded in

The mixed basis uses an increasing selection from the ordered 2n-member
family alpha_1,...,alpha_n,bar(alpha_1),...,bar(alpha_n). Selecting from the
first and second blocks records lambda and mu, respectively, with total
length m. This avoids dependent bookkeeping for separate p and q indices
without changing the family of products. Its proof first solves the two
one-form coordinates explicitly and then transfers the already proved
exterior-basis theorem through a constructed complex-coordinate comparison.
The restriction, extension, and product-compatibility maps are all proved;
no complexification or mixed-basis theorem is assumed.

Complex scalars have a separate abstract specification and an opaque sealed
implementation. Only [`ComplexScalarSealed.v`](../../theories/ComplexScalarSealed.v) imports MathComp's construction
of complex numbers as the scalar existence witness. Downstream proofs use
its stated scalar laws. The form, volume, determinant, complex-plane, and
orientation claims are proved in this repository from the earlier algebra.
The real base is an arbitrary `realFieldType`; completeness and algebraic
closure are not needed for these assertions.

The paired-vector definition is accompanied by an equality to the literal
interleaved word v_1 wedge i v_1 wedge ... wedge v_p wedge i v_p. The
characterisation and sign comparison retain arbitrary ambient dimension.
Finite dimension is required only where Federer chooses a basis of the
whole space. Empty products, zero-dimensional spaces, and form degrees
above the dimension are included. In degree zero the existing literal
simplicity convention still makes the unit the only simple scalar; its
complex orientation is positive.

The source comments record that limitation. The local erratum's entry for book
p. 26, line 24, corrects the typography of the imaginary unit; no additional
mathematical correction to 1.6.6 is needed.

Regression examples check the empty paired product and its positivity,
rotation of a complex line's generator by i, reversal of positivity on
negating a nonzero paired product, and determinant one in dimension zero.
Validation of the complete subsection is recorded in `verification.txt`.
