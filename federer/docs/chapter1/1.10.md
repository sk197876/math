# Federer 1.10: Symmetric forms and polynomial functions (pp. 43–49)

Source numbering and printed pages refer to

Part of the [Chapter 1 index](../toc.md). Each entry records the formalised assertions of one numbered item, their Rocq names, hypotheses, corrections and sources.

## 1.10.1 — Symmetric forms and induced maps

**Covered:** the symmetric multilinear form spaces, both displayed linear
isomorphisms, the evaluation bracket, the degree-zero convention, the graded
direct sum and the induced contravariant maps (Federer, pp. 43–44).
The input and coefficient spaces are arbitrary-dimensional; the results hold
over arbitrary fields, extending the book's real scalar field.

- [`SymmetricForms.v`](../../theories/SymmetricForms.v) defines forms as actual tuple functions satisfying
  multilinearity and symmetry. [`SymmetricFormPermutations.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormPermutations.v) proves that
  rearrangement symmetry is equivalent to the book's permutation-of-positions
  equation. The vector-space laws are pointwise.
- [`SymmetricFormWords.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormWords.v) and [`SymmetricFormExtension.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormExtension.v) extend forms through
  the abstract symmetric-algebra universal property and prove that their
  extensions vanish on every other homogeneous degree. They do not import
  the quotient construction.
- [`SymmetricFormIsomorphism.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormIsomorphism.v) proves linear maps in both directions and both
  inverse laws for forms, maps on the m-th symmetric power and homogeneous
  maps on the full symmetric algebra. [`symmetric_form_universal`](../../theories/SymmetricFormIsomorphism.v#L171) states the
  unique factorisation, with the displayed tuple evaluation identity.
- [`SymmetricPairing.v`](../../theories/SymmetricPairing.v) gives the equivalent bracket interpretations and proves
  bilinearity and extensionality. [`SymmetricFormZero.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormZero.v) identifies zero-forms
  with the coefficient space by mutually inverse linear maps, including
  zero-dimensional input spaces.
- [`SymmetricFormSum.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormSum.v) realises the direct sum using the proved homogeneous-Hom
  model. Its component maps, orthogonality, finite reconstruction and
  extensionality are proved. [`SymmetricFormDual.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormDual.v) characterises pullback by
  the bracket identity, gives its actual tuple formula, and proves that the
  full induced map is the direct sum of the degree-wise maps. Identity,
  contravariant composition and grading preservation are explicit.

The composition identity in Federer 1.10.1 is formalised with distinct
input-space and coefficient-space parameters to disambiguate the notation.

Regressions cover a nonzero degree-zero coefficient over the zero input space,
preservation of zero-forms under arbitrary pullback, and evaluation of a
one-form on the zero vector. Item 1.1.3 is unchanged.

## 1.10.2 — Products and dual monomial bases of symmetric forms

**Covered:** the product defined by the diagonal, its algebra laws and induced
maps, the unsigned shuffle formula, the dual monomial basis, and the mixed
coefficient product (Federer, pp. 44–45). The product constructions allow
arbitrary fields and dimensions. Characteristic zero is explicit for the
factorial-normalized finite monomial bases, matching the book's real case.

- [`SymmetricFormProduct.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormProduct.v) constructs the full and fixed-degree products using
  the abstract symmetric diagonal. It proves their compatibility, bilinearity,
  grading, associativity when the coefficient multiplication is associative,
  commutativity when that multiplication is commutative, and multiplicativity
  of pullback. No associative coefficient law is assumed for the construction.
- [`SymmetricFormUnit.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormUnit.v) proves the left and right unit laws and preservation
  of the unit by induced maps. [`SymmetricFormShuffle.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFormShuffle.v) gives exactly the
  unsigned binary shuffle sum, including empty argument blocks.
- [`MixedTensorPairing.v`](../../theories/MixedTensorPairing.v), [`MixedHomogeneousConvolution.v`](../../theories/MixedHomogeneousConvolution.v), and
  [`SymmetricMixedProduct.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMixedProduct.v) construct the generalized product for an arbitrary
  bilinear map W1 × W2 → W3, prove bilinearity and degree addition, and identify
  its specialization with the preceding product.
- [`BasisRescaling.v`](../../theories/BasisRescaling.v) and [`SymmetricDividedBasis.v`](../../theories/SymmetricDividedBasis.v) prove that the divided
  monomials e^alpha/alpha! form a basis. The intermediate coordinate modules
  construct their dual functionals, compute their products by means of the
  divided diagonal, and identify the degree-one coordinates with the given
  dual one-forms.
- [`SymmetricDualMonomialProducts.v`](../../theories/SymmetricDualMonomialProducts.v) uses the actual iterated symmetric
  multiplication to define omega^alpha and proves that it equals the
  normalized coordinate functional. [`SymmetricDualMonomialBasis.v`](../../theories/SymmetricDualMonomialBasis.v) proves
  both the Kronecker pairing against divided monomials and the stated basis
  theorem. The formulas include n = 0 and m = 0.

The local dual-basis proof computes the required normalized pairings from the
divided-monomial diagonal of 1.9.4, rather than repeating. It proves the
actual product identity, rather than replacing the products with an
independently defined dual basis.

Regressions cover constant-form multiplication for an arbitrary bilinear
coefficient product, the zero form in the mixed product with three distinct
coefficient spaces, and the empty dual monomial. Item 1.1.3 is unchanged.

## 1.10.3 — Symmetric interior products

**Covered:** both interior products, their characteristic pairings,
bilinearity and uniqueness, and both displayed monomial formulas including
their zero cases (Federer, p. 45). The operations work over arbitrary fields
and in arbitrary dimension. Characteristic zero is used for the divided
monomial formulas in a finite basis.

- [`SymmetricInteriorUP.v`](../../theories/SymmetricInteriorUP.v) states the two operations through their bilinearity
  and characteristic pairings, following the reference to 1.5.1.
- The right-interior modules construct the form by precomposition with
  symmetric multiplication. The left-interior modules contract the first
  tensor factor after the symmetric diagonal. The degree statements,
  characteristic pairings, existence and uniqueness are proved separately.
  [`SymmetricPairingSeparation.v`](../../theories/SymmetricPairingSeparation.v) supplies separation in arbitrary dimension.
- [`SymmetricRightMonomialContraction.v`](../../theories/SymmetricRightMonomialContraction.v) computes ordinary monomial
  coordinates after multiplication. [`SymmetricLeftMonomialContraction.v`](../../theories/SymmetricLeftMonomialContraction.v)
  selects the surviving summand of the divided-monomial diagonal.
- [`SymmetricInteriorMonomialFormulas.v`](../../theories/SymmetricInteriorMonomialFormulas.v) transfers those calculations to the
  actual products of the dual one-forms from 1.10.2. The right formula
  contracts e^alpha with omega^beta/beta!; the left formula contracts
  e^beta/beta! with omega^alpha. Both subtract alpha from beta when the
  coordinatewise inequality holds, and return zero otherwise. Injective
  component inclusion and the proved homogeneous-form identification avoid
  hiding equality behind implicit degree transports.

The proof uses containment in the required component. It does not require
surjectivity of a contraction, which fails for the zero covector. Regressions
cover both zero-input laws and the empty-degree pairing.

## 1.10.4 — Polynomial functions and Taylor formulas

**Covered:** homogeneous and general polynomial functions as actual maps,
uniqueness of their symmetric-form representations, the linear and algebra
identifications, degree, Taylor expansion, polynomial coefficient maps and
their translation identity, and the final coordinate expansion (Federer,
pp. 45–47). Characteristic zero is explicit in results that require factorial
division or uniqueness. No finite-dimensional assumption is imposed except
for expressing the final formula in a finite basis; that formula itself
holds for any finite family of vectors.

- [`HomogeneousPolynomialFunctions.v`](../../theories/HomogeneousPolynomialFunctions.v)
defines the representation predicate and proves linearity, scaling and
uniqueness using the polarization identity of 1.9.3.
[`PolynomialCoefficientUniqueness.v`](../../theories/PolynomialCoefficientUniqueness.v)
supplies a local vector-coefficient univariate root argument using synthetic
division.
[`SymmetricPolynomialInjectivity.v`](../../theories/SymmetricPolynomialInjectivity.v)
applies it to the scalar parameter in P(tx), separating the homogeneous
coefficients without importing a polynomial algebra implementation.
- [`PolynomialFunctions.v`](../../theories/PolynomialFunctions.v) defines the
space of actual maps with a proved finite representation, constructs the two
inverse linear maps, and proves their cancellation laws.
[`PolynomialRepresentations.v`](../../theories/PolynomialRepresentations.v)
gives the precise finite list from m=0 through M and the intrinsic degree
representation.
- [`GradedDegreeUP.v`](../../theories/GradedDegreeUP.v)
specifies degree by the upper-bound and attainment laws of the supremum. A
finite-maximum construction supplies existence behind the opaque
[`GradedDegreeSealed.v`](../../theories/GradedDegreeSealed.v) interface. The
public degree of zero is **zero**, following Federer
- [`SymmetricPolynomialProduct.v`](../../theories/SymmetricPolynomialProduct.v)
identifies the symmetric form product with pointwise coefficient
multiplication, allowing arbitrary bilinear coefficient multiplication. The
proof uses the divided-power diagonal; its cancellation is equivalent to the
printed shuffle-count calculation.
- [`PolynomialTaylorTerms.v`](../../theories/PolynomialTaylorTerms.v) proves the
homogeneous binomial expansion.
[`PolynomialTaylorTermPolynomial.v`](../../theories/PolynomialTaylorTermPolynomial.v)
curries linear contractions to prove that each term is a homogeneous
polynomial valued in a symmetric-form space.
[`PolynomialTaylorCoefficients.v`](../../theories/PolynomialTaylorCoefficients.v)
collects the finite double sum and proves that S_i is polynomial.
- [`PolynomialTaylorTransform.v`](../../theories/PolynomialTaylorTransform.v)
proves independence of the support bound, retention of that bound, and
composition of translations. Polynomial uniqueness gives the
coefficient-translation identity, as an alternative to the printed double-sum
rearrangement. [`PolynomialTaylor.v`](../../theories/PolynomialTaylor.v)
states both Taylor identities for actual polynomial functions.
- [`PolynomialCoordinates.v`](../../theories/PolynomialCoordinates.v) derives
the final sum over Xi(n,m) directly from the weighted multinomial identity of
1.9.3.

Regressions cover the zero-polynomial degree convention, a nonzero constant on
the zero space, translation of the zero polynomial, and the degree bound in
the degree-zero case.

## 1.10.5 — Inner products, mass, form norms and polynomial norms

**Covered:** every assertion in Federer, pp. 47–48, with the positive-degree
qualification needed for the unweighted decomposition formula. Input spaces
may have arbitrary dimension; finite dimension is imposed only for an optimal
finite decomposition with the stated bound on its number of terms. Norms of
forms and polynomials are extended real suprema, so unbounded maps are included.

- [`InnerSymmetricUP.v`](../../theories/InnerSymmetricUP.v) specifies
extension of the given polarity as an algebra homomorphism.
[`InnerSymmetricSealed.v`](../../theories/InnerSymmetricSealed.v) hides its
existence construction. [`InnerSymmetric.v`](../../theories/InnerSymmetric.v),
[`InnerSymmetricPositivity.v`](../../theories/InnerSymmetricPositivity.v) and
[`SymmetricInnerNorm.v`](../../theories/SymmetricInnerNorm.v) prove that its
evaluation pairing is an inner product in every degree. Finite-support
reduction supplies positivity in arbitrary dimension;
[`SymmetricInnerLowDegrees.v`](../../theories/SymmetricInnerLowDegrees.v)
recovers scalar multiplication in degree zero and the original inner product
in degree one.
- [`SymmetricNormalizedMonomials.v`](../../theories/SymmetricNormalizedMonomials.v),
[`SymmetricMonomialGram.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMonomialGram.v) and
[`InnerSymmetricBasis.v`](../../theories/InnerSymmetricBasis.v) prove the
alpha-factorial Gram formula and the orthonormality of the products divided by
sqrt(alpha!).
[`InnerSymmetricPermutationFormula.v`](../../theories/InnerSymmetricPermutationFormula.v)
proves the sum over permutations.
[`SymmetricInnerProductBound.v`](../../theories/SymmetricInnerProductBound.v)
gives the sqrt(m!) product bound;
[`SymmetricInnerPurePowers.v`](../../theories/SymmetricInnerPurePowers.v)
gives the exact pure-power length.
- [`RealNormUP.v`](../../theories/RealNormUP.v) states the seminorm and norm
hypotheses as laws of a supplied function.
[`ExtendedSupremumUP.v`](../../theories/ExtendedSupremumUP.v) and
[`SymmetricOperatorNormUP.v`](../../theories/SymmetricOperatorNormUP.v)
separately specify the supremum.
[`ExtendedSupremumSealed.v`](../../theories/ExtendedSupremumSealed.v) hides
its bounded/unbounded existence construction;
[`SymmetricOperatorNorm.v`](../../theories/SymmetricOperatorNorm.v) exposes
only the laws.
[`SymmetricOperatorTupleBound.v`](../../theories/SymmetricOperatorTupleBound.v)
proves the homogeneous evaluation estimate, including zero factors and
infinite form norms.
- [`SymmetricOperatorInteriorBound.v`](../../theories/SymmetricOperatorInteriorBound.v)
proves the displayed contraction bound.
[`SymmetricOperatorProductBound.v`](../../theories/SymmetricOperatorProductBound.v)
proves the binomial product bound for any bilinear coefficient multiplication
with a submultiplicative seminorm.
[`SymmetricOneFormPowerNorm.v`](../../theories/SymmetricOneFormPowerNorm.v)
proves ||phi^m||=m!||phi||^m for m>0 under the power-norm hypothesis. Powers
are actual right-associated products in the graded form algebra, so they agree
with the source's powers when the coefficient multiplication is associative.
Neither a unit nor finite dimension is required.
[`ExtendedSupremumPowers.v`](../../theories/ExtendedSupremumPowers.v) proves
the needed supremum-of-powers step locally, including positive infinity.
- [`SymmetricMassUP.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMassUP.v) specifies mass by the
dual supremum over scalar forms of operator norm at most one. The public
[`SymmetricMass.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMass.v) uses an opaque interface
and proves positivity and all norm laws in every degree. For positive degree,
[`SymmetricMassInfimumUP.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMassInfimumUP.v)
separately specifies the finite-product infimum.
[`SymmetricProjectiveMass.v`](../../theories/SymmetricProjectiveMass.v)
accesses its sealed witness;
[`SymmetricProjectiveSeminorm.v`](../../theories/SymmetricProjectiveSeminorm.v)
proves its seminorm laws.
[`SymmetricMassDuality.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMassDuality.v) proves
equality with the dual supremum.
- [`HahnBanach.v`](../../theories/HahnBanach.v) and
[`SeminormNormingFunctional.v`](../../theories/SeminormNormingFunctional.v)
supply the separation and norming-functional argument needed for duality. This
is a local proof of the finite-valued seminorm case of Federer 2.4.12, p. 86,
using one-dimensional extension intervals and a maximal linear graph.
[`HahnBanachSealed.v`](../../theories/HahnBanachSealed.v) hides the
graph-extension construction. No external Hahn–Banach or projective
tensor-norm theorem is imported.
- [`FiniteBoundedRetraction.v`](../../theories/FiniteBoundedRetraction.v)
extends coordinate functionals by the proved Hahn–Banach result.
[`SymmetricMassMapBound.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMassMapBound.v) transfers
mass through bounded linear maps.
[`SymmetricFiniteMassPositivity.v`](../../theories/SymmetricFiniteMassPositivity.v)
and [`SymmetricMassPositivity.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMassPositivity.v)
use a finite-dimensional reduction and bounded retraction to prove positivity
in arbitrary dimension. Auxiliary finite-norm comparison and compactness facts
are proved in the project using general topology as background.
- [`SymmetricMassProducts.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMassProducts.v) proves the
mass product inequality in all degrees, including scalar factors.
[`SymmetricMassConsequences.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMassConsequences.v)
proves mass(x^m)=N(x)^m for m>0 and attainment by a unit covector in every
degree.
[`SymmetricMonomialNormBounds.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMonomialNormBounds.v)
proves alpha! <= ||omega^alpha|| <= m! and alpha!/m! <= mass(e^alpha) <= 1 for
the actual dual monomial forms. The unit hypotheses may be inequalities, a
strengthening of the printed equalities; degree zero is included.
- [`SymmetricUnitProductsCompact.v`](../../theories/SymmetricUnitProductsCompact.v)
proves compactness of unit products in finite dimension.
[`SymmetricMassConvexHull.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMassConvexHull.v)
identifies their subconvex hull with the mass unit ball.
[`SymmetricOptimalDecomposition.v`](../../theories/SymmetricOptimalDecomposition.v)
proves attainment of the infimum with at most dim(Sym^m V) summands; the
public form is
[`symmetric_mass_optimal_decomposition`](../../theories/SymmetricMassConsequences.v#L47).
The proof uses the previously formalized convex dimension reduction, and
includes the zero vector and the zero-dimensional input space.
- [`PolynomialNormUP.v`](../../theories/PolynomialNormUP.v) and
[`PolynomialNorm.v`](../../theories/PolynomialNorm.v) specify and expose the
supremum of polynomial output lengths on the unit ball.
[`PolynomialDiagonalNorm.v`](../../theories/PolynomialDiagonalNorm.v) and
[`PolynomialPolarizationNorm.v`](../../theories/PolynomialPolarizationNorm.v)
prove m!||P|| <= ||phi|| <= m^m||P|| for P(x)=<x^m/m!,phi>, in every degree
and with infinite norms allowed. The upper bound uses the already proved
polarization formula and a local finite sum estimate.
- [`HilbertPolynomialNorm.v`](../../theories/HilbertPolynomialNorm.v) proves the
sharper equality m!||P||=||phi|| for inner-product inputs. A norming
functional handles arbitrary coefficient spaces, and restriction to the finite
span of each tuple handles arbitrary input dimension. This supplies a proof of
the equality Federer attributes to [H 1], rather than importing the theorem.
It is not asserted to reproduce the details of Hörmander's proof, nor to be
stated in either.
- [`PolynomialCoefficientNorm.v`](../../theories/PolynomialCoefficientNorm.v)
and
[`PolynomialWeightedNormBound.v`](../../theories/PolynomialWeightedNormBound.v)
prove the final weighted estimate, retaining the intermediate coefficient sum.
[`ScalarDividedMultinomial.v`](../../theories/ScalarDividedMultinomial.v)
proves that the sum of |t^alpha/alpha!| is (sum |t_j|)^m/m!. The estimate
requires only a finite family of unit-ball vectors, not a basis; empty
families, degree zero and infinite norms are included.

**Boundary clarification:** the unweighted decomposition infimum and the bound
on its number of terms require **m>0**. Every degree-zero empty product is
one, so a finite sum of such products cannot represent the negative unit.
[`SymmetricZeroDegreeDecomposition.v`](../../theories/SymmetricZeroDegreeDecomposition.v)
proves this counterexample. Degree-zero mass is instead the absolute value
under the scalar identification. This is a local clarification, not an entry
in Federer's saved erratum.

The exact power-norm and Hilbert polynomial-norm identities are checked
against Federer.

Regressions check the negative degree-zero unit and decomposition obstruction,
a nonzero constant form on the zero space, the distinct mass and inner-product
lengths of a unit vector's square, an unbounded scalar one-form's square, and
the degree-zero Hilbert polynomial norm identity.

## 1.10.6 — Hilbert norms of symmetric forms (pp. 48–49)

coefficient inner product space may have arbitrary dimension. The algebraic
and inner product results use real closed fields; the actual operator suprema
use the real-field structure already needed in 1.10.5. Every degree, including
zero, is retained.
