Both constructions work in arbitrary dimension. Scalar functionals separate
vectors by
[`linear_functionals_separate`](../../theories/PairingSeparation.v#L76), which
extends a functional on the line through a nonzero vector;
[`pairing_vectors_separate`](../../theories/PairingSeparation.v#L96)
transfers this to exterior powers via the alternating-form isomorphism. Thus
the uniqueness of the left operation does not assume finite dimension or
bidual surjectivity.

The right operation permits arbitrary vector-valued forms and works over
any field. The left operation uses scalar-valued forms and the existing
characteristic-different-from-two diagonal map. Both accept the book's
p <= q hypothesis. The final right identity is proved for vector-valued
forms too; the printed scalar-valued identity is its special case.

[`exterior_reindex`](../../theories/ExteriorHomogeneousProducts.v#L67) and [`form_reindex`](../../theories/ExteriorHomogeneousProducts.v#L95) identify equal natural-number degrees,
for example q-p+p=q and t-(r+s)=(t-s)-r. Their compatibility with the
actual vectors and homogeneous linear maps is proved. They change only
the type index. No quotient, change of sign, or extra mathematical hypothesis
is hidden in these identifications. Degrees zero and p=q are included.

The characteristic predicates precede both constructions; existence and
uniqueness are separate theorems. The source notations retain the symbols
⌟ and ⌞, with explicit arguments for the degree and characteristic proofs.
[`InteriorMultiplication.v`](../../theories/InteriorMultiplication.v) is the public entry point through 1.5.1.

The bases e and omega are arbitrary dual bases, with their duality expressed
by omega_i(e_j)=delta_ij. Omega is used to transfer the ordering calculation
for vectors to covector products, following the preceding sections of the
book. The calculation uses the already defined inversion sign; it does not
introduce an independent sign convention. Ordinal index i represents
Federer's i+1, which preserves the inequalities in both pair counts.

[`D_upper`](../../theories/DualityMaps.v#L58) is the source identifier for the superscript D^p, to distinguish
it from the subscript [`D_p`](../../theories/DualityMaps.v#L31). Their definitions use the top covector and
top vector directly, before proving the basis formulae and isomorphism
laws. On complementary basis products the two signs in either composite
are equal, so their product is one. Linearity then extends the inverse
identities to every vector and covector. No matrix inversion is used.

The two final displays belong to 1.5.2 although they are printed on page 23,
before the heading 1.5.3. They are included in this item. Explicit reindexing
maps only identify equal natural-number degrees; the underlying vectors
and homogeneous maps remain the same. The hypotheses p<=q, p<=n and
r+s<=n are supplied where the operations require them. Zero degrees,
p=n and the zero-dimensional space are included.

This item uses the existing characteristic-different-from-two hypothesis
and introduces no axiom. [`InteriorDuality.v`](../../theories/InteriorDuality.v) is its public entry point.

The positive degree j is written n+1. [`v_interior`](../../theories/InteriorOneDegree.v#L51), [`wedge_alpha`](../../theories/InteriorOneDegree.v#L58),
[`left_alpha`](../../theories/InteriorOneDegree.v#L153) and [`v_wedge`](../../theories/InteriorOneDegree.v#L160) are the operations already constructed in 1.5.1,
followed only by identification of the equal degrees n+1-1=n and n+1.
Their evaluation lemmas identify the unchanged vectors and homogeneous
maps. The names beta and psi retain their roles in Federer's decomposition.

The two product calculations follow the two summands of Psi and its
signed tensor product, using the characteristic constructions of 1.5.1.
The counit gives the identity term, and the degree-one condition gives the
minus sign. The auxiliary decomposition and its two annihilation conditions
are established before the main displayed statement. The two equivalences
and direct sum then follow in book order. The proof uses no finite basis
or assumption of finite-dimensionality.

When j=1, beta is a zero-form. The book's expression v interior beta then
requires a convention beyond the bound p<=q in 1.5.1. The theorem
[`v_interior_zero_degree`](../../theories/InteriorOneDegree.v#L135) proves directly that the corresponding Hom
composition is zero. Thus this boundary case is justified explicitly.
The nilpotency of both operations is also proved, from v wedge v=0 and
alpha wedge alpha=0, rather than assumed.

This item uses the existing characteristic-different-from-two hypothesis
and introduces no axiom. [`InteriorDecomposition.v`](../../theories/InteriorDecomposition.v) is its public entry point.
