In particular, this is **not a complete formalisation of §1.1**. The coordinate
example does not establish the general injectivity theorem of 1.1.3. A specific
real-number implementation has not been instantiated; the algebraic results
apply after supplying its `fieldType` structure.

The tensor product of V_1, …, V_m is the predicate
[`is_tensor_product_family`](../../theories/TensorFamilyUP.v#L39) on an
m-linear map; the factors are recorded by its domain. The quotient
construction in
[`TensorFamilyConstruction.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyConstruction.v)
serves only to prove existence, and the other results of
[`TensorFamilyUP.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyUP.v) and
[`TensorFamilySpan.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilySpan.v) use only the
universal property. Each homogeneous component of the tensor algebra is, by
`is_graded_tensor_algebra`, an m-fold tensor product of V in this sense. The
remaining §1.1 work includes the other canonical isomorphisms. The statement
of 1.1.3 should follow.

In [`TensorFamilyConstruction.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyConstruction.v), [`family_relation`](../../theories/TensorFamilyConstruction.v#L48) is the inductively
generated subspace: it contains the relations expressing additivity and
homogeneity in each component and is closed under zero, addition and scalar
multiplication. Its definition does not presuppose a basis. The tensor product
of V_1, …, V_m is the quotient of the finitely supported functions on
V_1 × … × V_m by this subspace. The construction is used only to prove
[`tensor_family_exists`](../../theories/TensorFamilyConstruction.v#L181); [`TensorSealed.v`](../../theories/TensorSealed.v) hides it behind the universal
property, and V ⊗ W is the case of the family (V, W).

## Chapter 1 conventions and source scope

The development is intrinsic and permits arbitrary-dimensional vector spaces.
It uses an arbitrary `fieldType` where possible. The graded universal
property and direct-sum/basis results in §1.3 retain Federer's hypothesis
that the characteristic differs from two; the square-zero universal
property and exterior-algebra construction work in every characteristic.
Identifications between differently represented spaces are specified linear
isomorphisms. Section 1.1 remains partial; §§1.2–1.10 cover their numbered
items with the qualifications in their entries, and the questions in
1.8.3–1.8.4 remain questions. Completion includes claims within the prose
and intermediate identities, not merely the headline statements.

Federer (1969), pp. 8–49, is the primary source. Source locators and the
erratum conventions are in [the reference guide](../references/README.md).
