The coordinate example does not establish its general injectivity theorem. A specific
real-number implementation has not been instantiated; the algebraic results
apply after supplying its `fieldType` structure.

The tensor product of V_1, …, V_m is the predicate
[`is_tensor_product_family`](../../theories/TensorFamilyUP.v#L39) on an
m-linear map; the factors are recorded by its domain. The quotient
construction in
[`TensorFamilyConstruction.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyConstruction.v)
serves only to prove existence, and the other results of
[`TensorFamilyUP.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyUP.v) and
[`TensorFamilySpan.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilySpan.v) use only the
universal property. Each homogeneous component of the tensor algebra is, by
`is_graded_tensor_algebra`, an m-fold tensor product of V in this sense. The remaining §1.1 work is the deliberately deferred 1.1.3.

## 1.1.2 — Complete canonical maps and bases (p. 10)

All mathematical assertions of this item are proved and validated. The public supplementary interface is
[`TensorFamilyCanonical.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyCanonical.v); it keeps the existing tensor spaces sealed and
is reachable by the kernel checks through [`tests/Examples.v`](../../tests/Examples.v).

For every permutation, dependent coordinate transport gives inverse tuple
maps. Their update laws prove multilinearity, and the two abstract tensor
universal properties give mutually inverse linear maps with the exact
printed reordered pure-product law. No common factor space is assumed.

[`TensorFamilyBlocks.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyBlocks.v) proves exact split/join and coordinate-update laws
for the first m and last k original factors. [`TensorFamilyGrouping.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyGrouping.v)
extends a multilinear function through each block's universal property,
proves bilinearity of the extension, and then proves that the grouped
space has the full original universal property. Abstract uniqueness gives
the grouping isomorphism. This permits arbitrary block sizes, with zero
blocks as a supporting extension of the printed positive-block setting.

The direct-sum argument retains precisely the three printed hypotheses
on f, phi, g and psi. Tensoring preserves all three identities. The mixed
composites are derived to be zero; the two projections form a linear
bijection with inverse the sum of the injections. No finite dimension is
assumed. The earlier scalar multiplication isomorphism is retained.

For arbitrary basis index sets J_i, products are a basis indexed by their
actual dependent Cartesian product. Products of coordinate functionals
prove independence by the universal property. Finite induction through the
argument positions, expanding each vector in its supplied basis, proves
spanning. This proof is independent of the grouping theorem and therefore
has no circular basis argument. Infinite and empty factor bases and the
empty list of factors remain included. The product-index description is
the general basis-cardinality dimension formula; finite index sets give
the actual MathComp dimension as the product of the factor dimensions.
The original tensor carrier receives the proved finite-dimensional
structure and is not replaced by a coordinate construction.

Three regressions retain the dimension-one empty tensor family, an actual
zero factor with empty basis, and nonzero basis tensors from an actual
natural-indexed free vector space. Every new declaration and the example
lemmas are included in the assumptions audit. No new source correction,
project axiom or external mathematical dependency is introduced.

## 1.1.4 — The general dual–tensor map (p. 11)

Every mathematical assertion of this item is proved and validated. [`HomTensorGeneral.v`](../../theories/HomTensorGeneral.v) retains the actual original
[`tensor_Hom`](../../theories/HomTensor.v#L48) transformation and its defining evaluation. Injectivity holds
for arbitrary-dimensional V and W. The proof uses the earlier proved
finite-support and finite-evaluation separation lemmas, without assuming
an ambient basis or adding a finite-dimensional hypothesis.

When W has a finite basis e_i, compose its dual coordinate functions with
f and sum those covectors tensored with e_i. The original Phi carries this
explicit candidate to f by basis expansion. General injectivity gives the
other inverse law, linearity, and independence of the selected target
basis. Together with the earlier finite-source inverse, this proves
surjectivity whenever either original space has a finite basis, precisely
the printed alternative. It does not require both spaces finite. The
original finite-source dual basis and its characterising equations remain
proved in [`FiniteDualDimension.v`](../../theories/FiniteDualDimension.v).

The supporting injectivity argument expands the printed basis-of-W
justification using finite evaluations on the finite support, so it never
chooses a global infinite basis. The target-basis inverse follows the
printed argument directly. The original tensor implementation is opaque;
all downstream proofs use its universal, evaluation and inverse laws.

[`HomTensorInfinite.v`](../../theories/HomTensorInfinite.v) proves that every tensor map has common finite
coordinate support after any linear readout into the countable free
space. The earlier proved countable inclusions and retractions in the two
original infinite spaces give an actual linear map with infinitely many
independent coordinate values. It has no tensor preimage. A proved
finite-basis-or-infinite alternative gives the exact surjectivity iff
criterion, without assuming an infinite ambient basis.

Four regressions exercise a nonzero map from the actual infinite free
vector space to a one-dimensional target and its constructed inverse,
the entire tensor with an actual zero target, and the actual canonical
map evaluated between two natural-indexed infinite free vector spaces,
and the identity of that free space lying outside the image of Phi.
All new declarations and the regression data are audited. No new source
correction, project axiom or external mathematical dependency is introduced.

The [item validation](../checkpoints/1.1.4-validation.json) records normal
registered compilation of both theories and the four boundary regressions,
the final incremental build in 36.47 seconds, complete recursive kernel
checking in 927.54 seconds, and 96 strict dependency queries in 131.98 seconds.
All 20,530 previous assumption sets are preserved; the complete log has
20,549 entries with only the three permitted boolp assumptions. Every
registered input stayed unchanged; no warnings, cleaning, adoption or
private proof-object copies were used. Inspected active artifacts have
group llm and group read/write permissions.

In [`TensorFamilyConstruction.v`](../../theories/TensorFamilyConstruction.v), [`family_relation`](../../theories/TensorFamilyConstruction.v#L48) is the inductively
generated subspace: it contains the relations expressing additivity and
homogeneity in each component and is closed under zero, addition and scalar
multiplication. Its definition does not presuppose a basis. The tensor product
of V_1, …, V_m is the quotient of the finitely supported functions on
V_1 × … × V_m by this subspace. The construction is used only to prove
[`tensor_family_exists`](../../theories/TensorFamilyConstruction.v#L181); [`TensorSealed.v`](../../theories/TensorSealed.v) hides it behind the universal
property, and V ⊗ W is the case of the family (V, W).

## Chapter 1 conventions and source scope

The development is intrinsic and permits arbitrary-dimensional vector spaces.
It uses an arbitrary `fieldType` where possible. The graded universal
property and direct-sum/basis results in §1.3 retain Federer's hypothesis
that the characteristic differs from two; the square-zero universal
property and exterior-algebra construction work in every characteristic.
Identifications between differently represented spaces are specified linear
isomorphisms. Section 1.1 is complete apart from the intentionally deferred
1.1.3; §§1.2–1.10 cover their numbered
items with the qualifications in their entries, and the questions in
1.8.3–1.8.4 remain questions. Completion includes claims within the prose
and intermediate identities, not merely the headline statements.

Federer (1969), pp. 8–49, is the primary source. Source locators and the
erratum conventions are in [the reference guide](../references/README.md).
