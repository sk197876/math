The form product is constructed from the diagonal map before the shuffle
formula is proved, following Federer's order. General coefficient algebras
are represented by a vector space with a bilinear multiplication;

also supplies the actual [`algebra`](../../theories/AlgebraUP.v#L25) and [`unital_graded_algebra`](../../theories/UnitalGradedTensor.v#L26) structures.

The iterated product is right-associated. Its formula is proved without
assuming associativity, so it applies in particular to Federer's associative
case. The proved associativity law allows the unparenthesised notation of
the book. The order of the coefficient factors is retained throughout.
[`nonempty_product`](../../theories/NonemptyProduct.v#L26) excludes the empty-family case in statements about
algebras without a unit; [`unital_forms_product`](../../theories/UnitalFamily.v#L47) uses the coefficient unit

As in 1.3.3, ordinal position i corresponds to Federer's i+1.

characteristic different from two, as assumed for the exterior diagonal.
The coefficient spaces and the source vector space may have arbitrary
dimension. The one-form formula uses the same dependent family of degrees
as the iterated formula, together with the explicit hypothesis that each
degree is one; every displayed single-argument evaluation has that degree.

#### Corrections to the coefficient-algebra parenthesis in 1.4.4

Here `K` is the scalar field (the real numbers in Federer), `W` is a
unital associative `K`-algebra, and `A^*(V,W)` is the algebra of alternating
`W`-valued forms. Distinguish the exterior algebra over `K` from the exterior
algebra over `W`; changing this coefficient ring changes the assertion.

**Existence of the scalar-based map requires an additional hypothesis.**
Federer's assertion for every associative `W` is false. For a one-form
`a : V -> W`, the alternating product satisfies

\[
(a\wedge a)(v,w)=a(v)a(w)-a(w)a(v).
\]

A homomorphism from `exterior_K Hom_K(V,W)` extending the identity on
one-forms would send the square-zero generator `a` to `a`, forcing the
right-hand side to vanish for every `v,w`. Take `V=K^2` and the free
associative algebra `W=K<X,Y>`, with `a(e_1)=X` and `a(e_2)=Y`.
Then `(a wedge a)(e_1,e_2)=XY-YX` is nonzero, as its coefficient at the
word `XY` is one. Thus the proposed homomorphism does not exist.
The implementation represents `K^2` by the free vector space on two
letters. The checked results are [`one_forms_wedge_two`](../../theories/OneFormCommutator.v#L101),
[`exterior_extension_forces_commuting_values`](../../theories/NoncommutativeObstruction.v#L38),
[`two_letters_do_not_commute`](../../theories/NoncommutativeObstruction.v#L89), and
[`noncommutative_exterior_extension_impossible`](../../theories/NoncommutativeObstruction.v#L108).

A sufficient correction is to require **commutative** `W`. Over the real
numbers (and the repository's fields of characteristic different from two),
the alternating algebra is then graded anticommutative and the required
unique unit-preserving graded homomorphism exists. This is the map
[`Omega_W`](../../theories/OmegaConstruction.v#L53), characterised by [`Omega_W_one`](../../theories/OmegaConstruction.v#L73) and [`Omega_W_unique`](../../theories/OmegaConstruction.v#L109).
Commutativity is sufficient here; no claim is made that it is necessary
for every particular choice of `V`.

**Finite dimension of `V` does not make that scalar-based map surjective.**
Its degree-zero source is `K`, whereas its degree-zero target is `W`.
It sends `c` to `c 1_W`. Take the commutative algebra `W=K x K`, with
coordinatewise multiplication and unit `(1,1)`. The constant zero-form
`(1,0)` has no preimage: `(1,0)=(c,c)` would imply `1=0`. Positive-degree
terms cannot supply a missing degree-zero value, since the map preserves
grading. This counterexample works for every `V`, including finite-dimensional
and zero-dimensional `V`. See [`Omega_W_coefficient`](../../theories/CoefficientImage.v#L56),
[`Omega_W_surjective_requires_scalar_coefficients`](../../theories/CoefficientImage.v#L72), and
[`coefficient_pair_not_surjective`](../../theories/CoefficientCounterexample.v#L114).

**The printed noninjectivity assertion also needs an exception.**
Take `V=0` and again `W=K x K`. Then `Hom_K(V,W)=0`, its scalar exterior
algebra is `K` in degree zero, and the map is `c |-> (c,c)`. It is injective
although `dim_K W=2`. See [`zero_domain_Omega_pair_injective`](../../theories/ZeroDimensionalCoefficients.v#L78) and
[`coefficient_pair_dimension`](../../theories/ZeroDimensionalCoefficients.v#L134). This refutes the unrestricted assertion.

For nonzero `V` and commutative `W` of dimension greater than one, the
noninjectivity assertion has the following corrected proof. Choose a nonzero
linear functional `ell : V -> K` and linearly independent `a,b` in `W`.
The one-forms `f(v)=ell(v)a` and `g(v)=ell(v)b` are linearly independent
(evaluate a linear relation at a vector where `ell` is nonzero). Consequently
`f wedge g` is nonzero in the scalar exterior algebra of `Hom_K(V,W)`.
Its image is zero, since

\[
(f\wedge g)(v,w)=\ell(v)\ell(w)ab-\ell(w)\ell(v)ab=0.
\]

Thus adding `V != 0` restores this assertion after imposing commutative
coefficients. The checked results named above establish the counterexample
to the unrestricted printed statement; this general replacement is explained
here by the independent-pair argument.

**The corrected coefficient-ring statement is the final statement of
Federer's parenthesis.** For commutative unital `W`, regard `Hom_K(V,W)`
as a `W`-module and use

\[
\Omega_W^{\mathrm{rel}}:
\Lambda_W^*\operatorname{Hom}_K(V,W)\longrightarrow A^*(V,W).
\]

This map is `W`-linear, is the identity on coefficients and on one-forms,
and is an isomorphism when `V` is finite-dimensional over `K`. Its degree-zero
source is now `W`, so the preceding degree-zero obstruction disappears.
The universal property, construction, and finite-dimensional inverse laws
are checked by [`relative_Hom_exterior_exists`](../../theories/RelativeOmega.v#L102), [`Omega_relative_unique`](../../theories/RelativeOmega.v#L85),
[`Omega_relative_W_linear`](../../theories/RelativeOmega.v#L74), and [`Omega_relative_finite_bijective`](../../theories/RelativeOmegaFinite.v#L160). The theorem
allows zero divisors in `W`; it is not restricted to coefficient fields.

They therefore do not supply corrections to its three false assertions.

The last assertion uses the **superscript** one in the book: the map on
one-covectors is contravariant. It is not replaced by the tautological
first exterior-power assertion.

[`A`](../../theories/TraceAlgebra.v#L36) is an actual subalgebra of the ordinary tensor product. Its component
maps are proved injective, their images are precisely its homogeneous
components, and every element is a finite sum of those components.
The calculation of its multiplication uses the two exterior signs, whose
product is one. [`Gamma`](../../theories/Gamma.v#L56) and [`Gamma_m`](../../theories/GammaPower.v#L59) use the natural pairings; the finite
bases compute their inverse maps and do not become additional hypotheses
about those pairings. The name [`Gamma_A`](../../theories/TraceDefinition.v#L56) denotes the same map followed by
its specified inclusion into A.

The proof of the divided-power formula expands products of commuting
square-zero terms and enumerates the increasing subsets. It first proves
the identity with a factorial multiplier in every allowed characteristic,
then divides when that factorial is nonzero. The determinant and
characteristic-polynomial formulae use characteristic zero, as is valid
for Federer's real scalar field. Results concerning exterior products
retain the characteristic-different-from-two hypothesis. Degrees zero,
empty products, and n=0 are included. The contraction formula is also
proved for j>n-m, when its binomial coefficient vanishes.

Rocq gives the two domains of the book's overloaded word [`trace`](../../theories/TraceDefinition.v#L115) separate
names: [`trace`](../../theories/TraceDefinition.v#L115) on A and [`trace_Hom`](../../theories/TraceDefinition.v#L149) on endomorphisms. [`exterior_trace`](../../theories/CharacteristicIdentities.v#L91) is its
application to an induced exterior map. The stated abbreviation and its
compatibility are proved; no matrix trace or matrix determinant is used.

The opening exception is recorded as a refutation rather than silently
adding a positive-dimension hypothesis. Its valid nondegenerate assertion
is proved for spaces containing independent pairs, which also covers
infinite-dimensional spaces. No additional axiom is declared.
[`GrassmannTrace.v`](../../theories/GrassmannTrace.v) is the public entry point through 1.4.5.
