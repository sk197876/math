### 1.9.1 — The symmetric algebra and its universal properties

The symmetric algebra is specified abstractly in [`SymmetricAlgebraUP.v`](../../theories/SymmetricAlgebraUP.v):
its multiplication is commutative, words have their length as homogeneous
degree, and symmetric multilinear maps factor uniquely through each power.
[`GradedSymmetricUP.v`](../../theories/GradedSymmetricUP.v) separately states Federer's graded algebra universal
property and proves uniqueness up to a unique graded unital isomorphism.
The ungraded commutative-target property is also derived; this is a local
strengthening of the printed graded statement.

Existence follows the book's commutator quotient. [`SymmetricIdeal.v`](../../theories/SymmetricIdeal.v) proves
that the span of adjacent-swap differences between arbitrary prefix and
suffix words is the least two-sided ideal containing the generator
commutators. It proves homogeneity, vanishing degree-zero and degree-one
components, and the direct-sum decomposition starting in degree two.
The private quotient modules prove commutativity, grading, and the
symmetric-multilinear factorisation. [`SymmetricQuotientPowers.v`](../../theories/SymmetricQuotientPowers.v) verifies
the displayed component-quotient isomorphism. [`SymmetricSealed.v`](../../theories/SymmetricSealed.v) hides
these constructions behind opaque module ascription.

The public algebra, grading and map modules use only the abstract interface.
They prove word spanning, multiplication and permutation laws, the mutually
inverse linear identifications `Sym_0(V)=K` and `Sym_1(V)=V`, and the unique
graded extension of every linear map into degree one of a commutative
unital graded target. The induced algebra map is the direct sum of its
symmetric-power maps; identity, composition and injection laws are explicit.
All spaces may have arbitrary dimension and the construction works over
any field. No square-zero relation or characteristic restriction is imposed.

**Local printing correction.** On printed p. 41 the direct-sum display for the
full symmetric algebra starts at `m=2`. It must start at `m=0`, as the next
sentence's identifications of degrees zero and one require. The ideal's sum
correctly starts at two. There is no p. 41 entry in the saved May 1987 Federer
erratum; this correction is not attributed to that erratum.

Source passages: Federer 1.9.1, pp. 41–42 The regression cases cover the
nonzero empty product in the zero ambient space, its zero first power,
nonvanishing of a repeated scalar-one generator, and the induced map on the
scalar unit.

### 1.9.2 — Direct sums, monomial bases and dimension

[`SymmetricDirectSum.v`](../../theories/SymmetricDirectSum.v) proves the binary direct-sum isomorphism with the
ordinary, commutative tensor product of the two symmetric algebras. Both
maps are graded unital algebra maps, and both compositions are identities.
The theorem takes abstract splitting maps, so it applies to any realisation
of the direct sum. The ordinary tensor multiplication is supplied by
[`OrdinaryTensorMultiplication.v`](../../theories/OrdinaryTensorMultiplication.v) using the existing tensor interface.

The proof of the monomial basis follows the dimension induction; splitting off
one coordinate and taking a tensor-product basis gives the general finite
case.
[`SymmetricCoordinateMonomials.v`](../../theories/SymmetricCoordinateMonomials.v)
identifies the resulting recursive basis with the book's products of powers,
and [`SymmetricMonomialBasis.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMonomialBasis.v)
transports it to any given finite basis of V. The degree-m component is
indexed by the finite set Xi(n,m) of nonnegative exponent tuples of sum m.

[`SymmetricIndexCardinality.v`](../../theories/SymmetricIndexCardinality.v)
proves the cardinality using a first-coordinate recursion and Pascal's
identity. This is a local counting proof of the same formula that.
[`SymmetricDimension.v`](../../theories/SymmetricDimension.v) gives dim
Sym_m(V)=C(m+n,n) when dim V=n+1. For V=0 the dimension is one in degree zero
and zero in positive degree. All results allow arbitrary scalar fields. No
external polynomial-algebra implementation is imported.

Sources: Federer 1.9.2, p. 42 Regressions cover zero ambient dimension, three
quadratic monomials in two variables, nonvanishing powers on the scalar line,
and the zero second symmetric power of the zero space.

### 1.9.3 — Multinomial and polarization formulas

[`SymmetricMultinomial.v`](../../theories/SymmetricMultinomial.v) proves the weighted multinomial formula for
(sum_i t_i v_i)^m/m!, with the book's exponent-tuple indexing and
coefficient t^alpha/alpha!. The proof proceeds by induction on the number
of vectors, using the locally proved binomial formula and a decomposition
of the exponent set by its first coordinate. Supporting modules state
factorial, monomial, and degree identities explicitly.

[`SymmetricPolarization.v`](../../theories/SymmetricPolarization.v) then follows Federer's argument: expand the sum
over signs, interchange the finite sums, and cancel every exponent tuple
except the all-ones tuple. The resulting sign sum is 2^m times the product
of the m generators. [`SymmetricPolarizationCoefficients.v`](../../theories/SymmetricPolarizationCoefficients.v) proves the
cancellation and the balanced-index argument separately. The results hold
in characteristic zero, extending the sources' real scalar field, and
include degree zero as an explicit empty-product extension.

Sources: Federer 1.9.3, pp. 42–43 Regression cases check the degree-zero
divided power, degree-one binomial identity, and the missing-exponent sign
cancellation (the latter over any field).

### 1.9.4 — The symmetric diagonal

[`SymmetricDiagonal.v`](../../theories/SymmetricDiagonal.v) defines the unique unital graded algebra map sending
v to v tensor 1 plus 1 tensor v, using the symmetric universal property.
It proves naturality and cocommutativity; [`SymmetricCoassociativity.v`](../../theories/SymmetricCoassociativity.v)
proves coassociativity using the same uniqueness argument.
[`SymmetricCounit.v`](../../theories/SymmetricCounit.v) additionally proves the augmentation and counit laws,
a supporting extension of the properties listed in the book.

[`SymmetricShuffleFormula.v`](../../theories/SymmetricShuffleFormula.v) proves the unsigned shuffle expansion for
a product of generators. The proof expands the product by subsets and
then reindexes those subsets by shuffles. The combinatorial infrastructure
is shared with the earlier exterior-algebra development; the tensor product
here uses ordinary multiplication, without exterior signs.

[`SymmetricDiagonalFormula.v`](../../theories/SymmetricDiagonalFormula.v) and [`SymmetricDiagonalBinomial.v`](../../theories/SymmetricDiagonalBinomial.v) give both
printed monomial formulas, grouped by total degree p and exponent tuples
beta <= alpha. The divided formula has factors e^beta/beta! and
e^(alpha-beta)/(alpha-beta)!; the ordinary formula has the product of
coordinatewise binomial coefficients. Both hold for any vector family,
so in particular for a basis. The grouped formulas assume characteristic
zero, which includes Federer's real field; the diagonal, shuffle expansion,
and one-variable binomial formula hold over arbitrary fields.

**Local printing correction:** Federer's ordinary monomial display ends
the coefficient product at alpha_m,beta_m, although alpha and beta have
n coordinates. The formal product runs over all n coordinates. This is
not an entry in the saved Federer erratum.

Sources: Federer 1.9.4, p. 43 The required denominator is (alpha-beta)!, as in
Federer and as used in the ensuing argument. This was checked against both
original PDFs and is not a correction attributed to Federer's erratum.
Regressions check the empty product in the zero ambient space, a zero
generator, and the mixed coefficient two in the diagonal of a square.
