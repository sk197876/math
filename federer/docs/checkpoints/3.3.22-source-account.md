# Federer 3.3.22 source account — 5 November 2026

Reviewed printed pp. 309–310 of
[Federer1969](../bibliography.md#federer1969). This closing item recounts
historical results and questions about density and rectifiability. Its
last paragraph explicitly says that the preceding results are neither
proved nor used in the book. They are therefore source commentary, not
additional hypotheses or claimed formalised theorems.

The first paragraph discusses the converse direction to the first
implication in 3.2.19: density one almost everywhere on a subset of
Euclidean space should imply countable Hausdorff rectifiability. Federer
reports the cases m=1,n=2; m=1 in every ambient dimension; and m=2,n=3.
His suggestion that the last method might extend is historical discussion.

The next paragraph formulates the then-conjectural assertion that a Borel
regular measure with a positive finite common integer-degree density
almost everywhere should give countable measure rectifiability. The
continuation on p. 310 reports the m=1 cases, the stronger printed
1.01 upper/lower-density condition, and partial results for higher degrees.
These are accounts of the literature as presented in 1969; no modern
answer, general density theorem or unstated measurability hypothesis is
assumed by this formalisation.

The final mathematical discussion contrasts the one-dimensional
connected finite-length result (with a reference to 2.5.16) with the
higher-dimensional obstruction. Its reference to 4.2.25 is forward-looking:
a compact totally disconnected subset of three-space can lie in a sphere
whose complement of that subset is smooth with finite area. That Chapter 4
construction remains a forward reference and is not formalised or imported
here. Chapter 4 has not begun.

The further unrectifiable examples listed at the end of 3.3.21 are likewise
bibliographical pointers; the actual numbered Cantor example itself is
proved and validated. The source account does not turn those cited papers
into additional completed examples. All mathematical items 3.3.1–10 and
3.3.12–21 have separate registered validation records. Items 3.3.11 and
3.3.22 have historical source accounts.
