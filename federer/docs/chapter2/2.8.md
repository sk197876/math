## 2.8.1 — Fine coverings and adequate families

Federer, pp. 141–142. [`CoveringFamilyUP.v`](../../theories/CoveringFamilyUP.v) specifies the standing
hypotheses and the two definitions. [`metric_open_ball`](../../theories/CoveringFamilyUP.v#L22) is U(a,r); the
existing [`metric_closed_ball`](../../theories/MetricClosedBalls.v#L16) is B(a,r). [`metric_set_bounded`](../../theories/CoveringFamilyUP.v#L29) expresses
boundedness by finiteness of the previously specified extended diameter.
The empty set has diameter zero.

[`is_covering_theory_context`](../../theories/CoveringFamilyUP.v#L37) states that F consists of closed sets, mu
is a measure on all subsets, open sets are measurable, and bounded sets
have finite measure. It does not require separability, compactness,
Borel regularity, or positive measure of nonempty sets. The context is
separate from the definitions of fineness and adequacy.

- `family_covers_finely F A`: every point a of A belongs to a member
  B of F contained in U(a,epsilon), for every positive epsilon.
- `is_adequate_cover_selection mu F A V G`: G is a countable disjointed
  subfamily of F, its union lies in V, and the uncovered part of
  V intersect A is null.
- `family_is_measure_adequate mu F A`: such a selection exists for every
  open V. No measurability of the target A is imposed.

[`CoveringFamilyLaws.v`](../../theories/CoveringFamilyLaws.v) derives monotonicity in the target and the family,
measurability of eligible members, finiteness of balls under the standing
hypotheses, and the empty/null boundary cases. In particular, a family can
be adequate for a nonempty null target without covering it finely.

[`AdequateCoverEstimates.v`](../../theories/AdequateCoverEstimates.v) makes the disjoint-selection estimates in the
motivating paragraph precise: the selected measure sum lies between
mu(V intersect A) and mu(V). The second bound uses measurability and
disjointness of the selected members. The theorems are
[`adequate_selection_target_bound`](../../theories/AdequateCoverEstimates.v#L23), [`adequate_selection_neighborhood_bound`](../../theories/AdequateCoverEstimates.v#L47),
and [`adequate_family_neighborhood_estimates`](../../theories/AdequateCoverEstimates.v#L111). Infinite values are retained.
The informal discussion of enlargements and bounded overlap motivates
the later covering theorems; no additional overlap theorem is asserted
under this item.

The definitions, consequences, and precise disjoint-selection estimates
of this item are formalised. All four new theory modules and the three
regression examples passed their individual Rocq checks without warnings.
The dependency queries for the 38 new declarations passed with only the
three permitted boolp assumptions. The five new modules also passed
individual kernel checks against their compiled dependencies. A full
rebuild, recursive whole-project kernel check, and full audit were not run,
as requested by the user. See [verification.txt](../verification.txt).

## 2.8.2 — Adequacy from local contraction

Federer, pp. 142–143. [`federer_2_8_2`](../../theories/CoverContractionCriterion.v#L21) in
[`CoverContractionCriterion.v`](../../theories/CoverContractionCriterion.v) proves the printed theorem: for a countable
family K, a function sigma defined on K with 0 < sigma(A) < 1, and
countable disjoint selections in each open W leaving at most
sigma(A) times mu(W intersect A) uncovered, F is adequate for union K.
The standing context is that of 2.8.1. The targets need not be measurable,
bounded, or of finite measure.

[`ContractionCoverUP.v`](../../theories/ContractionCoverUP.v) specifies the selection and exhaustion laws.
[`RecurrentEnumerationUP.v`](../../theories/RecurrentEnumerationUP.v) specifies the recurrent enumeration, including
finite nonempty families. Its existence proof enumerates K times the
naturals and is hidden by [`RecurrentEnumerationSealed.v`](../../theories/RecurrentEnumerationSealed.v).
[`RecurrentContraction.v`](../../theories/RecurrentContraction.v) proves that a decreasing finite nonnegative
sequence with cofinally recurring strict contractions tends to zero.
[`FiniteSubfamilyApproximation.v`](../../theories/FiniteSubfamilyApproximation.v) retains a strict error bound in a finite
subfamily, discarding any empty padding that is not eligible.

[`FiniteContractionSelection.v`](../../theories/FiniteContractionSelection.v) applies that approximation to the measure
restricted to the target. This avoids imposing target measurability and
retains Federer's factor sqrt(sigma). The finite closed selected unions
leave open remainders. [`CoverContractionExhaustionConstruction.v`](../../theories/CoverContractionExhaustionConstruction.v) proves
existence of this exhaustion; [`CoverContractionExhaustion.v`](../../theories/CoverContractionExhaustion.v) exposes only
the sealed abstract laws. [`ContractionExhaustionLaws.v`](../../theories/ContractionExhaustionLaws.v) proves countability,
disjointness, containment, and the exact residual-intersection identity.
[`ContractionExhaustionAdequacy.v`](../../theories/ContractionExhaustionAdequacy.v) proves the null remainder using recurrent
contraction. [`FiniteTargetContractionCriterion.v`](../../theories/FiniteTargetContractionCriterion.v) assembles this argument
when each target has finite outer measure.

[`FiniteOpenMeasureCover.v`](../../theories/FiniteOpenMeasureCover.v)
specifies a countable finite-measure open cover and proves its existence from
the standing context, also for an empty ambient space.
[`OpenRestrictionContraction.v`](../../theories/OpenRestrictionContraction.v)
and
[`CountableTargetContractionCriterion.v`](../../theories/CountableTargetContractionCriterion.v)
give the reduction to finite measure targets A intersect U_n. A representative
assigns one of the original contraction factors to each restricted target. All
selected construction witnesses are used through their abstract
specifications.

The erratum, p. 3, book p. 142 line 33, corrects the preliminary family
to the empty set. Formal index zero begins with the first actual
selection: W_0 is printed W_1=V and G_0 is printed G_1. This removes the
preliminary empty stage and does not change the resulting family.
The empty K case is proved separately. Regression examples check it,
repetition for singleton families, and the zero-error finite selection.

## 2.8.4 — Weighted disjoint selections

Federer, p. 143, with the correction in the erratum, p. 3, book p. 143
line 17. [`federer_2_8_4`](../../theories/WeightedCoverSelection.v#L43) in [`WeightedCoverSelection.v`](../../theories/WeightedCoverSelection.v) takes a bounded
nonnegative weight delta defined on F and a real factor tau>1. Every
member of F must be nonempty. It supplies a disjointed G contained in F
such that every T in F meets some S in G with delta(T)<=tau delta(S).
No positivity of the weight and no countability of the selection is assumed.

[`WeightedCoverSelectionUP.v`](../../theories/WeightedCoverSelectionUP.v) specifies the selected family and the
auxiliary class Q of saturated disjointed families used in the printed
proof. [`WeightedSaturationLaws.v`](../../theories/WeightedSaturationLaws.v) proves closure under arbitrary chain
unions and the insertion of a sufficiently large remaining set.
[`LargeWeightedMember.v`](../../theories/LargeWeightedMember.v) proves that such a member exists, including the
case of zero supremum and without assuming that a maximum is attained.
[`WeightedCoverSelectionConstruction.v`](../../theories/WeightedCoverSelectionConstruction.v) applies the background set-theoretic
Zorn principle, then rules out an eligible set disjoint from the maximal
family. [`WeightedCoverSelectionSealed.v`](../../theories/WeightedCoverSelectionSealed.v) hides this construction; public
consequences use only the selected family's laws.

The conclusion uses only set theory, so the formal theorem permits any choice
type X and omits the unused standing metric, closedness and measure
hypotheses. The auxiliary interface uses a total weight whose values outside F
are irrelevant; the public book theorem retains a weight with domain F itself.
The selected family is not asserted unique.

[`zero_weight_singleton_selection`](../../tests/CoveringExamples.v#L168)
checks that a nonempty singleton family can select itself with zero weight.
The empty eligible family is permitted.

## 2.8.5 — Covering by enlargements

comparison, without unfolding the selected maximal family. The module
also proves membership, inclusion of S in its own enlargement for
nonnegative delta(S) and tau>=1, and monotonicity in the eligible family.

## 2.8.6 — Removing finitely many selected sets

[`FiniteRemainderEnlargements.v`](../../theories/FiniteRemainderEnlargements.v) proves that, if F covers A finely and
H is a finite subfamily of the selected G, then the enlargements of

fineness supplies a covering member inside that ball. Its intersecting
selected member cannot belong to H. This follows the printed proof and
uses the selected family only through its abstract laws. The finite-G
regression takes H=G and proves that G itself covers the entire fine target.

## 2.8.7 — Adequacy from enlargement measure control

Federer, p. 144. [`federer_2_8_7`](../../theories/EnlargementAdequacy.v#L90) in [`EnlargementAdequacy.v`](../../theories/EnlargementAdequacy.v) proves the
complete theorem with the standing context of 2.8.1: F covers A finely,
delta is a bounded nonnegative function on F, tau>1, lambda>1, and
mu(hat S)<lambda mu(S) for every eligible S. Then F is adequate for A.
The target need not be measurable or have finite measure. No Borel
regularity, separability, positive weight or separate nonempty-member
premise is imposed.

[`FamilyWeightedEnlargements.v`](../../theories/FamilyWeightedEnlargements.v) gives the enlargement with delta defined

extension is exported. The public book theorem uses the exact family
domain; the auxiliary total representative is used only through its laws.

[`FineCoverOpenRestriction.v`](../../theories/FineCoverOpenRestriction.v) proves that the eligible sets contained in
an open W cover W intersect A finely. [`EnlargementMeasureBounds.v`](../../theories/EnlargementMeasureBounds.v)

closed and measurable. The finite-measure disjoint-family theorem from
2.1 gives countability of the selection. [`NonnegativeFamilyTails.v`](../../theories/NonnegativeFamilyTails.v)
proves that a finite nonnegative sum has arbitrarily small tails after

[`FiniteEnlargementAdequacy.v`](../../theories/FiniteEnlargementAdequacy.v)
applies the sealed weighted selection inside a finite-measure open W.

remaining selected sets. Small tails then force the uncovered outer measure to
zero. The global proof uses a countable finite-measure open cover and 2.8.3.

target are still permitted, with the estimate vacuous on F.

## 2.8.11 — Cardinal bound for a controlled family
