### 2.3.1 — Almost-everywhere conventions

The integrated build, full recursive kernel check and incremental assumption
audit passed.

[`MeasureAlmostUP.v`](../../theories/MeasureAlmostUP.v) formalises every convention in Federer p. 72: almost all
points, almost all points in a subset, almost containment, and almost equality
of functions. [`MeasureAlmostBasic.v`](../../theories/MeasureAlmostBasic.v) proves the supporting implication,
finite-conjunction and countable-conjunction laws from the existing measure
covering inequality. No external almost-everywhere filter is imported.

[`PartialFunctionUP.v`](../../theories/PartialFunctionUP.v) specifies a partial function through a single-valued
graph. Its domain and inverse images are defined by existence of graph values;
no value outside the domain and no inhabited target are assumed. This is
set-theoretic background for the printed partial-domain convention, not a
new measure axiom. [`PartialFunctionBasic.v`](../../theories/PartialFunctionBasic.v) derives the equality-locus laws
from that interface and supplies the ordinary total and empty graph examples.
[`MeasureAlmostEquality.v`](../../theories/MeasureAlmostEquality.v) proves symmetry, transitivity and the precise
reflexivity condition: the domain must contain almost all of the measured
space. Undefined values do not count as equal values.

The local erratum has no entry affecting this passage on p. 72. Four
regressions cover an empty relative set, an arbitrary total map, an empty
target over a zero measure, and failure of empty-domain reflexivity when the
ambient space is not null. Conjunction and equality laws do not require finite
measure or measurable truth sets.

### 2.3.2 — Measurable functions

The complete item passed an integrated build, full recursive kernel check
and incremental assumption audit of every new declaration.

The formalisation retains the partial-domain definition on Federer p. 73 and
proves Borel inverse images and the generating-family criterion (1)–(2),
Suslin inverse images (3), Borel postcomposition (4), the countable-product
criterion (5), and the almost-everywhere limit theorem for arbitrary metric
targets (6). The latter follows the printed union–union–intersection formula,
with null modifications. No separability or positive-total-measure premise is
added to the metric limit theorem.

The countable-product proof uses a sealed countable refinement by finite
coordinate cylinders. Its conull-domain premise remains explicit even for an
empty product. All four rational-ray tests are proved, as is replacement by
any dense subset of the extended real line. A sealed countable family of
thresholds inside the given dense subset supplies the countable argument; the
dense subset itself need not be countable. Both infinite endpoints are
included.

Total extensions are specified by graph preservation, proved to exist and
sealed. Federer's unqualified extension remark requires a nonempty target
when the source may be a nonempty null space: the empty function from a
one-point null source to the empty target is measurable but admits no total
extension to that target. This is a local boundary qualification, not an entry
in Federer's erratum. The general measurability theorems allow empty targets.

The numerical assertions on p. 74 include negation, positive and negative
parts (including the equivalence), absolute value, ordinary real addition,
subtraction and multiplication, and partial extended-real arithmetic. The
latter retains exactly the undefined scalar pairs of 2.1.1 and the explicit
conull-domain proviso. they do not assign public values to undefined
arithmetic. The scalar continuity adapters reuse the same narrow topology
background as the existing real Baire algebra; measurability is proved within
the project.

Partial pointwise suprema and infima have their exact common domain and
abstract least/greatest-bound laws, with sealed existence proofs. The empty
family gives the entire source as domain and values minus/plus infinity. Lower
and upper limits are specified by eventual lower/upper bounds and have the
full eventual common domain made explicit. A regression sequence is initially
undefined and subsequently constant, so its common domain is empty but both
limit domains are the whole source.

The separated-level criterion (7) follows Federer's finite even/odd band
induction, the finite band-series bound, small tails and the final finite-test
splitting estimate. Criterion (8) uses the sealed distance to a closed set
to reduce to (7). The identity-map specialization proves Caratheodory's
criterion (9). These are stated both with a positive uniform cross-distance
bound and with the book's positive extended-real infimum; their equivalence
includes the empty-set distance of positive infinity. No separability or
finite-total-measure assumption is introduced.

The scan was checked; the formalisation excludes the four zero/infinity pairs,
consistently with Federer 2.1.1, p. 51. This is a correction to that display.

Sources read: Federer 2.3.2, printed pp. 73–75 (including scanned-page checks
of the extended-real bars) The local Federer erratum has no entry correcting
this item's pp. 73–75.

### 2.3.3 — Prescribed-weight characteristic expansion

The complete item passed an integrated build, full recursive kernel check
and incremental assumption audit of every new declaration.

The formalisation gives Federer's pointwise expansion of every nonnegative
extended-real measurable function with total domain. The prescribed real
weights are positive, tend to zero, and have divergent sum. Their convergence
uses the existing order-neighborhood specification on extended reals; their
finite values carry the ordinary real convergence convention. The sum is the
project's sealed nonnegative sum from 2.1.1.

The greedy system is specified by its characteristic-function laws and the
printed threshold formula, with indices starting at zero. Scalar partial sums
and the resulting set/function system have separate existence proofs and
sealed interfaces. Measurability and convergence are derived from these
abstract laws. if the selected sum is finite, divergence of the prescribed
weights forces arbitrarily late omissions; vanishing weights then give the
reverse bound by arbitrary positive errors. The conclusion holds at every
source point, including infinite target values.

The measurable-set induction uses local numerical-comparison, piecewise-map,
real-addition and real-to-extended-real inclusion lemmas. It does not import
an external simple-function approximation theorem. Regression proofs check
selection at equality with the first weight, selection of every weight for an
infinite target, and omission of every positive weight for a zero target.

Sources: Federer 2.3.3, printed p. 76 (scan checked) Federer's erratum, p. 1,
book p. 76 line 3, corrects the typography of “and”; no mathematical
hypothesis is changed.

### 2.3.4 — Countably measurable sets and functions

The complete item passed an integrated build, full recursive kernel check
and incremental assumption audit of every new declaration.

A countably measurable set is the union of a countable family of measurable
sets of finite measure. The empty family is allowed. The equivalent sequence
formulation is proved, including the empty-family case. Such sets are
measurable, contain every measurable subset as another countably measurable
set, and include all null sets. Finiteness of the whole set is not required.

A countably measurable function is measurable and has a countably measurable
nonzero locus. The interface exposes the distinguished zero of the target;
this explicitly generalises the printed vector-space definition using only
the datum that definition needs. Undefined points do not enter the nonzero
locus. Supporting results cover the zero function and functions on a
countably measurable source when the distinguished zero is closed.

Regressions use counting measure on the natural numbers: its whole space is
countably measurable with infinite measure, and its zero function is countably
measurable. Sources: Federer 2.3.4, printed p. 76 No erratum correction
applies.

### 2.3.5 — Lusin's theorem

The complete item passed an integrated build, full recursive kernel check
and incremental assumption audit of every new declaration.

For a measurable partial function into a separable metric space, every
finite-measure measurable set A has a closed subset C with arbitrarily small
residual measure, contained in the function's domain, on which the function
is continuous. This holds for Borel regular measures on metric spaces. For
a Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space, C can be compact.
The whole source may have infinite measure; empty source sets, targets
and partial domains are retained.

The proof follows the small Borel partitions, closed inner approximants,
finite-prefix selection and common intersection of Federer's argument. The
partitions have an abstract specification and a sealed existence construction.
Finite closed pieces are likewise specified by their laws. Their relative
openness and uniformly vanishing image diameters give the epsilon-neighborhood
proof of continuity on the common intersection. This expresses the printed
finite-step/uniform-limit argument without assigning representatives to empty
pieces. The Radon case uses the same closed argument and then intersects
with a compact inner approximant, splitting the error in half.

The partial-function continuity specification uses relative open inverse
images and includes domain containment. For total representatives it is
proved equivalent to ordinary subspace continuity. Regressions cover the
empty-domain/null-source case and ordinary continuous restriction with
infinite total source measure. All measure arguments are developed locally;
only general topology and metric background are imported.

Sources: Federer 2.3.5, printed pp. 76–77 arbitrarily smaller tolerances
recover the book's strict bound. No mathematical correction to this theorem
occurs in the local erratum.

### 2.3.6 — Borel representatives, real extensions and Ulam-dense ranges

The complete item passed an integrated build, full recursive kernel check
and incremental assumption audit of every new declaration.

On a countably measurable source, the measurable partial function has an
almost-equal total Borel representative. The proof applies Lusin to a
countable finite-measure cover with errors tending to zero, then pastes the
closed continuity pieces and assigns one value on their null complement.
Separate metric/Borel-regular and Radon statements retain the assumptions of
2.3.5. A target point is supplied for the total representative. The empty
source is handled separately without this assumption; the one-point null
source and empty target give a counterexample to an unqualified total-map
assertion. This is a local boundary qualification, not Federer's erratum.

For real-valued functions on a normal source, Tietze extends the Lusin
restriction to a continuous map of the whole source. The discrepancy in A
has measure below the prescribed error, counting undefined original values
as disagreement. The extension is specified abstractly and sealed. The
bounded Tietze theorem and Urysohn separation are imported as general-topology
background from MathComp's `numfun`; the reduction to unbounded functions is
proved locally using two bounded extensions and a positive denominator.
No external measure theorem is used. A regression checks the unbounded
identity on the whole real line.

The last assertion replaces separability of the target by a dense subset
of Ulam cardinality, in both the closed and compact conclusions. The support
of the finite restricted image measure is closed, separable and carries all
that image measure, by the project's 2.2.16. A sealed Borel retraction gives
values in the support. The null exceptional part of A is removed before
applying Lusin, so the original function is continuous everywhere on C.
Empty targets and empty supports are treated through the null-set case.

The last sentence of the book is made explicitly relative to A: the support
of f#(mu restricted to A) controls values almost everywhere on A. It does not
in general control the function on the whole source. A regression takes A
empty and a one-point counting source: the restricted image support is empty
while the whole image measure has unit mass outside it. This qualification
is distinct from Federer's published erratum.

Sources: Federer 2.3.6, printed p. 77, with 2.3.5, pp. 76–77, and 2.2.16, pp.
71–72. No erratum entry changes 2.3.6.

### 2.3.7 — Egoroff's theorem

The full theorem and measurable-source strengthening passed an integrated
build, full recursive kernel check and incremental assumption audit of every
new declaration.

A sequence and limit of measurable partial functions into a separable metric
space, converging almost everywhere on an arbitrary finite-measure set A,
converge uniformly on a measurable set B with arbitrarily small measure of
A minus B. The main statement does not assume A measurable or require B to
lie in A. If A is measurable, a separate corollary gives B contained in A.
All sequence values and the limit are defined on B; empty targets remain
allowed.

The proof follows Federer's decreasing tail deviation sets with thresholds
2^-(i+1). Pair-distance continuity and the countable-base product theorem
give measurable deviations. Decreasing continuity is applied to the measure
restricted to A, so no measurability of A is needed. Summable error budgets
select one cutoff for each threshold. Their exceptional union is removed
from the common conull domain, and the resulting bound is uniform in x.
No external Egoroff theorem or measure-convergence theorem is imported.
Regressions cover a constant partial sequence on its exact domain, failure
of vacuous uniform convergence with undefined limit values, and the
null-source/empty-function case without a target point.

Source: Federer 2.3.7, printed pp. 77–78. The error bound sums the measures
of A intersected with each chosen exceptional tail. The erratum p. 1, book
p. 78 line 7, corrects the opening of 2.3.8, not Egoroff's theorem.

### 2.3.8 — Measurable normed functions and convergence in measure

The complete item passed an integrated build, full recursive kernel check
and fresh full assumption audit.

The definition of A(mu,Y) retains measurable partial functions essentially
valued in a separable linear subspace. Federer’s erratum, p. 1, book p. 78
line 7, changes “consider the vectorspace” to “consider the set”. Accordingly,
the literal vector-space and convex-hull statements are on the explicitly
identified total-domain subcollection. A separate finite-average theorem
retains each original partial domain exactly. Completeness is an independent
hypothesis for the subsequent convergence theorems; the assertions here that
do not need it are proved for arbitrary normed spaces.

Coverage includes:

- The essential-separability definition; a closed separable linear envelope
  for countably many separable sets; measurability of addition, subtraction,
  fixed scalar multiples and norm superlevels; and invariance under almost
  equality. Closed linear spans are specified abstractly and sealed, with
  the countable rational-hull construction private.
- The bracketed automatic-separability assertion on p. 78, using countable
  measurability of the source and a dense subset of Ulam cardinality in the
  target, through the already proved 2.2.16 and 2.3.6.
- The extended nonnegative infimum gauge, attainment at its own threshold,
  the triangle and scalar bounds, and the characteristic-function formula
  min(c,mu(A)). The numerical characteristic formula does not require A
  measurable; measurability supplies membership in A(mu,R).
- The extended pseudodistance, symmetry, triangle inequality, translation
  invariance and its zero-if-and-only-if-almost-equal criterion. Domain
  intersections and null modifications are explicit. Infinite distance is
  witnessed by the natural-value function for counting measure on nat.
- Convergence and Cauchy convergence in measure; almost-everywhere
  convergence implies convergence in measure when the whole source has
  finite measure. The proof reuses the decreasing deviation-tail estimate
  from Egoroff’s theorem, one threshold at a time.
- An unconditional converse counterexample on a probability space with a
  complete real normed target. A bijective enumeration of binary-prefix
  cylinders gives characteristic functions whose measures tend to zero,
  although every source point lies in arbitrarily late cylinders. The
  cylinder measure is constructed locally from a sealed countable-cover
  infimum. Compactness of the binary product and finite word counting prove
  total mass one; finite refinements prove cylinder measurability. No
  external measure or Lebesgue-measure module supplies this example.
- Concrete counting-measure counterexamples to gauge homogeneity and ball
  convexity. On a point of mass one, doubling the constant one leaves gauge
  one; on two atoms of mass one, functions (4,0) and (0,4) have gauge one
  but midpoint (2,2) has gauge two.
- The finite-partition convex-hull assertion, including radius zero, and the
  exact equal-weight representation on the domain of a partial function.
  Every summand belongs to A(mu,Y) with the required gauge bound.
- Continuity at zero for multiplication by a fixed scalar, and continuity
  as the scalar tends to zero when the function’s nonzero locus has finite
  measure. The latter uses the high-norm-tail argument printed on p. 79.

Regressions check the infinite-gauge/zero-scalar boundary, distinct partial
and total zero functions on a null source, and both branches of the
characteristic-gauge minimum on a point of mass one.

Sources: Federer 2.3.8, printed pp. 78–79 The counterexample’s auxiliary
covering-infimum law is the fixed-family part of Federer 2.10.1, pp. 169–170;
this does not claim completion of 2.10.1.

### 2.3.9 — Summable successive distances

The development follows the proof on printed pp. 79–80. For a sequence in
A(mu,Y) with a finite sum of successive measure distances, it constructs a
partial limit, proves almost-everywhere convergence, proves that the limit
belongs to A(mu,Y), and proves convergence in measure. No finite total-measure
hypothesis or finite gauge of the individual functions is required.

- [`NonnegativeSequenceTail.v`](../../theories/NonnegativeSequenceTail.v), [`NonnegativeSequenceTailStep.v`](../../theories/NonnegativeSequenceTailStep.v) prove the
  numerical tail decomposition, finiteness and convergence to zero from the
  project's abstract nonnegative sum.
- [`SummableMetricIncrementBound.v`](../../theories/SummableMetricIncrementBound.v), [`SummableMetricIncrementLimit.v`](../../theories/SummableMetricIncrementLimit.v) prove
  the Cauchy estimate and quantitative limit bound from metric completeness.
- [`SummableExceptionalTails.v`](../../theories/SummableExceptionalTails.v) proves the measure bound for the exceptional
  tails and the nullity of their intersection directly from covering.
- [`PartialFunctionPointwiseLimitUP.v`](../../theories/PartialFunctionPointwiseLimitUP.v) specifies the exact convergence graph;
  its existence construction is private behind [`PartialFunctionPointwiseLimitSealed.v`](../../theories/PartialFunctionPointwiseLimitSealed.v).
- [`MeasurableNormedLimit.v`](../../theories/MeasurableNormedLimit.v) proves that an almost-everywhere limit remains
  in A(mu,Y), using a common closed separable linear span.
- [`MeasureSummableIncrementExceptions.v`](../../theories/MeasureSummableIncrementExceptions.v), [`MeasureIncrementGoodPoints.v`](../../theories/MeasureIncrementGoodPoints.v)
  make the common undefined locus explicit and null, preserving all original
  partial domains.
- [`MeasureSummableLimit.v`](../../theories/MeasureSummableLimit.v) proves the almost-everywhere limit and the bound
  |h-g_j|_mu <= r_j. [`MeasureSummableConvergence.v`](../../theories/MeasureSummableConvergence.v) packages the complete
  existence and convergence theorem. [`MeasureSummableAlmostLimit.v`](../../theories/MeasureSummableAlmostLimit.v) proves
  the result for any almost-everywhere limit representative.

Regressions cover a stationary sequence whose members have infinite gauge,
and the exact empty domain of the limit of empty partial functions.

Sources: Federer 2.3.9, pp. 79–80 No erratum entry changes 2.3.9; the p. 78
line 7 correction to the underlying collection remains in force.
Natural-number indexing starts at zero instead of one without changing the
statement.

### 2.3.10 — Completeness of the measurable-function space

The full completeness theorem gives, for every Cauchy sequence in A(mu,Y),
a strictly increasing subsequence and a limit in A(mu,Y). The subsequence
converges both almost everywhere and in measure, and the whole sequence
converges in measure to the same limit. The source measure need not be finite;
the target is an arbitrary complete real normed space. The extended
pseudodistance and original partial domains are preserved.

[`CauchyIndexSelectionUP.v`](../../theories/CauchyIndexSelectionUP.v)
specifies the increasing cutoffs and their tail bounds. A private recursion
proves existence, and
[`CauchyIndexSelectionSealed.v`](../../theories/CauchyIndexSelectionSealed.v)
exposes only the witness and laws.
[`MeasureCauchySubsequence.v`](../../theories/MeasureCauchySubsequence.v)
obtains summable successive distances using the existing local geometric-sum
estimate.
[`MeasureFunctionCompleteness.v`](../../theories/MeasureFunctionCompleteness.v)
then applies 2.3.9 and the triangle argument in
[`MeasureCauchySubsequenceLimit.v`](../../theories/MeasureCauchySubsequenceLimit.v).
[`MeasureConvergentCauchy.v`](../../theories/MeasureConvergentCauchy.v)
records the supporting Cauchy property of convergent sequences.

Regressions cover cofinal strict index growth when all distances vanish and
arbitrary sequences of partial functions for the zero measure.

Sources: Federer 2.3.10, printed p. 80 Our tolerance is 2^-(n+2), using the
earlier geometric budget, rather than the printed 2^-n; it is positive and
summable and yields the same conclusion. No erratum entry changes 2.3.10.

The complete subsection passed a clean build, full recursive kernel check
and fresh full assumption audit under the required switch.
