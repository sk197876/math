### 2.6.1 — Cartesian product of measures

[`is_product_measure`](../../theories/ProductMeasureUP.v#L19) gives an abstract characterisation: the Cartesian product
is the largest measure bounded on each measurable rectangle A × B by
alpha(A) beta(B). [`product_measure_unique`](../../theories/ProductMeasureUP.v#L37) proves uniqueness from those laws.
The separate [`is_product_cover_infimum`](../../theories/ProductMeasureCoverUP.v#L16) records the defining countable-cover
infimum, without choosing an approximation sequence.

[`ProductMeasureConstruction.v`](../../theories/ProductMeasureConstruction.v) proves existence using the project's fixed-family
countable-cover envelope. Internally, inadmissible rectangle pairs have infinite
cost, which does not change the infimum over measurable covers. The witness is
hidden by [`ProductMeasureSealed.v`](../../theories/ProductMeasureSealed.v). Public [`product_measure`](../../theories/ProductMeasure.v#L14) exposes only proved
laws: it is a measure, satisfies the rectangle bound, is the greatest measure
with that bound, and satisfies the covering infimum specification.

[`product_measure_sequence_cover_bound`](../../theories/ProductMeasureSequenceInfimum.v#L18) and
[`product_measure_sequence_cover_greatest`](../../theories/ProductMeasureSequenceInfimum.v#L37) give the literal sequential infimum
of pp. 114–115. [`countable_zero_cost_enumeration`](../../theories/CountableCostEnumeration.v#L19) assigns each member of a
countable cover its injective natural code and fills gaps with empty rectangles;
the numerical grouping theorem proves that this preserves the cost. This
includes an empty covering family and allows repeated rectangles in arbitrary
sequence covers. Indices start at zero instead of the printed positive integers.

The measure laws are the only hypotheses on the two factors; finite total
measure and countable measurability are not assumed. Three regression examples
check zero times infinity, infinity times zero, and a null first-factor whole
space against an unrestricted second factor. The exact rectangle equality,
regularity and Fubini conclusions belong to 2.6.2 and are not asserted by this
item's rectangle bound.

Sources: Federer 2.6.1, printed pp. 114–115 The local erratum has no
correction to this item. The covering-envelope infrastructure previously
developed for 2.3.8 is reused; no external product- measure implementation is
imported.

### 2.6.2 — Fubini's theorem (complete)

The four numbered assertions are formalised under Federer's hypotheses.
The earlier checkpoint supplied regularity (1) and rectangle measurability;
[`product_measure_measurable_rectangle`](../../theories/ProductRectangleValue.v#L36) supplies the exact equality in (2),
[`product_set_fubini`](../../theories/ProductSetFubini.v#L16) proves both section formulas in (3), and
[`product_function_fubini`](../../theories/ProductFunctionFubini.v#L21) proves both actual iterated-integral identities in (4).
Neither factor is required to be finite or sigma-finite. Countable measurability
of the set, or of the function's nonzero locus, is retained where Federer
requires it. Integral values may be infinite.

For (2), a characteristic-function comparison proves that every measurable
rectangle cover has cost at least alpha(A) beta(B). The cover infimum then
gives equality, including zero times infinity and infinity times zero. This
establishes the rectangle value before developing the general repeated-integral
class; it is a documented variation in proof order, not an additional premise.

[`is_disjoint_rectangle_cover`](../../theories/DisjointRectangleCoverUP.v#L16) specifies the countable disjoint refinement
used on p. 116. Subtracting a rectangle splits each old rectangle into its
left and top pieces; finite iteration followed by countable disjointification
proves the refinement. Repeated and empty rectangles are permitted. The P1
class is specified by [`is_countable_rectangle_union`](../../theories/CountableRectangleUnionUP.v#L15), with an equivalence to
countable rectangle families, closure under finite intersections, measurable
sections at every point, and the actual repeated-integral equality.

The P2 step uses decreasing P1 sets with finite first measure. Continuity
from above holds for the inner sections off a null exceptional set, and the
project's dominated-convergence theorem gives the outer integral. The finite
hull and [`product_repeated_integral_hull`](../../theories/ProductRepeatedIntegralHull.v#L20) prove the displayed enclosing-set
assertion on p. 116, including the infinite-measure case W = X times Y.
The hull has the same product measure and repeated integral as the enclosed
set. No measurability of the original set is needed for this hull assertion.

Null sections follow directly from rectangle-cover integral majorants with
vanishing reciprocal bounds. This is a second documented proof-order
variation: it proves the null case before invoking the repeated-integral
hull. A null modification then proves the finite-measurable-set formula;
countable disjoint decomposition proves (3). Product transposition is proved
for every subset and preserves both measurability hypotheses, giving the
other integration order.

For (4), [`is_parameterized_integral`](../../theories/ParameterizedIntegralUP.v#L16) specifies the exact graph of an inner
integral. Its existence follows from integral uniqueness and is hidden by
[`ParameterizedIntegralSealed.v`](../../theories/ParameterizedIntegralSealed.v); downstream proofs use only the graph laws.
The inner function is defined precisely where its section has an actual
integral. No value is assigned to a nonintegrable section or to infinity
minus infinity. A total nonnegative representative, Federer's weighted
characteristic expansion (2.3.3), and countable integral addition (2.4.8)
prove the nonnegative case. Null-section transfer restores the original
partial domains. Positive and negative parts, with a defined difference of
their integrals, give the signed case by 2.4.4(6). The second order follows
by transposition and the project's image-measure integral theorem (2.4.18).

[`measure_summable_countably_measurable`](../../theories/SummableCountableMeasurability.v#L66) proves the parenthetical support
assertion for extended-real partial functions: reciprocal superlevels of
the absolute value have finite measure, and their union is its nonzero
locus. Thus [`product_summable_fubini`](../../theories/ProductSummableFubini.v#L15) needs only an actual finite product
integral. Infinite function values on a null set remain permitted.

Regression examples cover counting singletons and infinite rectangles,
null products with a counting factor, repeated rectangle unions, a countably
measurable set of infinite product measure, total zero integrands on arbitrary
factors, zero integrands with proper conull domains, and a negative constant
on factors of total mass one. The earlier null-strip regression remains.

Sources: Federer 2.6.2, printed pp. 115–117 the formal proof uses Federer's
indicated 2.3.3, 2.4.8 and 2.4.4(6). The local erratum has no correction at
book pp. 115–117. No external product-measure or Fubini theorem is used.

### 2.6.3 — Three limitations of Fubini's theorem (complete)

All three examples on printed pp. 117–118 are proved with the project's
actual measures, integral predicates and exact partial-function domains.

* [`LebesgueCountingDiagonal.v`](../../theories/LebesgueCountingDiagonal.v) proves that the product of Lebesgue measure
  and counting measure is Borel regular. The characteristic function of
  the diagonal over [0,1] is measurable and has product integral infinity,
  whereas its repeated integrals are zero and one. Neither the diagonal
  nor its characteristic function is countably product measurable.
  [`CountingProductDiagonal.v`](../../theories/CountingProductDiagonal.v) proves the infinite measure directly from
  arbitrary rectangular covers; no converse to Fubini is assumed.
* [`RationalFubiniCounterexample.v`](../../theories/RationalFubiniCounterexample.v) proves that the printed rational kernel
  is not Lebesgue product integrable, while [`RationalIteratedIntegrals.v`](../../theories/RationalIteratedIntegrals.v)
  proves both repeated integrals equal zero. [`RationalRectangleIntegral.v`](../../theories/RationalRectangleIntegral.v)
  retains the hypothetical integrability premise and proves the printed
  value m/(1+m)^2 on every open rectangle (0,a) times (0,ma), for a,m>0.
  The final contradiction uses m=1: bounded convergence would give 1/4,
  whereas Fubini and the already proved repeated integrals force zero.
  The scalar finite-integral formulas, the inverse-square tail integral,
  the two majorant bounds and the vanishing section integrals are proved
  in the supporting `Rational*` and `InverseSquare*` modules. An abstract
  uniform primitive criterion implies the Riemann integral by tagged sums;
  its rational witnesses then give actual Lebesgue integrals through the
  sealed scalar measure. No external calculus theorem replaces the
  computations, and endpoint singletons are explicitly removed as null.
* [`RepeatedIntegralFamilyCounterexample.v`](../../theories/RepeatedIntegralFamilyCounterexample.v) proves that the family F of
  sets with the specified repeated characteristic integral need not be
  a Borel family. It retains Federer's factor orientation, arbitrary
  disjoint measurable A1,A2 of equal positive measure (including infinity),
  and an arbitrary nonmeasurable B. The stated S,T belong to F, but their
  intersection does not. [`RepeatedIntegralSetUP.v`](../../theories/RepeatedIntegralSetUP.v) specifies F using the
  actual parameterized integral.

Sources: Federer 2.6.3, pp. 117–118 this development keeps Federer's
orientation. The local Federer erratum contains no correction to 2.6.3. Three
regression examples cover the zero coordinate axis, the first primitive at
a=y=1, and the positive scale-independent square value.

### 2.6.4 — Tensor products and product Daniell functionals (complete)

The total-domain spaces required by Federer's erratum are specified in
[`SummableRealSpaceUP.v`](../../theories/SummableRealSpaceUP.v); their existence construction is hidden behind
[`SummableRealSpaceSealed.v`](../../theories/SummableRealSpaceSealed.v). They retain literal functions, including
values on null sets. [`SummableTensorMap.v`](../../theories/SummableTensorMap.v) proves the separated-product
map bilinear and its induced linear tensor map injective, with arbitrary
factor measures and arbitrary dimensions. [`FunctionTensorInjectivity.v`](../../theories/FunctionTensorInjectivity.v)
proves the needed algebraic function-tensor injectivity directly, without
changing the unfinished material indexed under 1.1.3.

[`SeparatedProductIntegral.v`](../../theories/SeparatedProductIntegral.v) proves summability and the actual integral
formula for products of summable functions. [`SeparatedProductLoneNorm.v`](../../theories/SeparatedProductLoneNorm.v)
proves the corresponding product identity for the L1 seminorm, including
zero seminorms. [`SummableTensorIntegral.v`](../../theories/SummableTensorIntegral.v) proves the full first tensor
integral diagram using the actual integral maps and tensor uniqueness.
[`SummableTensorSeminormDensity.v`](../../theories/SummableTensorSeminormDensity.v) proves density in the existing abstract
L1 seminorm of the actual total-domain product carrier.

For density, [`ProductRectangleApproximation.v`](../../theories/ProductRectangleApproximation.v) obtains finite unions of
finite-factor rectangles approximating any finite measurable product set
in symmetric-difference measure. Infinite-factor rectangles in a finite
cover cost have zero cost and are removed with their null union retained
explicitly. [`FiniteRectangleUnionImage.v`](../../theories/FiniteRectangleUnionImage.v) puts their characteristic
functions in the tensor image by inclusion-exclusion. The
`SubspaceLoneApproximation*`, [`SubspaceSimpleDensity.v`](../../theories/SubspaceSimpleDensity.v),
[`SubspacePositiveDensity.v`](../../theories/SubspacePositiveDensity.v), and [`SubspaceLoneDensity.v`](../../theories/SubspaceLoneDensity.v) modules then pass
to all summable functions. The tensor image is used only as a linear
subspace; no unjustified closure under pointwise minima is assumed. No
ambient sigma-finiteness is required.

[`DaniellProductUP.v`](../../theories/DaniellProductUP.v) specifies the product of arbitrary signed Daniell
functionals on K(X), K(Y) for locally compact Hausdorff spaces.
[`IteratedCompactDaniell.v`](../../theories/IteratedCompactDaniell.v) proves existence by successive application to
compactly supported continuous sections, including the order bounds and
increasing-sequence continuity. [`DaniellProductUniqueness.v`](../../theories/DaniellProductUniqueness.v) proves
uniqueness on K(X times Y), using finite separated approximations with a
common compact support. The finite-partition approximation and the product
functional's existence witnesses are separately sealed. [`DaniellProduct.v`](../../theories/DaniellProduct.v)
exposes the resulting functional only through its specification and
uniqueness. [`CompactSeparatedSumLaws.v`](../../theories/CompactSeparatedSumLaws.v) extends agreement on generators to
all finite tensor sums; [`DaniellProductMonotone.v`](../../theories/DaniellProductMonotone.v) treats monotone factors.

[`DaniellProductRepresentation.v`](../../theories/DaniellProductRepresentation.v) proves the corrected final formula:
nu(h) is the actual integral of h against alpha times beta for every
h in K(X times Y). Uniform separated approximation gives measurability;
products of compact cutoffs give summable majorants. The proof works for
any measures representing the two factor functionals, so K-regularity is
not needed for this representation formula. It does not assert that the
product measure is K(X times Y)-regular.

Sources: Federer 2.6.4, pp. 118–119; the May 1987 erratum, p. 2, book p. 118
lines 20, 21, 24 and p. 119 lines 3, 4, 6 (total domains L~_1 in place of
L_1), and p. 119 line 19 (the corrected representation formula). The proof's
rectangle-pruning and fixed-support approximation steps are documented as
supporting arguments, not separate claims attributed to either source.

### 2.6.5 — Finite products and Euclidean Lebesgue measure (complete with correction)

Federer pp. 119–120. The unrestricted associativity assertion printed on
p. 119 is false. [`LebesgueAssociativityCounterexample.v`](../../theories/LebesgueAssociativityCounterexample.v) proves a concrete
counterexample using the actual Lebesgue measure of 2.5.17 and a nonempty
factor whose measure is infinite on every nonempty set. The diagonal over
[0,1], times that factor, has measure zero in the left-associated product
and infinity after reassociation. Its nullity in the two Lebesgue factors
is proved using measurability, singleton nullity and Fubini; it is not an
extra hypothesis in the final counterexample.

[`ProductMeasureAssociativity.v`](../../theories/ProductMeasureAssociativity.v)
proves the corrected canonical image-measure identity when the first and third
factors are countably measurable
([`product_measure_associative_outer_countable`](../../theories/ProductMeasureAssociativity.v#L116)).
No countability assumption on the middle factor is needed in the proved
argument. The corollary
[`product_measure_associative_countable`](../../theories/ProductMeasureAssociativity.v#L133)
retains countable measurability of all three factors. Commutativity needs no such
restriction:
[`measure_bijection_swap`](../../theories/MeasureBijectionCoordinateTransport.v#L26)
states the actual flip image identity.

[`FiniteCartesianMeasureUP.v`](../../theories/FiniteCartesianMeasureUP.v), its existence construction, opaque sealing
module and public interface define nonempty finite products by their
recursive product laws. [`FiniteCartesianRectangles.v`](../../theories/FiniteCartesianRectangles.v) proves measurable
box formulas and preservation of countable measurability. The independent
finite-tree development handles every parenthesisation of an ordered list
of countably measurable factors. [`finite_product_association_invariance`](../../theories/FiniteMeasureAssociations.v#L92)
provides inverse coordinate maps preserving both the measure and every
ordered factor value; [`finite_product_coordinate_map_image`](../../theories/MeasureTreeCoordinateInjectivity.v#L62) identifies an
explicit coordinate-preserving map with that canonical association.
This includes one-factor products and empty factor carriers. Finite
association is not asserted for unrestricted measures.

[`LebesgueLineUP.v`](../../theories/LebesgueLineUP.v) specifies scalar Lebesgue measure by its previously proved
2.5.17 characterisation. [`EuclideanLebesgueUP.v`](../../theories/EuclideanLebesgueUP.v) specifies the positive-
dimensional family by the scalar base and coordinate-product laws.
Both have existence proofs, opaque seals and public consequences. The
Euclidean carrier remains the project's finite-coordinate space with its
usual inner product, not a replacement nested-pair carrier.
[`EuclideanBlockMeasure.v`](../../theories/EuclideanBlockMeasure.v) proves that the usual first-m, last-n coordinate
split sends L^(m+n) to L^m times L^n ([`euclidean_block_measure`](../../theories/EuclideanBlockMeasure.v#L99)). Its proof
connects the actual coordinates to the finite-association theorem.
Dimension zero is explicitly extended by unit mass at the sole vector;
the block identity includes empty blocks.

[`ProductBorelRegularity.v`](../../theories/ProductBorelRegularity.v) proves
Borel regularity of products on spaces with countable open bases. This binary
result does not require countable measurability. It yields
[`euclidean_lebesgue_borel_regular`](../../theories/EuclideanLebesgueDimensions.v#L38)
in every dimension. The Euclidean family also has proved countable
finite-measure covers.

[`euclidean_polynomial_zero_set_null`](../../theories/EuclideanLebesgueDimensions.v#L63) proves the final printed assertion
for every nonzero polynomial function on R^n, including the documented
zero-dimensional extension. The proof follows Federer's induction and
Fubini argument. It uses the project's Chapter 1 polynomial functions:
coefficient slices are polynomial, a nonzero coefficient has a null zero
set by induction, and each remaining one-variable slice has finitely many
roots. Continuity gives measurability of the zero set. The supporting
finite-root proof uses the existing synthetic division argument. No
external polynomial-nullity theorem or additional project axiom is used.

Sources: Federer 2.6.5, printed pp. 119–120 The local LaTeX erratum's p. 119
corrections concern 2.6.4, not associativity in 2.6.5. Regression examples
cover zero-dimensional polynomials, empty blocks, the infinite middle factor,
its failure of countable measurability, and the concrete zero/infinity values
of the counterexample.

### 2.6.6 — Infinite products and compact inverse limits (complete)

Federer, pp. 120–121. The hypotheses are arbitrary compact Hausdorff
factor spaces with normalized monotone Daniell functionals. The development
allows arbitrary index sets, including finite and empty ones; no countability,
metrizability or sigma-finiteness assumption is added.

- `FiniteCoordinateProductUP`, `FiniteCoordinateProduct` and
  `FiniteCoordinateProductUniqueness` specify, provide through a seal, and
  characterize the finite products by their empty-product, adjoining-factor
  and consistency laws. Iterated coordinate integrations prove existence;
  commutation and permutation lemmas justify independence of their order.
- `FiniteCoordinateProjection`, `FiniteCoordinatePullback` and
  `FiniteCoordinateMarginal` define the actual spaces X_a and projections p_a,
  prove the injective pullback of K(X_a), identify its image L_a, and show that
  the finite marginals are normalized monotone Daniell functionals.
- `ProductCylinderDensity` proves uniform density of the union L using a
  finite-coordinate oscillation estimate on the compact product. The glued
  functional is linear, normalized, and bounded by the actual uniform norm.
- `InfiniteDaniellProductUP` and `InfiniteDaniellProduct` expose only the
  normalized Daniell and finite-marginal laws. Existence follows from the
  proved gluing and density, a continuous linear extension, and 2.5.13.
  `InfiniteProductMarginals` gives the printed projection characterization.
  `InfiniteProductContinuousExtension` proves continuity and linearity and
  uniqueness among all continuous extensions agreeing on finite cylinders.
- `CompactInverseSystemUP` and `CompactInverseLimitUP` specify directed compact
  Hausdorff inverse systems and their limits by projection and realization
  laws. Bonding maps need not be onto. Finite upper bounds and compact closed
  intersections prove existence of compatible coordinate families and the
  eventual-image property for neighborhoods of a limit projection.
- `InverseLimitPullbackDensity` proves density of the directed pullback union.
  A two-point cutoff argument supplies interpolation; a direct finite-maxima
  and finite-minima compact-cover proof supplies the lattice approximation.
- `InverseDaniellPullbackBound` and `InverseDaniellPullbackEquality` prove the
  bound on the actual limit and independence of a pullback presentation.
  `InverseDaniellFamilyUP` and the sealed `InverseDaniellIntegral` then give
  existence and uniqueness with all coordinate marginals.
  `InverseDaniellContinuousExtension` additionally proves uniqueness among
  all continuous extensions of the directed pullback functional. The inverse-limit
  theorem retains the normalized monotone hypotheses of the preceding
  discussion; it does not claim an unrestricted signed inverse-limit theorem,
  which is false without a bound on the variations.
- *Uniqueness among all Daniell integrals.* "mu is uniquely characterized by
  the condition p_a#(mu) = mu_a whenever a in A" is proved without
  restricting the competitor. Every Daniell integral D on K(X), X compact, is
  continuous for the supremum norm ([`compact_daniell_uniform_continuous`](../../theories/CompactDaniellUniformContinuity.v#L51),
  from 2.5.19). [`infinite_daniell_product_daniell_unique`](../../theories/InfiniteProductDaniellCharacterization.v#L51),
  [`infinite_daniell_product_daniell_characterization`](../../theories/InfiniteProductDaniellCharacterization.v#L68) and
  [`finite_product_marginals_determine_daniell`](../../theories/InfiniteProductDaniellCharacterization.v#L82) prove that D equals mu on K(X)
  exactly when its finite marginals are the mu_a
  ([`has_finite_product_marginals`](../../theories/InfiniteProductDaniellCharacterization.v#L34)). `InverseDaniellCharacterization` proves
  the same for the inverse-limit integral.
- *mu_a is the product of 2.6.4.* For i not in a, the map
  psi(u) = (u|a, u(i)) is a homeomorphism of X_{a cup {i}} onto X_a x X_i
  ([`finite_coordinate_split_homeomorphism`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateSplit.v#L122)), and k |-> mu_{a cup {i}}(k o psi)
  satisfies [`is_daniell_product`](../../theories/DaniellProductUP.v#L16) for mu_a and mu_i
  ([`finite_coordinate_adjoin_daniell_product`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateAdjoinProduct.v#L143)), hence equals
  [`daniell_product`](../../theories/DaniellProduct.v#L14). The product formula
  mu_a(prod_j f_j o pi_j) = prod_j mu_j(f_j) is
  [`finite_coordinate_product_separated_value`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateAdjoinProduct.v#L266); for disjoint a and c the image
  of mu_{a cup c} on X_a x X_c is the 2.6.4 product of mu_a and mu_c
  ([`finite_coordinate_disjoint_daniell_product`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateRestriction.v#L289)). The displayed chain
  mu_b(f o p^b_a) = mu_a(f) mu_{b-a}(1) = mu_a(f) is
  [`finite_coordinate_marginal_restriction`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateRestriction.v#L369). `FiniteCoordinateProductIdentification`
  restates these for the sealed [`finite_coordinate_product`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateProduct.v#L15).
- *X as an inverse limit.* The finite subsets of J with the spaces X_a form a
  compact inverse system ([`finite_coordinate_inverse_system`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateInverseLimit.v#L52)), X with the
  projections p_a is its inverse limit ([`finite_coordinate_inverse_limit`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateInverseLimit.v#L81)),
  and [`infinite_daniell_product`](../../theories/InfiniteDaniellProduct.v#L16) equals the corresponding
  [`inverse_limit_daniell_integral`](../../theories/InverseDaniellIntegral.v#L12) ([`infinite_daniell_product_inverse_limit_value`](../../theories/InfiniteProductInverseLimit.v#L67)).
  The specification of an inverse system requires bonding maps for every pair
  of indices; for a not contained in b the map fills missing coordinates from a
  point of X, and for a contained in b it is p^b_a
  ([`finite_coordinate_bonding_restriction`](../../theories/FiniteCoordinateInverseLimit.v#L40)). This verifies the observation of
  p. 120 that the passage to infinite products depends only on the
  inverse-limit representation and the consistency conditions.
- *Existence of compact inverse limits.* [`compact_inverse_limit_exists`](../../theories/CompactInverseLimitExistence.v#L42)
  ([`CompactInverseLimitExistence.v`](../../theories/CompactInverseLimitExistence.v)): the compatible families form a closed,
  hence compact, subspace of the product (Tychonoff; [Kelley](../bibliography.md#kelley2017), *General
  Topology*, Chapter 5, p. 143), which satisfies the specification.

General-topology support for the inverse-limit density proof: [Kelley](../bibliography.md#kelley2017), *General
Topology*, Dover 2017 reprint of 1955, Chapter 7 Problem R(a)(ii)–(iii), pp.
244–245. The proof uses the order-dual max/min sequence to the printed one.
The local Federer erratum has no relevant entry for pp. 120–121.

### 2.6.7 — Integration by parts for cumulative measures (complete)

[`radon_cumulative_integration_by_parts`](../../theories/RadonCumulativeIntegrationByParts.v#L23) proves the printed identity for
Radon measures alpha and beta over R. For finite cumulative functions
f(r)=alpha{t:t<r} and g(r)=beta{t:t<=r} on [a,b], both integrals are finite
and

    integral_(a,b] f d beta + integral_[a,b) g d alpha
      = f(b) g(b) - f(a) g(a).

[`is_cumulative_function_pair`](../../theories/CumulativeFunctionPairUP.v#L18) specifies the half-line laws abstractly.
[`cumulative_function_pair_exists`](../../theories/CumulativeFunctionPairUP.v#L35) proves existence from finiteness at b;
monotonicity gives finiteness throughout [a,b]. The public corollary
[`radon_cumulative_integration_by_parts_exists`](../../theories/RadonCumulativeIntegrationByParts.v#L57) combines existence with the
actual integral identity. Values outside [a,b] are unrestricted. The
half-open integrands are zero extensions, implementing the subset-integral
convention of 2.4.10, p. 85.

The proof follows the book's triangle S={(x,y):a<=x<y<=b}. Rational-cut
rectangles prove its measurability, and a finite rectangle bounds its
measure. Fubini gives both repeated integrals of S. Finite nested-set
subtraction identifies their sections; integral addition and subtraction
then give the formula. The supporting theorem needs only measurable lower
half-lines; the public theorem derives these from the Radon hypotheses.
No finiteness outside [a,b], atomlessness, or global countable-measurability
assumption is added. The degenerate interval a=b is also included.

Regression examples check the empty interval and common unit atoms at the
left and right endpoints. They preserve the different strictness of both
cumulative functions and both integration intervals. [`point_mass_real_integral`](../../theories/PointMassIntegral.v#L23)
proves evaluation for every total real-valued function under a unit point mass.

Sources: Federer 2.6.7, printed p. 121, all three displayed equations The
