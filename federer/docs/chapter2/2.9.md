### 2.9.1 — Standing hypotheses, the continuous part and derivates

Federer, pp. 152–153.
[`MeasureDerivationContext.v`](../../theories/MeasureDerivationContext.v)
specifies the book's class M as
[`is_derivation_measure`](../../theories/MeasureDerivationContext.v#L15):
measures that are Borel regular and finite on every bounded set.
[`is_measure_derivation_context`](../../theories/MeasureDerivationContext.v#L32)
adds phi in M and a phi Vitali relation V. This retains the original
bounded-set hypothesis rather than replacing it.

[`is_absolutely_continuous_part`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPartUP.v#L18) in [`AbsoluteContinuousPartUP.v`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPartUP.v)
specifies the greatest lower bound of psi(B) over Borel sets B with
phi(A minus B)=0. [`AbsoluteContinuousPartConstruction.v`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPartConstruction.v) proves
existence, and [`AbsoluteContinuousPartSealed.v`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPartSealed.v) exposes only its
abstract laws. The public `measure_continuous_part psi phi` and
[`measure_continuous_part_formula`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPart.v#L33) in [`AbsoluteContinuousPart.v`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPart.v)
provide exactly the printed psi_phi operator and infimum formula.
[`measure_continuous_part_le`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPart.v#L49) proves the final observation
psi_phi(A) ≤ psi(A), using a psi Borel hull of A.

[`AbsoluteContinuousPartBasic.v`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPartBasic.v)
also proves nonnegativity, monotonicity and the zero value on phi-null sets.
[`AbsoluteContinuousPartAttainment.v`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPartAttainment.v)
proves attainment by intersecting countably many nearly optimal Borel almost
covers; their discarded phi-null exceptions have null union. The infinite case
uses the whole space.
[`AbsoluteContinuousPartMeasure.v`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPartMeasure.v)
proves that the operator is a measure by taking a countable union of attaining
Borel covers and applying the numerator's covering inequality.
[`measure_continuous_part_is_measure`](../../theories/AbsoluteContinuousPart.v#L59)
exposes this law for the public operator. This completes the measure assertion
made in 2.9.1; its proof is the elementary opening step of 2.9.2. The
Borel-regular decomposition and the remaining conclusions of 2.9.2 are
separate work.

[`MeasureDerivates.v`](../../theories/MeasureDerivates.v) defines `measure_ratio_function psi phi` using
the existing partial arithmetic of 2.1.1, p. 51. Zero denominators
and infinity divided by infinity are undefined; a positive finite
denominator has the ordinary extended quotient, even for an infinite
numerator. `measure_derivate psi phi V` is the partial ordinary
covering limit from 2.8.16. Its graph requires fineness at the base
point and equality of the upper and lower covering limits. No
existence or finite-value theorem is assumed by the definition;
almost-everywhere existence is the later assertion of 2.9.5.

[`DerivationTheorems.v`](../../theories/DerivationTheorems.v) is the public section interface and is exported
by [`MeasureTheory.v`](../../theories/MeasureTheory.v). Four regressions in [`DerivationExamples.v`](../../tests/DerivationExamples.v)
check undefined 0/0 and infinity/infinity, unit denominator evaluation
with arbitrary numerator, and zero continuous part for a null reference
measure. The example module is registered for future project kernel
checks; no full rebuild or audit is launched by that registration.

### 2.9.2 — Borel restriction and absolutely continuous part

Federer, p. 153. [`federer_2_9_2`](../../theories/ContinuousPartDecomposition.v#L54) in
[`ContinuousPartDecomposition.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartDecomposition.v) proves the complete theorem under the
original standing context: phi in M, a phi Vitali relation V and psi in M.
The supporting [`measure_continuous_part_decomposition`](../../theories/ContinuousPartDecomposition.v#L23) isolates the two
measure-class hypotheses actually used. It supplies a Borel set B with
phi(X minus B)=0 and psi_phi = psi restricted to B. Both psi_phi and the
complementary restriction belong to the original class M. The measure
psi equals psi_phi exactly when every phi-null set is psi-null.

[`ContinuousPartDecompositionUP.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartDecompositionUP.v) states the conclusions as
[`is_continuous_part_decomposition`](../../theories/ContinuousPartDecompositionUP.v#L19); it includes the pointwise sum
psi(A)=psi_phi(A)+(psi restricted to the complement of B)(A) for every A.
This is the precise additive interpretation of the printed remaining-part
notation psi minus psi_phi, which would be undefined as ordinary
extended-real subtraction when both values are infinite.
[`MeasureAbsoluteContinuity.v`](../../theories/MeasureAbsoluteContinuity.v) gives the printed null-set definition over
all subsets, proves absolute continuity of the continuous part, and
proves the equality criterion from the infimum laws.
[`DerivationMeasureRestriction.v`](../../theories/DerivationMeasureRestriction.v) proves stability of M under Borel
restriction using the established Borel-regular restriction theorem and
bounded-set monotonicity.

The local-to-global proof follows the book. Attainment is restricted to
its Borel parent in [`ContinuousPartRestrictedAttainment.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartRestrictedAttainment.v).
[`ContinuousPartLocal.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartLocal.v) first compares two complementary finite sums
for a Borel subset S of a bounded Borel A. The cancellation and its
finiteness hypothesis are explicit. [`CommonBorelHulls.v`](../../theories/CommonBorelHulls.v) supplies a
simultaneous Borel hull inside A for phi and psi. The already proved
finite equal-measure criterion of 2.1.4 makes these genuine hulls;
[`ContinuousPartHulls.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartHulls.v) transfers the continuous-part value across the
phi hull, and the psi hull transfers the restricted value. This proves
the local identity for arbitrary S, without a measurability assumption
on S.

[`BoundedBorelPartitionUP.v`](../../theories/BoundedBorelPartitionUP.v) specifies disjoint bounded Borel pieces
covering X. [`BoundedBorelPartition.v`](../../theories/BoundedBorelPartition.v) proves their existence by
sequence disjointification of closed balls, with an explicit empty-space
case. [`ContinuousPartRestriction.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartRestriction.v) takes the union of the locally
selected subsets. [`MeasureDisjointIntersectionSum.v`](../../theories/MeasureDisjointIntersectionSum.v) expresses the two
countable sums by applying additivity to restricted measures, so the
argument remains valid for arbitrary test sets and infinite totals.
The resulting restriction identity yields Borel regularity of both parts.

The proof of Borel measurability in
[`ContinuousPartBorel.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartBorel.v) uses a direct
split of an attaining Borel almost cover by a Borel test set. This differs
from the book's invocation of 2.3.2(9). The remaining local and global proof
steps follow Federer. No decomposition or measure existence is added as an
axiom.

Four additional regressions check self-reference without finite total mass,
the empty ambient partition, the whole remaining component for a null
reference measure, and a reference-null atom whose continuous part vanishes.

erratum has no relevant correction to this theorem. All thirteen new theories,
the public export and eight derivation examples compile without warnings. All
32 new dependency queries use only the permitted assumptions, and fifteen
targeted kernel checks pass. Actual checks are recorded in
[verification.txt](../verification.txt). No full rebuild or audit was run.

### 2.9.3 — Strict lower-limit comparison

Federer, p. 154. [`federer_2_9_3`](../../theories/DerivationLowerComparison.v#L24) in [`DerivationLowerComparison.v`](../../theories/DerivationLowerComparison.v)
proves the printed lemma: if alpha and beta belong to the original M,
0<c<infinity, and the lower V limit of alpha/beta is strictly below c
at every point of A, then alpha_phi(A) ≤ c beta_phi(A). The target A
need not be measurable or bounded; infinite measure values are allowed.
The standing phi Vitali context and ordinary partial quotient are retained.

The proof follows Federer. [`ContinuousPartOpenApproximation.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartOpenApproximation.v) attains
the infimum and applies the already proved open approximation 2.2.2(2).
It derives the needed countable finite-measure open cover from M, without
weakening that class. [`CoveringLowerSublevel.v`](../../theories/CoveringLowerSublevel.v) makes the strict ratio
subrelation fine after localisation inside the open almost cover W.
An additional positive diameter cutoff preserves fineness and makes
all selected cells bounded. [`DerivationSublevelCells.v`](../../theories/DerivationSublevelCells.v) and
[`MeasureRatioComparison.v`](../../theories/MeasureRatioComparison.v) then obtain finite numerator and positive
finite denominator values from M and the partial graph.

[`DerivationSublevelSelection.v`](../../theories/DerivationSublevelSelection.v) applies the Vitali selection, discards
only phi-null remainders, and compares the disjoint alpha and beta sums.
[`ScaledMeasureApproximation.v`](../../theories/ScaledMeasureApproximation.v) removes the open approximation error;
the infinite right-hand measure case is explicit. No target measurability,
finite total mass, default value for 0/0, or differentiation theorem is
assumed. The diameter cutoff refines only the auxiliary proof relation.

### 2.9.4 — Lower and upper comparisons with the reference measure

Federer, p. 154. [`federer_2_9_4`](../../theories/DerivationComparison.v#L80) in [`DerivationComparison.v`](../../theories/DerivationComparison.v)
proves both printed implications for psi in the original M and 0<c<infinity.
A strict lower V limit of psi/phi below c on A yields
psi_phi(A) ≤ c phi(A); a strict upper V limit above c yields the
reverse inequality. Targets are arbitrary and may have infinite measure.
[`measure_continuous_part_self`](../../theories/DerivationComparison.v#L24) obtains phi_phi=phi directly from 2.9.2,
so the first implication follows from 2.9.3.

For the second implication, [`DerivationReciprocalLimit.v`](../../theories/DerivationReciprocalLimit.v) proves the
printed reciprocal-limit step. Choose a finite threshold d strictly
between c and the upper limit, using rational density even when the
upper limit is infinite. [`CoveringUpperValues.v`](../../theories/CoveringUpperValues.v) supplies above-threshold
values on arbitrarily small cells. A diameter cutoff makes those cells
bounded; M makes both masses finite, and [`FiniteMeasureRatioReversal.v`](../../theories/FiniteMeasureRatioReversal.v)
checks positivity before reversing the partial quotient. The reverse
lower limit is at most 1/d, hence strictly below 1/c. Apply 2.9.3 and
multiply by c, with infinite values allowed.

### 2.9.5 — Existence of finite nonnegative derivates almost everywhere

Federer, pp. 154–155.
[`federer_2_9_5`](../../theories/DerivationExistence.v#L77) in
[`DerivationExistence.v`](../../theories/DerivationExistence.v) proves the
entire printed theorem: for psi in the original M and the standing phi Vitali
context, phi almost every point has a defined V derivate with a finite
nonnegative value. The statement uses a partial graph witness, so existence is
proved and undefined values are excluded only on a null exceptional set.
[`measure_derivate_exists_ae`](../../theories/DerivationExistence.v#L24) also
gives the supporting conclusion that the null-cell subrelation is not fine at
almost every point, as stated.

The proof follows the book's three exceptional sets. [`VitaliNullFibers.v`](../../theories/VitaliNullFibers.v)
selects a countable disjoint almost cover from phi-null cells and bounds
their union by a zero numerical sum. [`DerivationRatioDomain.v`](../../theories/DerivationRatioDomain.v) derives
a positive scale with no null denominator cells outside that exceptional
set. Bounded small cells have finite denominator measure, so the quotient
domain remains fine. It also proves nonnegativity of defined ratios and
of the lower limit, without mistaking the latter for limit existence.

[`DerivationExceptionalSets.v`](../../theories/DerivationExceptionalSets.v) defines the infinite-upper-limit set Q
and the separated-limit sets R(a,b). The two comparisons of 2.9.4 force
their bounded subsets to have zero phi measure. The finite-mass arguments
are explicit in [`DerivationExceptionalArithmetic.v`](../../theories/DerivationExceptionalArithmetic.v).
[`BoundedNullLocalisation.v`](../../theories/BoundedNullLocalisation.v) passes from bounded subsets to the whole
exceptional set using the previously proved abstract countable bounded
Borel partition. Both measures are finite on each such cell under M.
[`DerivationRationalGaps.v`](../../theories/DerivationRationalGaps.v) uses positive rational pairs to exhaust every
possible strict gap between the nonnegative lower limit and the upper
limit, including infinite endpoints. Their countable union is null.
Off this union, the partial-domain fineness orders the limits, the
absence of gaps makes them equal, and exclusion of Q makes the common
value finite. No derivative existence theorem is imported or assumed.

### 2.9.7 — Integral representation of the continuous part

Federer, pp. 155–156. [`federer_2_9_7`](../../theories/DerivationIntegral.v#L52) in [`DerivationIntegral.v`](../../theories/DerivationIntegral.v)
proves the complete theorem: a phi measurable A is psi_phi measurable,
and its psi_phi mass is the integral of the partial derivate over A.
The integral specification proves existence and its value, including
positive infinity; it adds no finite total-mass assumption.

[`DerivationMeasurableCover.v`](../../theories/DerivationMeasurableCover.v) expands the first proof sentence. Apply
the finite Borel sandwich of 2.2.3 on the existing bounded Borel cells,
then take their union. This gives a Borel superset of any measurable A
with a phi-null remainder, including when A has infinite measure.
[`ContinuousPartMeasurableSets.v`](../../theories/ContinuousPartMeasurableSets.v) transfers that null remainder to
psi_phi and proves the measurable-set assertion.

[`DerivationZeroAndNonreal.v`](../../theories/DerivationZeroAndNonreal.v) removes the two value sets. Z contains
points with a defined zero derivate. W uses Federer's correction:
no finite real graph value exists, including points where the partial
derivate is undefined (erratum p. 3, book p. 155 line 26). The
existence theorem makes W phi-null, hence also psi_phi-null. The zero
set has zero psi_phi mass by the strict comparisons on bounded subsets
and the vanishing-coefficient argument in [`SmallCoefficientMeasureBound.v`](../../theories/SmallCoefficientMeasureBound.v).
Its integral-density mass is zero even if phi(Z) is infinite.

[`GeometricFunctionBands.v`](../../theories/GeometricFunctionBands.v) and [`DerivationGeometricCells.v`](../../theories/DerivationGeometricCells.v) give the
exact half-open bands A intersect {t^j ≤ D < t^(j+1)}, for integer j
and t>1. They are measurable, disjoint and cover A outside Z and W.
[`DerivateClosedLevelBounds.v`](../../theories/DerivateClosedLevelBounds.v) derives the endpoint mass bounds from
2.9.4 at t*c and c/t, then removes the multiplicative error; this
justifies the closed lower endpoints in the printed calculation.
[`DensityCellBounds.v`](../../theories/DensityCellBounds.v) bounds each cell's integral between the same
constant multiples using characteristic step functions.

[`DerivationDensityRepresentation.v`](../../theories/DerivationDensityRepresentation.v) follows the two geometric sums.
It uses the already sealed density-measure operator of 2.4.10 to express
the nonnegative integral sum through countable measure additivity.
[`DisjointAlmostCoverComparison.v`](../../theories/DisjointAlmostCoverComparison.v) proves the required sum comparison,
including infinite totals. At every t>1 the two target masses differ
by at most factor t in either direction. The established geometric
squeeze gives equality. [`DerivationIntegral.v`](../../theories/DerivationIntegral.v) identifies this upper
subset integral with the actual nonnegative measurable integral on
the original partial domain. No representation or differentiation
theorem is assumed as an axiom or imported from another development.

### 2.9.8 — Differentiation of locally integrable functions

Federer, p. 156.
[`federer_2_9_8`](../../theories/MeasureDifferentiation.v#L33) in
[`MeasureDifferentiation.v`](../../theories/MeasureDifferentiation.v) proves
the complete theorem for an extended-real phi measurable partial function with
finite absolute integrals over all bounded phi measurable sets. Its actual
integral means converge to the original function phi almost everywhere. The
original class M and phi Vitali relation are retained. Pointwise finiteness is
a proved almost-everywhere consequence, not an added hypothesis

[`MeasureMeanFunction.v`](../../theories/MeasureMeanFunction.v) specifies the mean by a graph witness for the
subset integral and the ordinary partial quotient. It assigns no value
to an undefined integral, a zero denominator or infinity/infinity.
[`CoveringLimitGraphEquivalence.v`](../../theories/CoveringLimitGraphEquivalence.v) transfers equal graphs on Vitali
cells to the covering limits without changing their partial domains.

The nonnegative case follows Federer.
[`DensityMeasurableAlmostCover.v`](../../theories/DensityMeasurableAlmostCover.v)
uses the measurable comparison between a density and a nonnegative upper
approximant.
[`MeasurableExtendedComparisons.v`](../../theories/MeasurableExtendedComparisons.v)
proves the comparison loci measurable by rational ray separation, using no
subtraction of infinities.
[`DensityBorelRegular.v`](../../theories/DensityBorelRegular.v) converts
measurable almost covers into Borel ones and intersects reciprocal-error
covers to attain the density mass.
[`LocallyFiniteDensityClass.v`](../../theories/LocallyFiniteDensityClass.v)
proves membership in the original M: bounded closures extend finiteness from
measurable bounded targets to all bounded sets. The density's preservation of
null sets gives psi_phi=psi.

[`NonnegativeMeasureDifferentiation.v`](../../theories/NonnegativeMeasureDifferentiation.v) applies 2.9.7 and identifies the
density with the derivate from finite local subset integrals.
[`FiniteSubsetIntegralEquality.v`](../../theories/FiniteSubsetIntegralEquality.v) and [`LocalSubsetIntegralUniqueness.v`](../../theories/LocalSubsetIntegralUniqueness.v)
expand that uniqueness inference by splitting bounded Borel parents
into the two ordered comparison loci. The internal finite-integral
order equality test proves agreement there; only null domain exceptions
are discarded. The resulting mean graph agrees with the measure ratio
on every Vitali cell, giving the nonnegative differentiation theorem.

[`LocalAbsoluteIntegrability.v`](../../theories/LocalAbsoluteIntegrability.v) specifies the local absolute-integral
hypothesis and proves finite integrals for both parts and their signed
difference. [`LocalAbsoluteFiniteValues.v`](../../theories/LocalAbsoluteFiniteValues.v) proves finiteness almost
everywhere by a countable bounded exhaustion. [`FiniteMeanPartValues.v`](../../theories/FiniteMeanPartValues.v)
uses positive finite denominators to identify the signed mean with the
difference of the two finite part means. [`CoveringFiniteLimitBounds.v`](../../theories/CoveringFiniteLimitBounds.v)
proves the finite epsilon criterion with explicit fineness of the
restricted domain. [`SignedMeanLimit.v`](../../theories/SignedMeanLimit.v) obtains nonzero denominators
at a positive scale outside the null-cell exceptional set, combines
half-error estimates, and establishes the fine common domain before
subtracting the limits. [`MeasureDifferentiation.v`](../../theories/MeasureDifferentiation.v) then recombines the
two parts at the original finite graph values. The final theorem keeps

### 2.9.23 — Lipschitz L¹ curve without a weak derivative

### 2.9.25 — Differentiation of a parameter integral
