### 2.1.1 — Extended reals and numerical sums (pp. 51–53)

The scalar parameter is a complete Archimedean real field (`realType`).
Index sets are predicates on arbitrary choice types; they need not be
countable or inhabited. The existing scalar, finite-set and classical
set-theoretic background is reused. No external measure, integration,
infinite-summation or series theorem is imported.

Undefined scalar operations and signed sums have type `option`; `None`
is distinct from either infinity. The total extended arithmetic used
inside proofs is exposed as a partial operation with the appropriate
domain. Its exceptional zero product is named separately. Limits use the
order-topology interval criterion, with one-sided neighborhoods at infinity.

### 2.1.2 — Measures and measurable sets (pp. 53–54)

[`MeasureUP.v`](../../theories/MeasureUP.v) specifies a measure on **all** subsets by nonnegative values
and the inequality for every countable covering family. Empty-set measure
and monotonicity are derived, as in the book. [`MeasureIndexedCover.v`](../../theories/MeasureIndexedCover.v)
permits repeated sets in an indexed cover without imposing injectivity.
[`MeasureSplit.v`](../../theories/MeasureSplit.v) proves the general splitting inequality, the sufficient
finite-test criterion, and measurability of the empty and whole sets.

[`CountingMeasureUP.v`](../../theories/CountingMeasureUP.v) characterises counting measure through the cardinality
bounds of finite subsets. The existence proof is private, the witness is
sealed, and [`CountingMeasure.v`](../../theories/CountingMeasure.v) proves from the abstract law that finite
sets have their cardinality as value, infinite sets have value infinity,
the covering inequality holds, and every subset is measurable. The supporting
cover inequality in [`NonnegativeSumCover.v`](../../theories/NonnegativeSumCover.v) even allows uncountable covers.
[`NonemptyMeasure.v`](../../theories/NonemptyMeasure.v) constructs the other printed example and proves that its
measurable sets are exactly the empty and whole sets, including empty and
singleton ambient spaces.

[`MeasureRestriction.v`](../../theories/MeasureRestriction.v) proves that
restriction by **any** subset is a measure and preserves every originally
measurable set. [`MeasurePushforward.v`](../../theories/MeasurePushforward.v)
proves the image measure law and the final equivalence: an inverse image is
measurable exactly when the corresponding target set is measurable for the
pushforward of **every** restriction. Functions are total, following Federer
The splitting equivalence itself is proved for arbitrary set functions.

Federer's erratum contains no correction to this item.

Four regressions check the two-point counting value, a nonmeasurable
singleton for the zero-or-one measure, the validity of restriction by that
nonmeasurable singleton, and the zero value of restriction by the empty set.

### 2.1.3 — Measurable-set laws (pp. 54–56)

All seven numbered assertions are implemented:

| Assertion | Public theorem |
| --- | --- |
| (1), complement | [`measure_measurable_complement`](../../theories/MeasureFiniteClosure.v#L17) |
| (2), countable unions and intersections | [`measure_measurable_countable_union`](../../theories/MeasureCountableClosure.v#L61), [`measure_measurable_countable_intersection`](../../theories/MeasureCountableClosure.v#L94) |
| (3), disjoint sequence additivity | [`measure_sequence_disjoint_additivity`](../../theories/MeasureCountableAdditivity.v#L44) |
| (4), increasing continuity | [`measure_continuity_below`](../../theories/MeasureContinuityBelow.v#L20) |
| (5), decreasing continuity with finite first measure | [`measure_continuity_above`](../../theories/MeasureContinuityAbove.v#L21) |
| (6), null-set measurability | [`measure_null_measurable`](../../theories/MeasureFiniteClosure.v#L73) |
| (7), inclusion–exclusion with one measurable set | [`measure_inclusion_exclusion`](../../theories/MeasureFiniteAdditivity.v#L37) |

Finite union, intersection and difference closure precede the countable
arguments. Additivity is also proved for arbitrary countable indexed disjoint
families, including empty families. Countable intersection closure includes
the empty-family case, with intersection equal to the whole ambient space;
Federer states the nonempty-family case. Both continuity statements use the
extended-real order topology and start natural-number indices at zero.
Increasing continuity allows an infinite limit. Decreasing continuity retains
the finite-first-measure hypothesis.

The proofs use Federer's disjoint increments, finite unions and restrictions.
For countable-union measurability, finite splitting bounds pass directly to
the numerical sum. This is a change in proof order: countable closure is
established before continuity from above, avoiding the simultaneous limit of
the two test-set pieces. Continuity from above then follows the book's
relative-complement argument, using the proved continuity of defined
extended-real subtraction. Inclusion–exclusion uses only splitting and
addition; no finite-measure assumption or undefined subtraction is introduced.

it does not include Federer's (7). All three claims are regression lemmas in
[`MeasureExamples.v`](../../tests/MeasureExamples.v). Counting-measure source
headers now also cite this exact; stripping comments recovers their preceding
mathematical source unchanged.

### 2.1.4 — Measurable hulls (p. 56)

[`is_measure_hull`](../../theories/MeasureHull.v#L18) specifies a measurable superset whose intersection measure
agrees with that of the original set for every measurable test. The
finite-measure criterion [`measure_hull_of_finite_equal_measure`](../../theories/MeasureHull.v#L41) is proved
by splitting both sets and cancelling a finite remainder, following the
book's comparison. [`measure_hull_equal_measure`](../../theories/MeasureHull.v#L28) records the consequence
obtained by testing with the whole space.

Two regressions test its scope: a nonmeasurable singleton for the zero-or-one
measure has the whole two-point space as a hull, whereas the naturals without
zero and all the naturals have equal infinite counting measure but fail the
hull test at zero.

### 2.1.5 — Regularity and regularisation (pp. 56–57)

[`is_regular_measure`](../../theories/RegularMeasureUP.v#L18) specifies an equal-measure measurable superset for every
set. All five numbered assertions are proved from this specification:

| Assertion | Public theorem |
| --- | --- |
| (1), increasing continuity for arbitrary sets | [`regular_measure_continuity_below`](../../theories/RegularMeasureContinuity.v#L19) |
| (2), finite sets have measurable hulls | [`regular_measure_finite_hull`](../../theories/RegularMeasureUP.v#L25) |
| (3), finite additive measurable union has measurable pieces | [`regular_measure_additive_partition`](../../theories/RegularMeasurePartition.v#L65) |
| (4), image-measurability implies preimage-measurability for a finite measure | [`regular_measure_pushforward_measurable_preimage`](../../theories/RegularMeasurePushforward.v#L20) |
| (5), classification of measurable sets for restriction to a finite-measure set | [`regular_measure_restriction_measurable_iff`](../../theories/RegularMeasureRestriction.v#L77) |

The proofs use the book's measurable supersets, tail intersections, hulls
and null remainders. In (5), the restricting set itself need not be originally
measurable. Its restricted measurable sets are exactly `(B intersection S)
union C`, where B is originally measurable and C lies outside S.

All three printed counterexamples are verified as measures. On an infinite
space, assigning zero to the empty set, one to nonempty finite sets and two
to infinite sets defeats (1) and (2). On three points, assigning two only
to the whole space and one to every nonempty proper subset defeats (3);
its indicator map to two points has counting image measure and defeats (4).
The third example assigns `card(A)` when A lies in the two-point set S and
two otherwise. This uses **Federer's erratum p. 1, book p. 57 line 26**,
which corrects the printed `card(S)`. Its restriction has every set
measurable, but an inside singleton lacks the representation in (5).

[`is_measure_regularisation`](../../theories/MeasureRegularisationUP.v#L18) specifies the infimum of the original measures
of measurable supersets. Consequences are proved from its lower-bound and
greatest-lower-bound laws. A private construction proves existence, and
[`MeasureRegularisationSealed.v`](../../theories/MeasureRegularisationSealed.v) exposes an opaque witness through those laws.
The public [`measure_regularisation`](../../theories/MeasureRegularisation.v#L18) is a regular measure, preserves the
values and measurability of originally measurable sets, and adds no
measurable set of finite new measure that was not already measurable.
The infimum is attained by a countable intersection of approximating
measurable supersets; that fact also supplies the covering-inequality proof.

Four regressions check counting measure as a regularisation fixed point, the
empty-set value, the change of a singleton's value in the first example, and
the corrected third example's empty/singleton/two-point values.

### 2.1.6 — Ulam cardinals (pp. 58–59)

[`is_ulam_set`](../../theories/UlamCardinalUP.v#L24) expresses the point-null finite-measure definition on cardinal
representatives. Injection comparison expresses cardinal bounds, and
[`ulam_set_cardinal_invariant`](../../theories/UlamCardinalUP.v#L44) proves independence of the representative.
The measured carrier may be a subset of an ambient type; this retains
empty carriers. The following printed mathematical assertions are proved:

| Assertion | Public theorem |
| --- | --- |
| Equivalence with disjoint null families | [`ulam_set_null_families_iff`](../../theories/UlamNullFamilies.v#L68) |
| Aleph zero and every smaller cardinal are Ulam | [`ulam_set_countable`](../../theories/UlamCountable.v#L33) |
| Downward closure | [`ulam_set_downward`](../../theories/UlamCardinalUP.v#L33) |
| (1), union/supremum closure with Ulam index cardinality | [`ulam_set_union`](../../theories/UlamUnion.v#L26) |
| (2), successor of an Ulam cardinal | [`ulam_set_successor`](../../theories/UlamSuccessor.v#L22) |
| Least non-Ulam cardinal cannot be accessible | [`least_non_ulam_not_accessible`](../../theories/UlamAccessibility.v#L44) |
| Such a cardinal is uncountable | [`least_non_ulam_uncountable`](../../theories/UlamAccessibility.v#L60) |

[`MeasureDisjointFamily.v`](../../theories/MeasureDisjointFamily.v) proves the measure law for the subfamily-union
operator and the disjointness identities needed for measurability. A choice
of a covering member at each point gives the null-cover consequence used
in the union and successor arguments. The union theorem holds for arbitrary
representative sets; for initial ordinal representatives their union is
the printed supremum.

[`is_cardinal_successor`](../../theories/CardinalSuccessorUP.v#L19) is an abstract cardinal-comparison specification:
strict comparison and no intermediate cardinal. The general set-theoretic
well-ordering principle produces a representative with all proper initial
segments bounded by the predecessor. The public successor theorem includes
finite and empty predecessors. Four regressions cover empty and natural
cardinals, the specification of zero's successor, and the theorem at zero
and one.

The matrix `U(b,a)` follows Federer p. 59 and the correction `a in alpha`
in erratum p. 1, book p. 59 line 3. Each column has countably many positive
entries, by finite additivity and the locally proved countable-support
summation theorem. There is one deliberate proof change: instead of
estimating the cardinality of exceptional pairs by `aleph-zero times alpha`,
the null-cover theorem shows their projection has measure zero. If the
whole measure is nonzero, a point outside that projection gives the same
null row and final predecessor/singleton/tail decomposition.

Accessibility is specified by a successor certificate or a union of smaller
representatives indexed by a smaller cardinal, up to equipollence. This is
the printed cofinal-union description for initial ordinal representatives.
The least-non-Ulam conclusion is conditional; no non-Ulam cardinal is assumed
to exist. The final historical consistency remark citing [T A] is recorded
as metamathematical context, **not** asserted as a Rocq theorem or an axiom.
The project does not formalise a metatheory of ZFC consistency.

Our successor proof starts with any well ordering and selects an equipollent
subset, so this is supporting context rather than an attribution of the
identical formal lemma. The local [Kelley](../bibliography.md#kelley2017) *General Topology* (Dover 2017
reprint of 1955) supplies the background explicitly cited by Federer: Appendix
Definition 87/Theorem 88, p. 263, and Definition 148/Theorems 154, 158–159,
pp. 275–276. The only additional external ingredient is the proved general
well-ordering principle from `mathcomp.classical.wochoice`; no external
measure or cardinal-arithmetic theorem replaces Federer's argument.

## Section notes

### Chapter 2 conventions and verification records

Federer's Chapter 2 occupies printed pp. 51–206. Its public interface is
`Federer.MeasureTheory`; existence witnesses stay behind sealed modules.
Each section's account includes the assertions in the prose and examples,
with boundary qualifications and corrections explicitly recorded. Historical
and metamathematical remarks are context rather than axioms. The continuum
hypothesis appears only as an explicit premise in the relevant affirmative
case of 2.5.10; 2.10.10 also supplies that item's geometric example.

Federer's “measure” means an outer measure on all subsets. Extended-real
arithmetic remains partial at undefined expressions, with the exceptional
zero-times-infinity convention named separately. Chapters 1–2 and 14 support
§2.2. All locators are edition-specific.

Earlier releases validated §§2.1–2.6 by a clean build, recursive kernel
check and fresh full audit; §2.7 used a clean build, recursive check and
audit of changed dependencies. These are historical records, superseded
only by the actual later scopes recorded in [verification.txt](../verification.txt).
The [documentation index](../toc.md) gives current coverage and validation.
