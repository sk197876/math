### 2.4.1 — Step functions and the assigned numerical sum

A step function is measurable, has countable real range, and has a defined
extended numerical sum of value times fibre measure. The range need not be
finite and the assigned sum may be positive or negative infinity. The
convention 0 times infinity equals 0 is explicit; an undefined difference
of infinite variations is excluded. Partial domains remain conull rather
than being silently replaced by total functions.

[`MeasureStepFunctionUP.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepFunctionUP.v) states the step predicate and the assigned-sum
specification. [`MeasureStepIntegralSealed.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepIntegralSealed.v) exposes only a chosen value and
its law; [`MeasureStepIntegral.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepIntegral.v) proves the public value is independent of
the proof of membership. [`MeasureStepFibres.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepFibres.v) and
[`MeasureStepCommonRefinement.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepCommonRefinement.v) prove the countable fibre-partition and the
row/column measure identities. [`MeasureStepSumFibres.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepSumFibres.v) groups the common
refinement by r+s=t.

[`MeasureStepAddition.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepAddition.v) follows the printed proof through weighted product
sums, defined pointwise addition and grouping, using the local numerical-sum
laws of 2.1.1. Its hypothesis retains the required defined sum of the two
assigned values. [`MeasureStepScaling.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepScaling.v) proves real homogeneity, treating
zero separately so the theorem also covers infinite original sums.
Countable-range and measurability closure are proved for both operations.
Constants are step functions even on spaces of infinite measure.

Regressions cover the constant one on the infinite counting space and its
zero multiple, opposite infinite constant sums excluded by the additivity
hypothesis, and constants on null or empty sources.

### 2.4.2 — Upper, lower and ordinary integrals

The upper/lower approximants are real-valued step functions satisfying the
respective inequality almost everywhere against an extended-real-valued
partial function. The upper and lower integrals are specified by their
extremal laws over the assigned values of these approximants. A private
existence proof uses the project's extended-real bounds; a sealed interface
exposes the values only through these laws.

[`MeasureIntegralUP.v`](../../theories/MeasureIntegralUP.v) also specifies integrability by measurability and a
common upper/lower value, and summability by existence of a finite integral.
[`MeasureIntegral.v`](../../theories/MeasureIntegral.v) proves the equivalence with equality of the public bounds,
identifies the integral with either bound, proves independence from the proof

The public integral is available only for a function proved integrable.

[`MeasureIntegralEmptyBounds.v`](../../theories/MeasureIntegralEmptyBounds.v) proves the printed empty-family conventions:
no upper functions gives upper integral +infinity, and no lower functions
gives lower integral -infinity. Regressions instantiate both cases with the
respective infinite constant on a counting source of mass one.

### 2.4.3 — Upper and lower integral inequalities

All seven assertions are formalised, with infinite values and partial
function domains retained:

1. The lower integral equals minus the upper integral of the negative.
2. Almost-everywhere pointwise comparison implies comparison of upper integrals.
3. An almost-everywhere nonnegative function has nonnegative upper integral.
4. An upper integral below positive infinity implies a finite upper integral
   for the positive part and pointwise values below positive infinity almost
   everywhere. The original upper integral may be negative infinity.
5. Multiplication by a positive real scalar commutes with upper integration.
6. If the sum of the upper integrals is defined and below positive infinity,
   the partial pointwise sum has conull domain and its upper integral is
   at most that sum.
7. The lower integral is at most the upper integral.

[`MeasureIntegralNegation.v`](../../theories/MeasureIntegralNegation.v), [`MeasureUpperIntegralMonotone.v`](../../theories/MeasureUpperIntegralMonotone.v),
[`MeasureUpperIntegralNonnegative.v`](../../theories/MeasureUpperIntegralNonnegative.v), [`MeasureUpperIntegralPositivePart.v`](../../theories/MeasureUpperIntegralPositivePart.v),
[`MeasureUpperIntegralScale.v`](../../theories/MeasureUpperIntegralScale.v), [`MeasureUpperIntegralSubadditive.v`](../../theories/MeasureUpperIntegralSubadditive.v), and
[`MeasureIntegralBoundsOrder.v`](../../theories/MeasureIntegralBoundsOrder.v) contain the principal results.
The supporting modules establish positivity of assigned step sums,
positive-part closure with its precise variation sum, strict approximation
from the abstract infimum laws, and addition of upper step functions.

The proof follows Federer's order argument. In (6), real upper bounds
are chosen using the previously established rational density; step-function
addition then contradicts any strict failure of subadditivity. In (7),
negation, subadditivity and nonnegativity give the printed contradiction
using f-f only on its actual domain. No cancellation of equal infinities
is permitted. The inequalities remain valid for arbitrary partial functions,
a slight extension of the book's conull-domain premise: existence of an
upper function or the finite-bound hypotheses supply the needed domains.

Regressions cover the positive part of constant -1 for infinite counting
measure (original assigned sum -infinity, positive-part sum zero) and the
empty domain of constant positive infinity minus itself.

### 2.4.4 — Integrability, algebra and absolute values

All eight assertions and the regressions passed the integrated verification.
The formalisation includes step-function identification, nonnegative
integrability, nonzero scalar homogeneity in both directions, addition for
all defined extended integral sums, comparison with automatic integrability,
the part-difference criterion, the absolute-value bound, and absolute-value
summability.

The nonnegative proof follows Federer's two cases. Positive measure of the
infinite level yields arbitrarily large characteristic lower functions.
Otherwise geometric cells [t^j,t^(j+1)) produce a lower step function u with
t*u an upper function. Cell coverage is proved locally by an Archimedean
power estimate and first crossing; the scalar quantisation is specified by
laws, constructed privately and sealed. Infinite values are replaced by zero
only inside the approximant, with their nullity checked for comparisons.
The approximant retains f's conull partial domain, whereas Federer assigns
zero also at points outside that domain. This documented null-set variation
preserves the upper/lower comparisons.

The comparison proof uses upper/lower monotonicity with the finite-part
criterion to make the book's implicit integrability conclusions explicit;
this is a documented variation on the printed difference argument.
Addition retains finite, positive-infinite and negative-infinite sums.
Opposite infinities remain undefined. The final arguments identify the
absolute integral with the sum of the two nonnegative part integrals.

### 2.4.5 — Characteristic functions and outer measure

The three assertions are stated for arbitrary characteristic maps satisfying
the abstract one/zero laws:

1. A measurable set's characteristic function is integrable, with integral
   equal to the measure of the set, including infinite measure.
2. For every set, its measure lies between the lower and upper integrals
   of its characteristic function.
3. For a regular measure, the upper integral equals the measure of the set.

[`MeasureCharacteristicIntegral.v`](../../theories/MeasureCharacteristicIntegral.v), [`MeasureCharacteristicUpperBound.v`](../../theories/MeasureCharacteristicUpperBound.v),
[`MeasureCharacteristicLowerBound.v`](../../theories/MeasureCharacteristicLowerBound.v), and [`MeasureCharacteristicRegular.v`](../../theories/MeasureCharacteristicRegular.v)
contain the principal results. [`MeasureAlmostSubsetBound.v`](../../theories/MeasureAlmostSubsetBound.v) and
[`MeasureStepFibreUnion.v`](../../theories/MeasureStepFibreUnion.v) supply the null-exception comparison and the
countable-additivity calculation used in the proofs.

The lower bound uses levels between zero and one and the upper bound levels at
least one. Zero-measure fibres are retained in these countable families; their
summands vanish. The lower comparison passes through the positive variation of
the assigned sum, without imposing finiteness. The regular case uses a
measurable superset of equal measure, as in the source.

Regressions cover the empty characteristic function on an arbitrary
measure space and the infinite integral of the characteristic function
of all natural numbers under counting measure.

### 2.4.6 — Fatou's lemma

[`MeasureFatou.v`](../../theories/MeasureFatou.v) states Fatou's lemma for nonnegative measurable
extended-real functions, preserving infinite integral values and partial
domains. It proves integrability of the pointwise lower-limit function
as well as the integral inequality. Nonnegativity may hold almost
everywhere, a null-set extension of the printed hypothesis. Abstract
integral-value and sealed lower-limit formulations are both provided.

The proof follows Federer's finite-family argument. A lower step sum is
bounded by its positive variation, which is tested on finite positive
level sets. Removing a common null exceptional set preserves their
measures. The increasing sets B_i,n exhaust these test sets; finite sums
of weighted characteristic functions bound each sequence integral.
Continuity from below for measures, finite increasing-sum bounds and
the scalar contraction squeeze give the result. No external integration
or measure-convergence theorem is imported.

[`ExtendedLowerLimitUP.v`](../../theories/ExtendedLowerLimitUP.v), [`ExtendedLowerLimitConstruction.v`](../../theories/ExtendedLowerLimitConstruction.v),
[`ExtendedLowerLimitSealed.v`](../../theories/ExtendedLowerLimitSealed.v), and [`ExtendedLowerLimit.v`](../../theories/ExtendedLowerLimit.v) provide the
scalar lower limit through abstract supremum laws and a sealed witness.
The pointwise lower limit continues to use the existing §2.3 interface.
The remaining support modules make the finite simple-function comparison,
tail-set exhaustion, null restriction and scalar limiting steps explicit.

A regression uses the characteristic functions of successive singleton
natural numbers with counting measure. Every integral is one; the
pointwise lower limit is zero with integral zero. Thus Fatou's inequality
can be strict.

### 2.4.7 — Lebesgue's increasing convergence theorem

[`MeasureIncreasingConvergence.v`](../../theories/MeasureIncreasingConvergence.v) proves increasing convergence of the
integrals to the integral of the pointwise limit. It explicitly proves
existence of the pointwise increasing limit on a conull set, integrability
of the limit, and convergence of the integral values, including infinity.
Abstract integral-value and sealed-limit versions are provided.

The proof follows Federer: monotonicity bounds every sequence integral
by the limit's integral, and Fatou's lemma gives the reverse limiting
inequality. The supporting modules identify the lower limit of an
increasing scalar sequence with its supremum and transfer this result
to partial-function graphs. Convergence uses the existing order-interval
definition of increasing extended-real limits.

The countable intersection of the good sets provides the common domain used in
the proof. No finiteness of the total measure or of the limiting integral is
required.

On the natural numbers with counting measure, the negatives of the
characteristic functions of successive infinite tails increase pointwise to
zero. Every integral is minus infinity, whereas the integral of the limit is
zero.

### 2.4.8 — Integration of countable nonnegative sums

[`MeasureCountableNonnegativeSum.v`](../../theories/MeasureCountableNonnegativeSum.v) proves that the integral of a countable
pointwise nonnegative sum is the sum of the integrals. It retains arbitrary
countable index sets, the empty family, partial summand domains and infinite
values. The summands are pointwise nonnegative, as in Federer. Abstract
integral-value and public sealed-sum versions are provided.

The proof uses a sealed increasing finite exhaustion of the countable
index set. Finite integral additivity gives the integrals of the finite
partial sums; §2.4.7 gives their limit. The numerical-sum exhaustion law
of 2.1.1(13) identifies this limit with the sum of the integral values.
The eventual domain of the finite sums is proved to be exactly the common
domain of all summands.

The pointwise sum is specified abstractly by its graph and the existing
numerical-sum laws. Its implementation is sealed. The specification also
handles signed sums at precisely the points where those sums are defined,
as supporting infrastructure; the interchange theorem is proved only
under the stated nonnegativity hypothesis. Undefined sums and points
outside any summand's domain receive no value. In the nonnegative case,
the domain is the full common domain. The empty sum is total and zero.

Regressions cover the empty family on an arbitrary measure space and a
countable family of constant-one functions on a unit mass, whose
pointwise sum and integral are both infinity.

### 2.4.9 — Lebesgue's bounded convergence theorem

[`MeasureBoundedConvergence.v`](../../theories/MeasureBoundedConvergence.v) states the bounded-convergence theorem
with arbitrary partial extended-real measurable functions. A summable
majorant bounds the absolute values almost everywhere, and the sequence
converges almost everywhere in the extended-real order topology. The
conclusions include finite integral values for the sequence, its limit
and the absolute differences, convergence of the error integrals to zero,
and convergence of the signed integral values.

The proof follows Federer's Fatou argument for `2h - |fn-g|`.
Summability gives almost-everywhere finiteness of h. Countably many
bounds and the pointwise limit yield a common conull region of finite
values. Domination implies summability, and integral invariance under
null modifications identifies the lower-limit integral with that of 2h.
The scalar consequence of Fatou is expressed through eventual strict
lower bounds, equivalently to Federer's displayed limsup calculation.
Here 2h is represented by h+h. No external integration theorem is used.

Supporting results include invariance of integral values under almost
equality, absolute summability and domination criteria, finiteness almost
everywhere, and integral differences. Scalar limits retain their abstract
order specification. Exact partial domains, including undefined infinite
subtractions on exceptional sets, are preserved throughout.

Regressions cover empty partial domains under a null measure and a
constant negative sequence on a unit mass, ensuring that neither total
domains nor nonnegative sequence terms are inadvertently required.

Sources: Federer 2.4.9, printed p. 85 The scan confirms the direction of
Federer's displayed inequality: zero is at least the limsup of the nonnegative
error integrals. No relevant erratum entry.

### 2.4.11 — Absolute continuity of the integral

[`MeasureIntegralAbsoluteContinuity.v`](../../theories/MeasureIntegralAbsoluteContinuity.v) states the complete strict estimate:
for every summable partial function f and positive epsilon, there is a
positive delta such that every measurable A with measure at most delta
has absolute integral over A strictly below epsilon. No finite ambient
measure is required.

The supporting modules define upper truncations retaining the original
partial domain, prove their measurability and increasing convergence,
and apply 2.4.7 to obtain a small integral of the truncation remainder.
The subset estimate bounds the restricted function by the whole remainder
plus a positive multiple of the characteristic function of A. Choosing
ceiling c=n+1 and delta=epsilon/(2c) gives Federer's strict inequality.
This comparison is equivalent to the two restricted terms in his proof;
it does not require constructing a value for an undefined signed integral.

Regressions exercise an empty partial domain under a null measure and a
singleton characteristic function under counting measure on the infinite
ambient space of natural numbers.

Sources: Federer 2.4.11, statement on printed p. 85 and proof on p. 86 the
formal target retains Federer's strict conclusion. No relevant Federer erratum
entry applies.

### 2.4.14 — Holder's inequality and equality criterion

[`MeasureHolder.v`](../../theories/MeasureHolder.v) states the complete finite-conjugate-exponent theorem for

real coefficient, in either orientation, on a conull set. The coefficient

its integral first establishes summability of the norm product. Positive

[`MeasureHolderNormProduct.v`](../../theories/MeasureHolderNormProduct.v)

### 2.4.19 — Jensen's inequality

The complete item passed the clean §2.4 build, full recursive kernel check
and fresh full assumption audit, including all regression examples.

[`MeasureJensen.v`](../../theories/MeasureJensen.v) supplies the full inequality for an abstract real Banach
space, a closed convex set C, a nonnegative convex function F continuous
only on C, a finite positive measure, and an L1 partial function g with
conull inverse image of C. [`measure_jensen_integral_values`](../../theories/MeasureJensen.v#L53) proves both
membership of the normalised vector integral in C and the extended-real
inequality for arbitrary integral witnesses. [`measure_jensen`](../../theories/MeasureJensen.v#L82) additionally
provides existence of both integrals, using their sealed interfaces.
The scalar integral may equal positive infinity. Null modifications retain
the original partial-domain convention. F is represented on the ambient
space, but all continuity, convexity and sign hypotheses concern only C;
its off-C values on the exceptional null set are irrelevant.

The proof follows the three stages in the book. [`FiniteImageJensen.v`](../../theories/FiniteImageJensen.v)
derives the finite-image inequality from actual fibre masses summing to
the total mass. Convex translation handles sets that do not contain zero,
and the finite convex inequality is proved using the epigraph.
[`ClosedSetFiniteApproximationUP.v`](../../theories/ClosedSetFiniteApproximationUP.v) specifies Borel finite-image C-valued
approximants with pointwise convergence and the reference-point error
bound. The existence proof uses a separable closed range, the sealed
Borel retraction and nearest finite-prefix approximation; its witness is
hidden by [`ClosedSetFiniteApproximationSealed.v`](../../theories/ClosedSetFiniteApproximationSealed.v). The error bound proves
L1 convergence. [`BoundedRangeJensen.v`](../../theories/BoundedRangeJensen.v) applies finite-image Jensen and
bounded convergence, deriving mean membership from closedness and the
scalar limit from relative continuity. This supporting stage permits
unbounded C: the integrable reference-point majorant replaces the printed
bounded-C premise, while F remains bounded.

[`ConvexClosedSublevels.v`](../../theories/ConvexClosedSublevels.v) proves that the simultaneous norm and F sublevels
are closed and convex. The final exhaustion uses the same sublevel family
as Federer, with cofinal thresholds containing a fixed point c of C.
One implementation difference is explicit: [`JensenSublevelApproximation.v`](../../theories/JensenSublevelApproximation.v)
puts c outside each inverse-image set, keeping the source measure and its
normalising mass fixed instead of normalising restricted measures.
The added complement contribution vanishes in L1, dominated by |g-c|.
When the scalar integral is finite, F(g_n) is dominated by F(g)+F(c), so
its integrals converge too. The infinite-integral case is separate;
mean membership follows from the already proved bounded case with F=0.
This changes the bookkeeping of the printed final limit, not its
hypotheses or inequality, and introduces no finite-dimensional restriction.

Sources: Federer 2.4.19, printed p. 91; May 1987 erratum p. 2, book p. 91
lines 10–11 (L1 notation and proof heading) The finite convex-combination
background is independently expanded in its 10.5(3), printed pp. 97–98; it
does not state the measure-theoretic inequality.

Regressions check a possibly nonzero singleton C with F unrestricted off
C, retention of mean membership when the scalar integral is positive
infinity, and impossibility of a conull empty range at positive mass.
