## 2.5.1 — Lattices of functions and their integral functionals

[`FunctionLatticeUP.v`](../../theories/FunctionLatticeUP.v) specifies
Federer's lattice by its five closure laws: addition, nonnegative scaling,
binary minimum, minimum with a nonnegative constant, and subtraction of
ordered pairs. The functions are total and finite real-valued. The definition
permits an empty lattice and does not impose vector-space structure.
[`FunctionLatticeLaws.v`](../../theories/FunctionLatticeLaws.v) derives zero
from a member, unrestricted binary maxima, both parts, and the positive
sublattice. The maximum assertion incorporates Federer's erratum p. 2, book p.
91 line 28: its sign restriction is removed. The proof uses f+(g-min(f,g)),
whose difference is ordered even when the maximum is negative. the
formalisation follows corrected Federer.

[`ContinuousFunctionLattice.v`](../../theories/ContinuousFunctionLattice.v) verifies the all-functions and continuous-
functions examples for arbitrary source topology. [`SummableRealFunctionLattice.v`](../../theories/SummableRealFunctionLattice.v)
verifies the summable-functions example using the project's integral
arithmetic and domination results. The constant truncation need not itself
be summable; its nonnegative value gives |min(f,c)|<=|f|. No finite-total-
measure assumption is introduced.

[`MonotoneDaniellIntegralUP.v`](../../theories/MonotoneDaniellIntegralUP.v) states the four functional laws in the final
paragraph on p. 92 and in 2.5.2. The name preserves Federer's distinction
in 2.5.6 between a monotone Daniell integral and a general signed Daniell
integral. [`RealIntegralFunctionalUP.v`](../../theories/RealIntegralFunctionalUP.v) specifies an integral assignment on
L; existence is proved separately and hidden by an opaque seal.
[`RealIntegralFunctional.v`](../../theories/RealIntegralFunctional.v) exposes the map and proves additivity, positive
homogeneity, order preservation and increasing continuity on every
summable sublattice. Increasing continuity for signed summable functions
is reduced to 2.4.7 by subtracting the first term. Off-L values of an
ambient representative are unspecified and irrelevant to all laws.

Regressions cover the empty lattice, a positive lattice that is not closed
under negation, and failure of summability when zero is truncated by a

## 2.5.2 — Representation of a monotone Daniell integral

[`MonotoneDaniellRepresentation.v`](../../theories/MonotoneDaniellRepresentation.v) states the full representation theorem:
every additive, positively homogeneous, monotone and increasing-continuous
real functional on Federer's function lattice has a representing measure,
and every lattice member is summable for that measure. The lattice need
not be a vector space, contain the constant one, or be nonempty. Functions
are total and finite real-valued; the ambient functional's values outside
the lattice remain irrelevant.

The proof follows Federer 2.5.2, printed pp. 92–93.

- Specify suiting sequences, their limiting costs by supremum laws, and the
set function by infimum laws. Prove existence and hide the witness behind
[`DaniellCoverSealed.v`](../../theories/DaniellCoverSealed.v).
- Prove the
measure laws using finite diagonal sums over a sealed exhaustion of an
arbitrary countable indexing set, followed by the summable-error selection
made explicit. This supplies the full countable-cover formulation of the
project's measure definition.
- Prove the supported-minorant bound, construct
the printed lattice cutoff, and split a suiting sequence into its minimum with
the cutoff and its positive remainder. Passing both increasing costs through
their bound gives level separation and hence measurability by the previously
proved 2.3.2(7).
- Compare consecutive truncation layers with weighted
characteristic functions, telescope their functional values and integrals, and
pass first to unbounded truncation levels and then to a zero lower cutoff. The
latter limit uses the cofinal sequence 1/(n+1). Infinite candidate integrals
remain allowed until equality with the finite functional value proves
summability.
- Pass to signed lattice members by f-minus=f-plus-f, using only
ordered subtraction, and expose the representing measure through
[`DaniellMeasureUP.v`](../../theories/DaniellMeasureUP.v), a proved existence
construction, and
[`DaniellMeasureSealed.v`](../../theories/DaniellMeasureSealed.v).

The empty case is explicit: the raw infimum of suiting-sequence costs is
infinite even on the empty set when the lattice is empty, so that formula
cannot supply a measure in this case. The representation assertion itself is
vacuous and has the zero measure as a witness. it is a boundary clarification
rather than an entry in Federer's erratum. The public specification requires
the envelope formula only for a nonempty lattice, retaining it for the
following 2.5.3 regularity discussion. No uniqueness of arbitrary representing
measures is asserted.

Natural sequences are indexed from zero, with the corresponding empty
finite-sum base case. The May 1987 erratum has no correction to 2.5.2; the
preceding lattice maximum uses its p. 2 correction to book p. 91 line 28.

Regressions cover representation on the empty lattice, failure of the raw
empty-lattice envelope to be a measure, evaluation on a positive cone that
is not closed under negation, and the zero functional forcing zero total
mass for the envelope measure.

## 2.5.3 — Regular representation, approximation, and independence

The target is Federer 2.5.3, printed pp. 94–95. The local May-1987 erratum has
no entry affecting book pp. 94–95.

For every representing measure, [`FunctionLatticeLevelRightLimit.v`](../../theories/FunctionLatticeLevelRightLimit.v)
proves the displayed formula for phi{f>t}, with f in L+ and t>0.
The limit ranges over all positive real widths tending to zero; the
proof first establishes convergence for reciprocal widths, then uses
monotonicity and cofinality. Positive homogeneity places the width's
inverse outside the functional exactly as in the book.

[`FunctionLatticeLevelFamilies.v`](../../theories/FunctionLatticeLevelFamilies.v) defines F0, F1 and F2 exactly as in
Federer, with natural indices starting at zero. Measurability,
finiteness of F0/F2, agreement of every pair of representing measures
on these families, and independence of F2 from the chosen representation
are proved separately. The finite mass of the first set in an F2
sequence is retained. The later examples of nonuniqueness referred to
in the opening sentence belong to 2.5.4 and 2.5.14.

[`FunctionLatticeRegularityUP.v`](../../theories/FunctionLatticeRegularityUP.v)
specifies Federer's finite-hull definition of L regularity. Suiting sequences
give F1 outer approximations; finite prefix intersections and arbitrarily
small errors give equal-mass F2 hulls.
[`RegularDaniellRepresentation.v`](../../theories/RegularDaniellRepresentation.v)
proves regularity of the sealed measure from 2.5.2 and its uniqueness among
regular representing measures. An order-bound argument replaces the printed
double-limit extraction, without weakening the hull conclusion. Every
representing measure is dominated by the regular one.
[`RegularDaniellEnvelope.v`](../../theories/RegularDaniellEnvelope.v) derives
the suiting-envelope law from the abstract regularity and representation laws,
so downstream statements need not mention a construction.

[`RegularDaniellConsequences.v`](../../theories/RegularDaniellConsequences.v) exposes all four further conditions:

- [`regular_daniell_inner_supremum`](../../theories/RegularDaniellConsequences.v#L20):
for a finite measurable A, its measure is the supremum of the measures of
K={h>=1} minus E contained in A, where h belongs to L+ and E belongs to F1.
The difference-set family is retained exactly. the quantitative bound at most
3 delta suffices for Federer's printed strict 4 delta estimate and the
resulting supremum law.
- [`regular_daniell_positive_l1_density`](../../theories/RegularDaniellConsequences.v#L35):
for a nonnegative summable partial function u and delta>0, some h in L+ has
integral |h-u| < 4 delta. The proof supplies a finite integral witness for the
error. Suiting sequences and increasing convergence approximate characteristic
functions, and the integral's finite-simple supremum reduces the general case
to finite positive combinations. This makes explicit the final reduction in
Federer, while. Definedness and nonnegativity are required only almost
everywhere; exceptional infinite values and partial domains remain allowed.
- [`regular_daniell_countably_finite_iff`](../../theories/RegularDaniellConsequences.v#L53):
T has an arbitrary countable cover by finite-measure sets exactly when a
series of functions in L+ dominates its characteristic function. The series
itself may have infinite sum; the empty covering family is included.
- [`regular_daniell_measurability_iff`](../../theories/RegularDaniellConsequences.v#L68):
a subset B is measurable exactly when B intersect {f>=1} is measurable for
every f in L+. Finite test sets are covered by countably many such levels, and
the infinite-test case follows from the measure splitting inequality.

The empty-lattice boundary is explicit. Closure under the five operations
in 2.5.1 permits empty L, and 2.5.2 still has a vacuous representation.
However F0, F1 and F2 are all empty, so no measure can be L regular:
even the empty set would need an F2 hull. Thus regular existence in
2.5.3 requires a nonempty lattice. This is a local boundary clarification,
not an entry in Federer's erratum. Abstract regularity itself implies
nonemptiness, so the four public consequences need no extra premise.

[`SequenceLimitCounterexample.v`](../../theories/SequenceLimitCounterexample.v) proves the final independence assertion
on the exact lattice of all convergent real sequences. The sequence-limit
functional is specified abstractly, supplied by an existence proof and
hidden behind [`SequenceLimitFunctionalSealed.v`](../../theories/SequenceLimitFunctionalSealed.v). It is additive, positively
homogeneous and monotone, but fails increasing continuity: finite-prefix
indicators increase pointwise to one while their functional values remain
zero. Indices start at zero, corresponding to the book's positive integers
by n+1. No weakened finite-support substitute for the lattice is used.

Regressions check failure of regularity for the empty lattice even with
zero measure, the prefix/constant-one jump of the sealed limit functional,
and the density theorem for a function with empty but conull domain under
zero measure.

## 2.5.6 — Additive variations and the variation integral

The names Daniell integral and monotone Daniell integral refer to the exact
four-law specifications introduced for 2.5.5 and 2.5.1–2.5.2. The subsequent
references to 2.5.7, 2.5.12 and 2.5.13 are previews of applications.

`LatticeAdditiveFunctional` isolates additivity alone and proves zero value,
reflection of each positive order interval by k ↦ f-k, and the resulting
transfer of real upper bounds to -mu. `LatticeExtendedVariation` defines
mu+ by the sealed supremum and mu- by the negative sealed infimum, proves
the equivalent dual supremum formula, and proves the exact finiteness
equivalence on L+. Neither a bound, homogeneity, nor continuity is assumed;
both variations may be infinite.

`VectorFunctionLattice` specifies the additional vector-space closure laws.
`VectorVariationFiniteBounds` uses the positive and negative parts for one
inequality and independent k,l in [0,f], with |k-l| <= f, for the reverse
inequality. `VectorVariationSupremum` proves the exact displayed formula
mu+(f)+mu-(f)=sup{|mu(g)|:g in L, |g| <= f}, including infinite variations.
The outer absolute value is present in Federer's printed formula. Negation
closure and additive negation explain this equivalence.

`VariationIntegralMeasure` names the sum of the real positive and negative
functionals and the sum of their representing measures. It proves the sum
measure laws, allowing infinite masses, and measurability of any set measurable
for both component measures. [`VariationMeasureIntegral.v`](../../theories/VariationMeasureIntegral.v) proves that the
variation measure is the measure associated with the variation integral.

- [`variation_measure_nonnegative_integral`](../../theories/VariationMeasureIntegral.v#L38): for measures psi+ and psi- and
  a nonnegative total extended-real function f, the integral of f for
  psi+ + psi- is the sum of its integrals for psi+ and psi-; infinite

- [`daniell_variation_integral_monotone`](../../theories/VariationMeasureIntegral.v#L124): under the hypotheses of 2.5.5,
  mu+ + mu- is a monotone Daniell integral on L+.
- [`variation_measure_regular`](../../theories/VariationMeasureIntegral.v#L140): the sum of two L regular measures is L

- [`variation_measure_associated`](../../theories/VariationMeasureIntegral.v#L193): psi+ + psi- is therefore the L+ regular

- [`daniell_variation_measure_representation`](../../theories/VariationMeasureIntegral.v#L229): for a Daniell integral mu on

## 2.5.7 — Representation of bounded linear functionals on total Lp spaces

[`lp_linear_functional_representation`](../../theories/LpLinearFunctionalRepresentation.v#L46) proves all three alternatives of
Federer 2.5.7, pp. 98–99: finite conjugate exponents, p=1 with countably
measurable X, and p=infinity with increasing continuity. The functional is
Daniell; an actual total Lq density represents it by the signed integral on
every total Lp test, its Lq norm equals the operator seminorm, and it is
almost everywhere unique. The endpoint assumptions are separate conditional
hypotheses; finite total measure is never imposed. Countably measurable
nonzero loci are retained at infinity, as in 2.4.12.

[`is_lp_functional_density`](../../theories/LpFunctionalDensityUP.v#L17) is
the abstract integral specification. `LpFunctionalDensity` derives norm and
uniqueness consequences independently of the existence proof. The operator
seminorm is the supremum over the closed unit ball, including the zero space
and zero seminorm. This implements Federer's erratum p. 2, book p. 98 lines
10–11 and 14, and p. 99 lines 13, 14 and 17. Source comments distinguish
supporting lemmas from printed claims and cite.

The proof develops finite-p increasing continuity, positive and negative
variations, the dominated-density family, finite-Lq and dyadic-average
maximization, and the residual-measure separation argument. It proves actual
maximizers and admissible patches, rather than assuming a maximal density.
Fatou supplies the one-sided pairing inequality needed for increasing closure;
the real lower-limit replacement is proved measurable, countably supported,
and Lq-bounded. The separating lower limit belongs to Lp by domination by the
original test, without an unrestricted lower-limit closure assumption.
Signed representation follows by subtracting the two positive densities.
The exact norm and almost uniqueness use the previously proved 2.4.16 duality.

Eight regression examples cover the zero functional, its closed-ball norm,
finite-p Daniell continuity, the zero dyadic score, and zero-norm densities
in all three exponent cases. All 219 named declarations, including three
record projections, are included in the assumption audit. No new global
axioms or external measure/integration development is introduced.

## 2.5.8 — Corrected Daniell Radon–Nikodym theorem

[`radon_nikodym_daniell_representation`](../../theories/RadonNikodymDaniell.v#L18)
proves Federer 2.5.8, p. 100, with his erratum p. 2, book p. 100 lines 3 and
5: a Daniell integral on total L-infinity functions that annihilates every
zero-essential-norm function has an actual total L1 density. Its signed
product integral represents the functional on every test. Exact norm and

[`CompactSpaceWeakDual.v`](../../theories/CompactSpaceWeakDual.v) states the
coincidence of the two weak topologies for compact Hausdorff X. K(X) is any
realisation in the sense of
[`CompactFunctionSpaceUP.v`](../../theories/CompactFunctionSpaceUP.v), normed
by sup im |f|, and its conjugate space with the weak topology of 2.5.12(2) is
[`weak_dual_space`](../../theories/VectorDaniellHypotheses.v#L65). For every
identification Phi in the sense of
[`CompactFunctionDualUP.v`](../../theories/CompactFunctionDualUP.v),
[`compact_space_weak_topologies_coincide`](../../theories/CompactSpaceWeakDual.v#L179)
proves that Phi(a) is a Daniell integral, that every Daniell integral agrees
on K(X) with some Phi(a), that Phi(a) and Phi(b) agree on K(X) only if a = b,
that Phi is continuous, and that the open sets of the conjugate space are
exactly the inverse images under Phi of the weakly open sets of K(X)*. The
weakly open sets contain with A every functional agreeing with A on K(X)
([`daniell_weak_open_saturated`](../../theories/CompactSpaceWeakDual.v#L143)),
so Phi is a homeomorphism up to this agreement.
[`compact_function_dual_map_weak_homeomorphism`](../../theories/CompactSpaceWeakDual.v#L200)
specialises the theorem to
[`compact_function_dual_map`](../../theories/CompactFunctionDual.v#L16) of
2.5.20. The item is complete.

## 2.5.20 — Disintegration of Daniell integrals on compact metric products

Both paragraphs on printed p. 114 are formalised. The development retains
Federer's spaces `L = K(X)`, `E = K(Y)` with the uniform norm, the continuous
vector families [`Q`](../../theories/ProductSpaceMaps.v#L38), and the actual linear isomorphism
`S(w)(x,y) = w(x)(y)`. The function space and its dual interpretation have
abstract specifications and sealed existence constructions.

- [`CompactFunctionSpaceUP.v`](../../theories/CompactFunctionSpaceUP.v), [`CompactFunctionSpace.v`](../../theories/CompactFunctionSpace.v),
  [`CompactFunctionJointContinuity.v`](../../theories/CompactFunctionJointContinuity.v), and [`CompactFunctionCurrying.v`](../../theories/CompactFunctionCurrying.v) give
  the function-space laws, uniform norm, joint evaluation continuity and
  the bijective currying map. Compactness yields a countable metric base;
  finite rational approximation cells prove separability of `K(Y)`.
- [`ContinuousRadialSplit.v`](../../theories/ContinuousRadialSplit.v) and [`ContinuousVectorFamily.v`](../../theories/ContinuousVectorFamily.v) verify the
  vector-lattice hypotheses, including continuity of radial splitting
  at zero norm. [`CompactProductVectorFunctional.v`](../../theories/CompactProductVectorFunctional.v) proves linearity and
  finite variation bounds of `T = mu o S`. Compactness supplies the
  decreasing-sequence continuity needed for the application of 2.5.12.
- [`CompactProductVectorRepresentation.v`](../../theories/CompactProductVectorRepresentation.v) obtains the measure and dual
  density from 2.5.12. [`CompactFunctionDual.v`](../../theories/CompactFunctionDual.v) interprets each bounded
  functional on `E` as a Daniell integral on `K(Y)`, preserving weak
  continuity. [`CompactProductDaniellDecomposition.v`](../../theories/CompactProductDaniellDecomposition.v) gives weak
  measurability and the actual integral formula for every member of
  `K(X × Y)`.
- [`DaniellDisintegrationSupport.v`](../../theories/DaniellDisintegrationSupport.v) proves the almost-everywhere inclusion
  of `spt k(x)` in the fibre of `spt mu`. Countable basic rectangles and
  countable dense families of supported tests give one exceptional null
  set. Scalar uniqueness establishes the zero pairings on rectangles
  outside the support; uniform continuity of each Daniell functional
  extends this conclusion to all supported tests.
- [`DaniellDisintegration.v`](../../theories/DaniellDisintegration.v) exposes the sealed measure/kernel pair with
  its Radon, regularity, measurability, integral and support laws. Its
  specification also retains the variation of [`T`](../../theories/AssociatedSubspace.v#L76), needed to identify
  the same measure in the final paragraph.
- [`DaniellGraphDisintegration.v`](../../theories/DaniellGraphDisintegration.v) proves the graph specialization for a
  continuous `g : Y -> X`, including the formula for the original Daniell
  integral, support in `g^-1{x}`, and identification of the original
  disintegration measure with the `K(X)` regular measure representing
  `g#(gamma+ + gamma-)`. [`WeightedDaniellVariation.v`](../../theories/WeightedDaniellVariation.v) proves the required
  weighted variation equality by the continuous regularized quotient
  `v/(a+epsilon)`, including points where `a = 0`.

Empty compact factors and the zero function space are allowed. No
nonvanishing test-function hypothesis is imposed. The graph measure
identity uses abstract positive and negative variations and proves the
identity for the measure already produced by disintegration.
