## 2.2.1 — Borel families, generation and support (pp. 59–60)

[`is_borel_family`](../../theories/BorelFamilyUP.v#L15) retains all three
printed closure conditions, relative to an arbitrary subset X. Empty countable
unions and nonempty countable intersections have the book's conventions. The
complement-plus-union and complement-plus-intersection redundancy proofs are
both supplied. The smallest family is `{empty, X}`; the largest is the power
set of X. Intersections of arbitrary classes are taken inside that power set,
so an empty class gives the largest family. this convention difference is
explicit.

Generated Borel families and both weaker countably closed families are
specified by their laws, generator containment and minimality. Private
intersection constructions prove existence and are sealed. The two weaker
classes allow either all countable unions or only disjoint countable unions,
while retaining nonempty countable intersections. Both generation criteria are
proved: if the generators include the empty set and their complements lie in
the generated weaker family, that family is the generated Borel family. This
uses Federer's erratum p. 1, book p. 60 line 4. A local counterexample shows
why the extra generator hypothesis matters: an empty generating family
produces `{empty}`, which is not Borel over nonempty X.

[`borel_set`](../../theories/TopologicalBorel.v#L21) is the sealed family
generated by open sets. Its closure laws, open and closed membership, and
difference closure are public. The two metric generation statements follow
from the closed-sequence-cover property: closed sets generate Borel sets under
countable unions/intersections; open sets do so even with only disjoint
countable unions. The metric cover proof uses closed complements of unions of
interiors of small balls, allowing the weaker general pseudometric interface
without assuming its balls are open. Federer's first statement with closed
generators is retained.

Measurable sets form a Borel family, and measurability of all open sets
therefore implies measurability of all Borel sets. Borel partitions retain
Federer's arbitrary target subset, whereas. Empty and singleton partitions are
included.

The support is specified by closedness and the open-neighborhood nonzero
criterion. A private existence proof constructs the complement of the union of
open null sets; the public operator is sealed. Its defining formula,
positive-neighborhood equivalence and disjointness from open null sets are
proved abstractly. No nullity of the whole complement is assumed: that is a
later Radon consequence.

Four regressions check the empty ambient family, the missing-empty-generator
counterexample, an empty Borel partition, and the empty support of zero.

## 2.2.2 — Closed and open approximation (pp. 60–61)

Both assertions are formalised for the more general pseudometric interface.
[`metric_borel_closed_approximation`](../../theories/BorelClosedApproximation.v#L24) gives a closed inner approximation to a
Borel set of finite measure. [`metric_borel_open_approximation`](../../theories/BorelOpenApproximation.v#L22) gives an open
outer approximation to a Borel set covered by countably many open sets of
finite measure. The latter allows the target set itself to have infinite
measure. Every tolerance is required to be strictly positive.

The proofs follow Federer's class of closed-approximable sets and geometric
error estimates. Intersections require no global finiteness; unions use finite
prefixes of the closed approximants and decreasing continuity. Those sets need
not be measurable, so their complements cannot simply be treated as measurable
for the unrestricted measure. The finite-case theorem is then applied to the
measure restricted to the target Borel set.

The local geometric tolerances are epsilon times 2^-(n+2), reserving half
the requested error. Telescoping finite sums and the 2.1.1 numerical-sum
laws give a total at most epsilon/2 and therefore a strict final error.
There is no external infinite-series or measure-theory dependency.
Countable indexed unions allow empty padding; nonempty intersections use
an enumeration that repeats an original member instead of inserting empty
sets. The open approximation is the printed union of V_i minus C_i.

Three regressions check the empty-set inner approximation, an empty open
cover, and the impossibility of strict zero-tolerance error. The local erratum
has no correction to this item.

## 2.2.3 — Borel regularity (pp. 61–62)

[`is_borel_regular_measure`](../../theories/BorelRegularMeasureUP.v#L17)
retains the two defining requirements: all open sets are measurable and every
subset has a Borel superset of equal measure. The measure laws are separate.
Ordinary regularity and measurability of all Borel sets follow. Ordinary
regularity together with open measurability is not used as a substitute for
Borel regularity.

For a measurable A of finite measure,
[`borel_regular_finite_sandwich`](../../theories/BorelRegularSandwich.v#L20)
constructs Borel D subset A subset B with mu(B minus D)=0, following Federer's
choices of B and a null Borel cover E of B minus A, and D=B minus E; the
formal statement here retains Federer's finite hypothesis. Both metric
approximation results of 2.2.2 extend to measurable sets, with their original
finiteness/finite-open-cover conditions. Restriction by a Borel set preserves
Borel regularity. the latter has a stronger general restriction criterion than
the printed Borel-set case.

Borel regularisation is specified by greatest-lower-bound laws over Borel
supersets. Countable Borel intersections prove attainment, after which the
measure laws, Borel measurability, Borel regularity and agreement on Borel
sets follow abstractly. The extended-infimum existence witness is private and
sealed. The public operator exposes the printed formula without unfolding that
witness.

Three regressions check empty-set preservation, preservation of an infinite
whole-space value, and the empty-set Borel sandwich. The local erratum has no
correction to this item

## 2.2.6 — The positive-integer sequence space

Federer p. 63 specifies the Cartesian power of the discrete positive
integers and its exact weighted metric. Natural indices and values encode
positive integers by adding one; the difference of two coordinate values
is unchanged by this encoding. The carrier, coordinate bijection and metric
series are specified abstractly, then constructed and sealed.

- `SequenceSpaceUP`, `SequenceWeights`, `BoundedDistanceScalar`, and
  `PositiveIntegerDistance`: coordinate representation and the exact
  series with weights `2^-(i+1)` and summands `|m-n|/(1+|m-n|)`.
- `SequenceMetricBounds`, `SequenceMetricLaws`, `SequenceMetricPrefixes`,
  and `SequencePowerBounds`: finiteness, metric laws, tail bounds,
  and recovery of coordinates from sufficiently small distances.
- `SequenceSpaceConstruction` and `SequenceMetricConstruction` are private
  witnesses; `SequenceSpaceSealed` hides their implementations and
  `SequenceSpace` exposes their proved abstract laws.
- `SequenceProductTopology` and `SequenceCoordinateContinuity`: finite
  initial-coordinate cylinders form a neighborhood base; continuity into
  the space is equivalent to eventual equality of every coordinate.
  This characterises the product topology of discrete coordinate factors.
- `SequenceCoordinates`, `SequenceCylinders`, `SequenceSeparability`, and
  `SequenceCompleteness`: arbitrary coordinate realisation, disjoint open
  and closed first-coordinate cylinders covering the space, a countable
  dense subset of eventually zero-coded sequences, and completeness
  from eventual coordinate constancy of Cauchy filters.
- `NaturalPairing`, `SequenceCountablePower`, and
  `SequenceCylinderHomeomorphism`: a countable Cartesian power is
  homeomorphic with the space by index reordering; every first-coordinate
  cylinder is homeomorphic with the space by head insertion and tail
  deletion. The product uses the general Cartesian product topology;
  both homeomorphism assertions expose continuous inverse maps.

Neither product completeness nor a preconstructed Baire-space metric is
imported: both are proved from the sealed representation and series laws.
Regression examples cover the zero prefix, default sequence, head/tail
cancellation and flattening a constant array.

## 2.2.7 — Lipschitz maps and the convex-domain criterion

Federer pp. 63–64 defines Lipschitzian maps using positive finite bounds,
introduces the least constant, and proves a local-quotient criterion on
convex normed domains. `LipschitzUP` retains the positive witness in the
Lipschitzian definition and specifies the optimal constant by attainment
and minimality among nonnegative bounds. A private infimum construction
proves existence; `LipschitzConstantSealed` and `LipschitzConstant` expose
only its abstract laws. Empty domains and zero optimal bounds are included.
The local definition uses ambient neighborhoods intersected with the domain.

`MetricSlopeLimsup` spells out the printed upper limit as the infimum over
positive radii of the supremum of punctured local distance quotients, using
the sealed extended-real bounds of 2.1.1. It proves equivalence with the
local eventual-bound condition used in the segment argument. In particular,
the empty punctured-neighborhood supremum is minus infinity.

`NormMetric` derives the general metric from a supplied abstract norm;
it assumes no inner product. `ConvexMetricSegment` proves the identities
for `a+t(b-a)`. `IntervalLocalBound` follows Federer's set S and supremum
argument, proving that the supremum belongs to S directly from the local
quantitative bound, then ruling out a supremum below one by extension to
the right. `ConvexLocalLipschitz` transfers this argument to every segment
and lets the bound decrease to M. `ConvexLipschitzCriterion` states the
complete equivalence in terms of the printed limsup and the public Lip(f).

The comparison bound M is explicitly nonnegative. This matters for empty or
isolated domains: the pointwise limsup condition alone may be vacuous, whereas
the least nonnegative Lipschitz bound cannot be negative. Constant maps also
show why the *optimal* bound must allow zero, despite the positive witness in
the opening definition. These are explicit boundary conventions, not
corrections attributed to Federer's erratum; that erratum has no entry for
this passage.

Four regressions cover the constant-map optimum, the empty-domain optimum,
a positive witness for constant maps, and impossibility of a negative bound
on the empty-domain optimum.

## 2.2.8 — Parametrisation of complete separable metric spaces

Federer pp. 64–65 proves that every complete, separable, nonempty metric
space is the image of the positive-integer sequence space under a locally
Lipschitzian map. [`complete_separable_sequence_parametrisation`](../../theories/SequenceParametrisation.v#L21) retains
these hypotheses and additionally exposes the proved continuity of the map.
Completeness is specified by convergence of proper Cauchy filters with
existential limits, avoiding an unintended chosen-point requirement.

The proof follows the closed-tree argument. `MetricClosedBalls` proves the
elementary ball facts, and `SeparableClosedCover` constructs countable covers
of each nonempty closed parent by nonempty closed sets of arbitrarily small
diameter. It intersects small balls centered in a countable dense set with
the parent, discards empty intersections and enumerates with repetition.
`ClosedShrinkingIntersection` proves the branch-intersection theorem using
the filter generated by the nested closed sets and abstract completeness.

`ClosedCoverTreeUP` specifies closedness, nonemptiness, child covers and the
printed `2^(-k-2)` diameter bound. The private recursive construction proves
existence; `ClosedCoverTreeSealed` hides the witness. Finite words are stored
in reverse order so that adjoining the newest coordinate is structural
recursion; their represented order and level match the book. The root is
the entire target and carries no diameter bound.

`ClosedCoverMapUP` specifies membership in every branch set. Existence and
uniqueness follow from the shrinking-intersection theorem; a private selection
is hidden by `ClosedCoverMapSealed`. `ClosedCoverSurjectivity` chooses, for
any target point, successive children containing it, realises those coordinates,
and invokes uniqueness. No proof computes through a sealed witness.

`SequenceFirstDifference` and `ClosedCoverMapLipschitz` compare the first
different coordinate's half-weight with the common-prefix diameter. This
replaces Federer's intermediate dyadic-annulus selection while retaining his
exact metric and the same estimate: distances below one quarter do not
increase. Balls of radius one eighth therefore have Lipschitz constant one.
`ClosedCoverMapContinuous` proves the resulting continuity explicitly.

Ordinary recursion replaces the maximal-principle presentation of its first
footnote. Regressions use the sequence space itself as target, check the
unrestricted root and exercise the local bound on coincident sequences.

## 2.2.9 — Borel embeddings of the sequence space

[`complete_nonisolated_sequence_borel_embedding`](../../theories/SequenceBorelEmbedding.v#L25) formalises the general
assertion on Federer p. 65: a complete, nonempty metric space without
isolated points contains a Borel subset homeomorphic to the sequence space.
There is no separability or local compactness premise. `NoIsolatedPoints`
retains the definition by nonopen singletons and proves the equivalent
punctured-ball condition. `HomeomorphismOnto` specifies an injective,
continuous map with the stated image that is open into the relative topology.

`OpenRefinementUP` and its sealed existence construction supply countably
many small nonempty open children with closures inside their parent.
Repeatedly splitting a nonempty remainder proves existence. The general
construction gives disjoint closures, but `OpenEmbeddingTreeUP` requires
only disjoint open siblings, as printed. The distinction is necessary for
adjacent interval children on the real line. The children need not cover
the parent, unlike the closed-cover tree in 2.2.8.

The sealed open-tree and branch-map constructions use the proved shrinking
closed-intersection theorem from 2.2.8. `OpenEmbeddingBorelImage` defines
Gamma by the printed intersection of positive-level open unions.
`OpenEmbeddingImageEquality` obtains compatible words for each Gamma point
and realises its branch. `OpenEmbeddingInjectivity` and `OpenEmbeddingOpenness`
use disjoint level open sets to recover coordinates. The local Lipschitz and
continuity proofs retain the exact 2.2.6 metric; as in 2.2.8, the first
different coordinate replaces the intermediate dyadic-annulus choice.

[`real_sequence_borel_embedding_countable_complement`](../../theories/RealSequenceBorelEmbedding.v#L24) proves the additional
real-line assertion. `RealMetricBackground` gives the existing standard real
topology an explicit absolute-distance interface on a fresh carrier, avoiding
competing canonical instances. `RealOpenIntervals` uses elementary order
and topology. `RealMetricCompleteness` imports the standard real completeness
proof through the narrow `complete_normed_module` module; this is background
for the hypothesis that the real line is complete, not an imported embedding
theorem.

The real interval construction is local. A uniform integer grid gives the
first level. For each bounded parent, two reflected ladders approach its
endpoints; the private formula h*N/(N+n), with N sufficiently large, has all
gaps below the prescribed tolerance. Subsequent proofs use only the sealed
ladder laws. Bijective countable enumerations preserve sibling disjointness.
`IntervalRefinementUP` specifies the interval endpoints, closure containment,
strict diameter, disjointness and countable loss. The grid and bounded
refinements are sealed separately, then assembled into a sealed real interval
tree. `OpenEmbeddingCountableComplement` proves that every point outside
Gamma is lost at a finite node; countably many finite words and countable
loss at each node give a countable complement.

Regressions check the zero-level union, nonemptiness of the produced real
Borel set, and adjacent disjoint open intervals whose closures share an
endpoint.

## 2.2.11 — Universal sets and a Suslin non-Borel set

## 2.2.15 — Baire functions and the metric-target criterion

`BaireFunctionUP` specifies sequentially closed function classes and the
smallest such class containing the continuous maps. The intersection-of-classes
existence proof is hidden behind `BaireFunctionSealed`; `BaireFunctions` exposes
only closure and minimality. This follows Federer pp. 70–71 without importing
an external Baire hierarchy or descriptive-set-theory theorem.

`PointwiseOpenPreimage` proves the exact open-preimage expression cited from
2.3.2(6), pp. 73–74, using closed distance cores, eventual membership and
countable Borel operations. `BorelPointwiseLimits` and `BaireBorel` show that
Borel maps form a Baire class and that every Baire map is Borel. The target
is an arbitrary metric space; separability is not required for this inclusion.

`BairePairs`, `BairePostcomposition` and `BaireRealAlgebra` derive the real
algebra laws from minimality. Closed-set characteristic functions are limits
of continuous distance cutoffs. The local proof uses max(0,1-n*d(x,A)) in
place of the printed exponential 2^(-n*d(x,A)); this documented proof variant
avoids developing an exponential solely for this approximation. Empty closed
sets are treated separately. The family of sets with Baire characteristic
functions is a Borel family, giving `BorelCharacteristicBaire`.

`RealIntegerCells` retains the printed signed integer cells
j/i <= f(x) < (j+1)/i, with i=k+1. The approximants are specified by cell
membership and cellwise values, constructed privately using integer floor,
and sealed. `IntegerCharacteristicSums` reindexes the printed sum over all
integers with an injective partial decoding; missing codes contribute zero.
Exactly one cell can contribute at each point, so finite partial sums are
eventually equal to the approximant. Its error is less than 1/i. Thus
[`real_borel_function_baire`](../../theories/RealBorelBaire.v#L17) and [`real_borel_iff_baire`](../../theories/RealBorelBaire.v#L31) prove the full real-valued
converse for any metric source, without boundedness or separability assumptions.

`BaireTargetNecessity` and `BaireTargetSufficiency` prove the final separable
metric-target characterisation on p. 71; Federer states it without a proof.
For necessity, Baire maps from R have finite sets of values approximable by
one continuous curve: finite-sample convergence makes this property sequentially
closed. Sealed finite Borel interpolation tests every prescribed finite target
set. For sufficiency, the curve condition makes every countable Borel step
map Baire by approximating its successive finite prefixes. A second pointwise
limit uses sealed approximations of a Borel map through a countable dense
sequence. First-hit Borel labels come from the abstract laws of a sealed
countable-cover index. No completeness hypothesis is introduced.

[`borel_baire_target_criterion`](../../theories/BaireTargetCriterion.v#L17) states the printed equivalence for a nonempty
separable target. [`borel_baire_target_criterion_with_empty`](../../theories/BaireTargetCriterion.v#L33) gives the precise
version allowing empty metric spaces: either the target is empty or it has
the finite-curve property. This is a local boundary correction, not an entry
in Federer's erratum. Indeed every map into an empty target is Baire, whereas
the printed condition, even applied to the empty finite set, demands a curve
from R into that target. `BaireEmptyTargetBoundary` proves this counterexample.

`BinaryTargetCounterexample` supplies the nonempty counterexample to the
unrestricted-target converse mentioned by Federer: the separable two-point
real subspace {0,1} cannot satisfy the curve condition, since connectedness
of real intervals prevents a continuous curve from attaining both values.
Consequently a real-domain Borel map into that target is not Baire. The
connected-interval theorem is general topology from the already used
`normed_module` background, not an imported Baire theorem.

Federer's local LaTeX erratum has no correction affecting these passages on
pp. 70–71. Six regressions cover the unbounded real identity, a signed lower
cell endpoint and its quantisation, the real target's curve condition, an
empty source and the empty-target boundary.

## 2.2.16 — Separable support and Ulam concentration

[`finite_measure_support_separable`](../../theories/FiniteMeasureSupportSeparable.v#L22) proves the first conclusion of Federer
p. 71: a finite metric measure whose open sets are measurable has separable
support. The conclusion is about the actual metric subspace of support
points, including an empty support. The ambient space need not be complete
or separable. `SeparatedMetricSetUP` specifies maximal strictly separated
subsets; the private Zorn existence proof is hidden behind `MaximalSeparatedSealed`.
Maximality gives the printed 2/n-net property. Disjoint radius-1/n balls at
support points have positive measure, so the 2.1.6 countability theorem makes
each net countable. Their union is dense in the support.

[`finite_measure_ulam_dense_support_complement_null`](../../theories/UlamMeasureSupport.v#L20) proves the second
conclusion on pp. 71–72, retaining finiteness, measurability of opens, and
the existence of a dense Ulam set. `DenseUlamSupportCoverUP` and its sealed
witness provide the open null cover of the support complement. Dense-centred
balls are indexed by the dense set times the natural radius indices. The
already proved 2.1.6 union property makes this product Ulam. This includes
finite and empty dense sets, without asserting that this particular redundant
ball indexing has the same cardinality as the dense set.

`OpenCoverClosedRefinementUP` specifies Federer's C_n(V): closed sets inside
the respective open members, covering their union over all levels, with
uniform separation at a fixed level. The private construction follows the
printed well ordering and removal of preceding open sets. `MetricInteriorCores`
expresses distance at least 1/n from the complement by the equivalent
universal metric inequalities. Thus a whole-space open member is handled
without a real-valued distance to an empty set.

`SeparatedClosedUnions` proves that every subfamily union at a fixed level
is closed, with no countability assumption on the family. `UlamOpenNullUnion`
then applies the second Ulam property of 2.1.6 to each level and the measure
covering inequality to their countable union. It assumes only the required
measurability of subfamily unions, obtained from closedness; it does not
assume every subset of the support complement is measurable. All downstream
measure arguments use the sealed cover and refinement laws.

The working LaTeX erratum has no relevant correction on pp. 71–72. Four
regressions cover whole-space interior cores, empty maximal separated sets,
countable dense sets, and separability of the zero measure's support on any
ambient metric space.

## 2.2.17 — Proper images of Radon measures

`ProperMapUP` retains Federer's p. 72 definition of properness by compact
inverse images, with continuity separate, and his countable compact-cover
hypothesis in its literal set-family form. [`federer_2_2_17_radon_pushforward`](../../theories/RadonProperPushforward.v#L51)
proves that the pushforward is a measure and a Radon measure under all the
printed hypotheses. The source locally compact Hausdorff conditions are
part of the existing Radon specification.

`ProperContinuousClosed` proves the closed-map lemma from compact target
neighborhoods, compact inverse images, closed intersections and continuous
compact images. `ProperImageComplement` exposes the printed observation
that the complement of the image is open and that all its subsets have
zero pushforward measure. `PushforwardMeasurableSets` proves the direct
measurability implication from inverse images, completing the open-set
measurability step left as an exercise by Federer.

`RadonProperPushforward` proves compact finiteness and inner regularity
through compact preimages and images. For outer regularity it uses the
explicit simplification. Closedness of f makes W open; it contains B and its
inverse image lies in U. This documented proof variant replaces Federer's
countable compact-slab argument. It proves a stronger whole-domain theorem
without the countable compact-cover hypothesis, while the final Federer
theorem retains that hypothesis. that extension is not claimed here.

`ProperPushforwardOuterBound` also proves the displayed additive-error
estimate on p. 72, for arbitrary sets B including those of infinite measure.
The proof uses the Radon infimum law and the same open-neighborhood construction.
No new external mathematical module is introduced beyond the existing general
topology and scalar background.

Four regressions cover identity properness, identity Radon pushforward with
possibly infinite total mass, compact-source countable covers, and an exact
zero-measure open neighborhood for the empty target set.
